# Interaction Dynamics - Second-Order Networks under a Common Exogenous Background

## 0. Positioning and Assumptions

This paper studies the situation of two configurations in a [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31): in one, a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that has only [first-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c24) and a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that has [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at each other; in the other, several [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), all with [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01), [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at one another. The two configurations use the same set of constraints; they differ only in one parameter, whether [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) is present.

<note>

*This paper looks at interaction between acquaintances: on one side an infant who does not step back to think "this is only my view", on the other an adult who does; the second half then looks at several adults interweaving. Both sides follow the same rules and differ only in whether they have the ability to "step back and think".*

</note>

The conclusions of this paper come in two levels. A constraint-level conclusion is derived only from the laws and premises listed in the next section; if any one of them can be overturned, the conclusion fails. A conditional conclusion additionally uses an assumption or an empirical antecedent listed below; the text states which one it uses, and if reality violates it, that conclusion fails. An empirical antecedent serves only as an input to a derivation and is not used to prove that a constraint holds.

<note>

*Conclusions are of two kinds: those derived from the rules alone, and those that also rely on an assumption or an observation of reality. For the latter the text says clearly which one is used, and if that one is overturned the conclusion falls with it. Observations can help reasoning, but they cannot be used to prove the rules themselves.*

</note>

A [configuration assumption](concepts.md#c01) is an assumption made about a set of concrete subjects: at the stated stage, some of the subjects have only [first-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c24), and the rest have [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) and also [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02). This paper makes that assumption for A and B (Premise 5): at the stated stage, A has only [first-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c24), and B has [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) and also [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02). It is an assumption, not a theorem; it is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02) that whether a subject has [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) at a given moment can be confirmed from outside; it can be refuted only by finding a mechanism that proves the subject's [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) can only fall out of [locked-in rounds](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c25), or can only fall out of [random rounds](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c26).

<note>

*"A is an infant and B is an adult" is only a condition set in advance for modeling, not something proved. We have no way of determining from outside whether someone has, at a given moment, the ability to step back and think about themselves, so we can only assume it. The only way to overturn it is to prove that every output of this subject is in fact fixed in advance, or is merely random.*

</note>

An [exogenous background](concepts.md#c02) is a background that, within a stated time window and [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23), is treated as entering from outside the model and as not updated by the [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) in the model (Premise 6). It is a scoping assumption and does not claim that culture is unchanging; what enters the model is the background, not the content that each [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) obtains from it, because each [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can obtain only the part that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it. When the time window is lengthened to the point where the background changes significantly, this assumption must be relaxed, and the conclusions that depend on it fail accordingly.

<note>

*Here culture is treated only as a background given from outside; this paper does not study how it changes. This does not mean culture never changes, nor that everyone learns the same thing; once the time span is long enough for culture to change visibly, this assumption can no longer be used.*

</note>

Institutions, norms, and customs are, in principle, patterns formed in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) by a [second-order network](concepts.md#c04), not a different kind of entity. Because the time window of this paper is short and the participants are few, they are treated together with culture as an [exogenous background](concepts.md#c02); the only question asked is through whom they [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at whom, not how they are formed. This is a scoping decision of this paper and not a restriction on the definition of a [second-order network](concepts.md#c04).

<note>

*Institutions, norms, and customs are what grows out of long-term interaction between people, and they belong to this network to begin with. Because this paper covers a short time and few people, it first treats them as a background given from outside and does not ask how they form. This is only a trade-off made in this paper; it does not mean the network contains no such things.*

</note>

This paper deals with the structure of the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31), and with the "beginning" and the "back-and-forth" that can be derived from it. Words such as trust, security, commitment, fairness, attraction, and attachment, which point to inner states or motives, are all treated in this paper as the attribution of some [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) or of the researcher; the only question asked is what they do within a [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31), and their truth is not adjudicated (4.6, 4.9). Self-disclosure, power and dependence, culture, and strangers and weak ties are outside the scope of this paper and are left to later sub-papers. Individual differences (personality and attachment style treated as traits that carry across partners), coalitions and exclusion among three or more people, the life course, and methods for analyzing dyadic data are also not developed here and are left to later papers.

<note>

*This paper looks at the structure of the loop, not at every phenomenon of interaction between acquaintances. Words such as trust, security, and attraction are handled only as someone's view, and no judgment is made on whether they are true or false. Self-disclosure, power and dependence, culture, and strangers and weak ties are the topics of other papers; individual differences, coalitions and exclusion, the life course, and how to analyze dyadic data are also left for later.*

</note>

A, B, and the C, D, and E that appear later are all synthetic individuals used for demonstration, not measured cases.

<note>

*A and B are characters made up to explain the model, not an actual infant or a particular parent.*

</note>

```text
Note: Institutions, norms, and customs are, in principle, patterns of a Second-Order Network; this paper treats them together with culture as an Exogenous Background, which is a scoping decision and not a restriction on the Second-Order Network.
Note: A Configuration Assumption is not a theorem; an Exogenous Background does not claim the background is unchanging; what enters the model is the background, not what each Self draws from it, and each Self gets only the part that Arrives at it.
Note: It is Not Guaranteed that whether a subject has Second-Order Distinction at a given moment can be confirmed from outside (an upstream conclusion); this paper only uses it and does not derive it.
```

## 1. The Laws and Premises Used in This Paper

This paper uses the [Law of Flux](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c24): the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) in which a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) is situated keeps changing, and at each of its [moments](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09), the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it differs from what it was before that [collapse](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c08). It uses the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25): when a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), its next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) cannot be uniquely derived in advance; what can be derived is the [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). It uses the [Law of Attrition](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c26): a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can at no [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) make a complete [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of itself. It uses the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28): each position in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) occurs only once, and when it is redone, a choice that is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) is not necessarily the same. The four laws are used as axioms, and this paper does not prove them again.

<note>

*Four rules: history keeps changing; when something is not fixed in advance, the next step cannot be calculated precisely; no one can see themselves completely; the past cannot be redone. These are starting points; this paper only uses them and does not prove them.*

</note>

This paper has four further premises. Premise 1: every [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at that [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) itself before its next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09). Premise 2: for any [decision](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c06) of a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), its content is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), not a complete description of the object that is said or done. Premise 3: a result that falls out of a [computation](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c05) is not a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) falls out only in a [decision](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c06). Premise 4: for any [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04), including itself, there is always a part of its [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) that has not [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at this [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16).

<note>

*Four premises: each of one's own judgments returns to oneself; the words one says and the things one does, in content, are each a judgment and not a complete description of the object; a result that has been fixed in advance does not count as a judgment; for anything, some part of what it has lived through never reaches the one who looks at it.*

</note>

This paper adopts the conclusions of the [three walls](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c23): the [wall of provenance](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c08), by which a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) cannot obtain all of its own past [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13); the [wall of the blind spot](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c09), by which a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) cannot see its present self; and the [wall of the undetermined](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c10), by which a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) cannot calculate what is certain about the future. It also adopts the [forced wager](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c11) and the [illusion](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c14): the [illusion](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c14) is a condition and not an error; [second-order](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) does not take a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) out of the [illusion](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c14), it only makes it possible to ask about it.

<note>

*This paper uses these conclusions directly: one cannot obtain the whole past, cannot see the present, and cannot calculate the future precisely; every decision is like a wager one is forced to make; a person is always inside an illusion, and being able to step back and think only makes this possible to ask about.*

</note>

This paper adds one constraint, called [unconfirmability of drives](concepts.md#c03): for a subject that is not in [lock in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), its drives, that is, motives, needs, and purposes, cannot be completely observed from outside, cannot be confirmed by self-report, and a judgment about the motive made by others after the fact is only a [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) or an [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) and does not count as verification; the motive itself does not [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others by itself. It applies to every subject that is not in [lock in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), regardless of age and regardless of whether [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) is present; whether a subject is in [lock in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) cannot be confirmed from outside and can only be assumed (Premise 3). Its grounds are: the subject's self-report, when it is not in [lock in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) and comes from a [decision](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c06), is in content a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) and not a complete description of the cause (Premise 2); the subject cannot see the very [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) it is making (the [Law of Attrition](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c26)); when others say "what he did it for", if it takes one of the other's [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) as the other's view, that is the others' [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), and it may also be their [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) of the other's [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), not a reading of the other's state; a judgment about the motive after the fact is likewise only another's [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) or [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45); and others also cannot uniquely derive in advance the next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) of a subject that is not in [lock in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) (the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25)). The motive itself is not an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and does not [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others by itself.

<note>

*A new rule: why anyone (infant or adult) did something cannot be confirmed from outside. The reason they give is just one more statement, and they cannot see themselves clearly either; others' explanations are only guesses. What can be seen from outside is only what they did and what happened afterwards.*

</note>

The methodological prohibitions of this paper are: drives may not be written as facts that can be observed, confirmed by self-report, or verified after the fact; an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) may not be explained with "in order to", and one may write only that, in a certain dimension, an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of a certain kind has a higher [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); the emergence of [second-order](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) may not be written as necessarily produced by external material; the presence or absence of [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) may not be inferred from a diagnosis; the [exogenous background](concepts.md#c02) may not be written as culture being unchanging, and gaps may not be patched with explanatory items that have only a name and no criterion ("culture" is a common example), and a concrete [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) input must be specified; external statistics, order, or a single record path may not be written as a direct reading of true [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) or of path dependence; the step from numbers to action may not be written as computable; a sense of certainty may not be taken as evidence; it may not be said that reflection necessarily brings better judgment; it may not be said that the [illusion](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c14) has been eliminated; stability of an [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) may not be written as approaching the other; and when speaking of [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37), [divergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c38), or [stasis](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c52), the dimension must be stated.

<note>

*The list of things this paper forbids itself from saying: it may not say that someone certainly did something for some reason; it may not say that culture is unchanging; it may not use "culture", a word with only a name, as an all-purpose explanation; it may not treat statistics as having seen into the inside; it may not say that action can be calculated; it may not take a sense of certainty as evidence; it may not say that reflection necessarily makes judgment better; it may not say that the illusion has been eliminated; it may not say that the more familiar, the closer to the other's truth; and saying "becoming more alike" requires saying in which respect.*

</note>

The researcher is also inside the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31). The researcher's observations, questions, instructions, and records, if recorded as the researcher's [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the observed [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), they enter the other's [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and, by the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28), cannot be taken back; when the other's subsequent [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), influenced by them, [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) again at the researcher and take part in its [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), the two sides form a [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31). So it is not presumed that the researcher necessarily stands outside the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31); only an observation made through records alone, which does not [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the observed, leaves its [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) unchanged. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

## 2. The Loop Model

The model of this paper is a recursion that advances by [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09). ### 2.1 Component 1: Three Loops

What is passed on a [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) is [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28). <note>

This is the third component: this paper derives no conclusion of the "guarantee" kind. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*How the next step will go cannot be calculated precisely in advance; what has happened in the past also changes at every moment. So this paper will not say "it will certainly be so"; at most it says "cannot be excluded" or "not guaranteed".*

</note>

That a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) has [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) is a [configuration assumption](concepts.md#c01) made about one of its stages and not a guarantee about each of its [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28). By the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25), for a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), the next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) cannot be uniquely derived in advance; so having the ability does not imply using it this time; using [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) is also a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) and remains bound by the [Law of Attrition](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c26). How the ability changes with age and with how one gets along is biological, determined by experience and not decidable by logic, and this paper does not derive it. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Being able to step back and think about oneself is an assumption made at modeling time about a certain stage, not a claim that the person is stepping back each time. Having the ability does not mean it was used this time, and using it does not mean one saw oneself clearly. How the ability changes with age and getting along depends on biology and is decided by observation, not by logic.*

</note>

The [rounds](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c04) at the two ends need not be aligned. By the [Law of Flux](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c24), the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) differs at every [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09); when an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the other, the other's [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) need not be the same as when it fell out. So this paper does not assume that the two ends are synchronous and introduces no common clock; the sequence labels in the text are only an ordering. This is a constraint-level conclusion. For biological humans there is also a physical limit: neural conduction and reaction take time (Premise 11), and the other's reaction to an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) cannot occur at the same time as that [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28). This layer is a conditional conclusion and uses Premise 11.

<note>

*An exchange between two people is not both pressing a button at the same moment: by the time what you said reaches the other, the other has already lived through other things. So this paper does not assume the two sides are synchronous, and the sequence in the text is only an ordering. For real people there is also a physical reason: neural conduction and reaction both take time, and the other cannot react in the very instant you speak.*

</note>

Because the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) keeps accumulating, the same [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) may have a different [impact](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c47) and [discrepancy](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c49) on the same [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) at different [moments](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09), so the system is not guaranteed to be stationary: the same input does not guarantee the same effect. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*The same sentence, said today or said next year, acts differently on the same person, because in between they have lived through other things. So one cannot assume that the same input has the same effect.*

</note>

### 2.4 Component 4: One-Way Accumulation

By the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28), each position in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) occurs only once, and what has already happened cannot be taken back. This is the fourth component, most often used on [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05), which is counted by dimension. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*What has happened cannot be taken back; one can only keep going forward. The shared experience of two people also accumulates this way, and it is counted item by item, not as an overall good or bad.*

</note>

[Dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) is counted by dimension. The same [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) may already have [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) with B in one dimension, have none with C in the same dimension, and have none with B in another dimension; zero at any one place does not cancel the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) already present at other places (the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28)). The same [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can therefore be, with different counterparts, in different situations at the same time, and need not pass through certain stages in sequence.

<note>

*Shared past is counted item by item: with one person there is some for this matter and none for that matter, and with another person it is a different set. Zero in one item does not turn the other items into zero. A person can have both acquaintances and strangers at the same time, and does not need to become one kind first in order to become the other.*

</note>

The researcher may regard the accumulated [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) as the layer that changes slowly and regard the round-by-round [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and feedback as the layer that changes fast; the former is called the slow variable and the latter the fast variable. This is a [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) the researcher makes in observation and is not an explanation of why the [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) changes: the [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) cannot be read (the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25)), and the researcher can only see that some things change fast and some change slowly. [Impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45), familiarity, and [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06) are functions of the accumulated [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13); attraction, [conflict](concepts.md#c10), and dissipation are the shapes that the fast layer takes on the background of the slow layer (see 4.7 to 4.9). Accumulation only adds and never subtracts, which does not mean quality improves monotonically. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Shared experience accumulates slowly, so it looks slow; the reaction in each round of exchange looks fast. This is the speed we see from outside and not an explanation of why things change inside, because we cannot see the other's inner state. Familiarity and tacit coordination are slowly accumulated; attraction, flare-ups, and fading are the shapes that the fast layer takes against the slow background. More and more experience does not mean getting better and better.*

</note>

```text
Conclusion 2-10: Dedicated History is counted by dimension; zero at one place does not cancel the History already present at other places (from definition of Dedicated History + Law of Non-Restoration)
Conclusion 2-14: Each position in History occurs only once and what has occurred cannot be taken back; Dedicated History is counted by dimension (from Law of Non-Restoration + Conclusion 2-10)
Step 2-18-1: The researcher may regard the accumulated History as the slow layer and regard Output and feedback as the fast layer; this is a Cut and does not explain why Weight changes (from Law of the Black Box + definition of Cut)
Conclusion 2-18: Under this Cut, the accumulated History is the slow variable and Output and feedback are the fast variable; accumulation only adds and never subtracts, which does not imply that quality improves monotonically (from Step 2-18-1 + Conclusion 2-14 + definition of Impression + definition of Tacit Coordination)
```

### 2.5 Component 5: Attribution as Output

By the definition of [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), the attribution that a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) with [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) makes about the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of another [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of its own. By [unconfirmability of drives](concepts.md#c03), motives cannot be confirmed from outside. So every word that points to an inner state or a motive is an attribution of some [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) or of the researcher; when an attribution is spoken or enacted, it is an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others, enters the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13), and changes subsequent [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). This is the fifth component, and 4.6 applies it to words such as deception, coercion, and trust. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*A view of another is one's own view and not a fact; once the view is spoken, it becomes a new event and affects later exchanges. Words about motives are all handled this way.*

</note>

```text
Conclusion 2-15: A word that points to an inner state or a motive can be a Distinction, a View Attribution, or an Impression of Weight of some Self or of the researcher; once voiced, it is an Output, and the part that Arrives at others enters their History and takes part in their subsequent Weights (from definition of View Attribution + definition of Impression + definition of Output + Conclusion 1-5 + definition of Wo)
```

### 2.6 Configuration Variable and Six Operations

The two configurations differ in only one place: a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) with [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) has one more link, in which it can take one of its own [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) as a view and make a further [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) about it; a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) with only [first-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c24) does not have this link. All of the above are constraint-level conclusions.

<note>

*The two kinds of people differ in one thing only: for a person who can step back and think "this is only my view", the cycle has one more step; for one who cannot, that step is absent.*

</note>

On this [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) this paper performs six operations, each asking what the laws and premises permit and what they forbid: adding a node, removing a node, [mismatch](concepts.md#c08), attribution misalignment, history accumulation, and history return (see 3.5). Section 3 deals with the case of only two nodes, in which [mismatch](concepts.md#c08) and [repair](concepts.md#c09) are in 3.4; Section 4 deals with multiple nodes. The patterns that these operations grow are the conclusions of this paper. Which components each operation uses is shown in the matrix in Section 6; history return has been merged into 3.5.

<note>

*Every section that follows does one thing to the same cycle: adding a person, removing a person, failing to match, attributions that disagree, shared experience growing, and what has been lived through returning in the next round. Each section asks only: what do the rules permit, and what do they not permit.*

</note>

## 3. Theme One: A Builds a Self in a Loop with B

### 3.1 The Starting Point

This section uses Component 2 and Component 4 (Conclusion 2-12, Conclusion 2-14).

By the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28), each position in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) occurs only once; since A enters carrying prenatal contact (Premise 7), A's [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) is not reset to zero at birth, and a new relationship does not mean starting everything over. A and B have as yet no [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) in this dimension. [Dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) is the part of [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) between two [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), in a stated dimension, formed by the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at each other; it belongs only to the two of them; having as yet no [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) limits only this relationship. Empirically, A enters carrying prenatal contact, and B enters carrying the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has already [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at B, which includes the part that reached B from the [exogenous background](concepts.md#c02), and only that part, if any, is in B's [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13); the part that has never [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at B is not in the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at B (Premise 4). This section is a conditional conclusion, and the empirical antecedent it uses is birth and prenatal contact (Premise 7).

<note>

*A does not start from a blank sheet: A had contact with this world before birth, and B comes carrying a whole life. "No dedicated shared past yet" only means that between these two people there is not yet a shared experience that belongs to just the two of them.*

</note>


### 3.2 A's Output and B's Candidate Attributions

This section uses Component 5 (Conclusion 2-15).

By [unconfirmability of drives](concepts.md#c03), one may not write that A cries "in order to be held" as a confirmed fact. This example records A's crying as one [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); what B faces is a set of [candidates](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c18): hungry, tired, wet, in pain, wanting to be held. B's attributing the crying to one of them can be B's [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) (if B takes A's crying as a view of A) and can also be B's [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) of A's [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); neither is a reading of A's state, and it is not guaranteed that an externally available criterion determines whether it is right or wrong. Why B picks these [candidates](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c18) comes from B's own [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13), and this paper does not trace it further. This section is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*When an infant cries, the adult obtains only the fact that "the infant is crying". Whether it is hunger, tiredness, or a wet diaper is the adult's guess. Where the options for the guess come from is the adult's own past experience, and this paper does not trace further down.*

</note>

This limit is not confined to infants. When a person says "I do everything for my family", others obtain an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), and the motive is the others' interpretation; by Premise 2, the person's own account is also only another [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), not a complete description of the cause. This is a calibration and is not a source of derivation.

<note>

*Adults are the same: the reason they give is just one more statement. This passage only compares with reality to see whether it looks alike, and is not used as a basis for reasoning.*

</note>

```text
Step 3-2-1: If this example records A's crying as one Output, then it is one Output (from definition of Output)
Step 3-2-2: One may not write that A cries "in order to be held" as a confirmed fact (from Conclusion 2-15 + Conclusion 1-5)
Conclusion 3-2-3: When B attributes the crying to one of the Candidates, it can be a View Attribution of B (if B takes A's crying as a view of A), and it can also be B's Impression of A's Weight; neither is a reading of A's state, and it is not guaranteed that there is an externally available criterion that decides whether it is right or wrong (from Premise 5 + definition of View Attribution + definition of Impression + definition of Candidate + Conclusion 1-5)
```

### 3.3 Trial and Error and Calibration

This section uses Component 2 and Component 5 (Conclusion 2-12, Conclusion 2-15).

Each attempt by B, feeding, changing, holding, is, as long as it is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), a [forced wager](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c11): the result cannot be calculated precisely, cannot be taken back afterwards, and doing nothing is also a result. A's subsequent [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) are the new [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) that B obtains, and B adjusts later attempts accordingly. Stopping crying is only one [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of A and does not by itself constitute a test of the attribution: it is not guaranteed that why A cried and why A stopped can be confirmed from outside ([unconfirmability of drives](concepts.md#c03)), and it is not guaranteed that there is an externally available criterion for whether the attribution is right. B's [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) may be stable, but stability does not mean being closer to the real A. B can take its own [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) as a view and revise it; this is an asymmetric link in the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31), which A does not have. This section is a constraint-level conclusion; the part concerning B's revising its attribution uses the [configuration assumption](concepts.md#c01) and is conditional.

<note>

*The adult tries again and again: changing the diaper, feeding, picking up, and watching how the infant responds. The crying stopping does not mean the guess has been verified. Becoming more and more sure also does not mean getting closer to the infant's real state. The adult can think "my guess is only one view"; the infant has no such step, and this is the only difference between the two sides.*

</note>

```text
Step 3-3-1: Each attempt by B when not in Lock-in is a Forced Wager (from definition of Wo + definition of Decision + definition of Forced Wager)
Step 3-3-2: If A's subsequent Outputs Arrive at B, they are new Arrivals that B receives, and B's subsequent trial Weights are affected by those Arrivals (from Conclusion 3-2-3 + definition of Arrival + definition of Wo)
Step 3-3-2-2: If this example records stopping crying as one Output of A, then it is one Output of A (from definition of Output)
Conclusion 3-3-3: If this example records stopping crying as one Output of A, then it is only one Output of A and does not by itself constitute a test of the attribution: it is not guaranteed that why A cried and why A stopped can be confirmed from outside, and it is not guaranteed that there is an externally available criterion for whether the attribution is right (from Step 3-3-2-2 + Conclusion 3-2-3 + Conclusion 2-15)
Step 3-3-4: B's Impression being stable does not mean being closer to the real A (from Conclusion 3-2-3 + Conclusion 2-12)
Conclusion 3-3-5: B can take its own attribution as a view and revise it, and A does not have this link (from Premise 5 + Conclusion 2-5 + Step 2-6)
Note: Conclusion 3-3-5 is conditional and uses Premise 5.
```

### 3.4 Mismatch and Repair

This section uses Component 3, Component 4, and Component 5 (Conclusion 2-13, Conclusion 2-14, Conclusion 2-15).

A [mismatch](concepts.md#c08) is, in a dimension and under a compatibility criterion specified by the researcher, the incompatibility of the adjacent [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) sequences of two [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19); a [repair](concepts.md#c09) is, after a [mismatch](concepts.md#c08), the renewed compatibility of the subsequent sequence in the same dimension and under the same criterion. The criterion looks at whether the sequences are compatible, not at whether either party "wanted something and did not get it" or "had an expectation violated"; those carry drives and, by [unconfirmability of drives](concepts.md#c03), are not written.

<note>

*"Not matching" and "matching again" look only at whether what the two people did one after the other fits together, and not at what anyone wants inside. The standard for what counts as fitting together is stated in advance by the researcher.*

</note>

In this [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) a [mismatch](concepts.md#c08) is not an occasional fault but a possibility that the structure does not exclude. Every time B makes a [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) about A's crying and then acts, it is a [forced wager](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c11), and the attribution is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02); if B's action and A's next [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) are incompatible under the specified criterion (for example, crying continues after feeding), this stretch of the sequence is a [mismatch](concepts.md#c08). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*The adult changes the approach and the infant still cries; this too is something this cycle is bound to produce. It is not that someone did something wrong; every attempt is a wager to begin with.*

</note>

A [repair](concepts.md#c09) only indicates that the sequence is compatible again; it does not indicate that the attribution has been tested, nor that A "got what A wanted". Renewed compatibility of the sequence may come from B switching to another [candidate](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c18), from A's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) itself changing, from a change in the [exogenous background](concepts.md#c02), or from a [random round](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c26), and the difference cannot necessarily be seen from the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28). So a [repair](concepts.md#c09) is only a record, not a test, and has the same structure as "stopping crying does not by itself constitute a test of the attribution" in 3.3. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*That they later matched can only be described as matching; it cannot be said that the guess was verified, nor that the infant was satisfied. It may be that the adult changed the approach, that the infant changed by itself, that the environment changed, or it may be mere coincidence, which cannot be told apart from outside.*

</note>

A [repair](concepts.md#c09) is not guaranteed after a [mismatch](concepts.md#c08). By the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25), the next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) of a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) cannot be uniquely derived in advance, so the subsequent sequence may become compatible again, may remain incompatible, or may turn into [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of the kind of refusal, warning, and counteraction (see 4.6). By the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28), [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that have fallen out cannot be taken back; they remain in the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) of each side they have [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at and affect subsequent [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). Conversely, the absence of a [repair](concepts.md#c09) also does not imply that a [repair](concepts.md#c09) will never occur from now on: the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25) likewise does not permit this assertion. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*After not matching, matching again is not certain. They may keep failing to match, or a fight may break out, and what has already happened cannot be taken back and stays in the experience of both people. But it also cannot be said that it will certainly never get better: how the next step goes, no one can calculate precisely.*

</note>

By the [configuration assumption](concepts.md#c01), B can treat a [mismatch](concepts.md#c08) as a clue and go back to take its own attribution as a view and revise it; A does not have this link. So if the sequence becomes compatible again after a [mismatch](concepts.md#c08), when tracing the change one starts by looking at B's [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), because only B's side has this link; this is an order of searching, not a determination that the cause is on B's side. This paragraph is a conditional conclusion and uses the [configuration assumption](concepts.md#c01).

<note>

*When things do not match, the adult can think "my guess is only one view" and change the approach, while the infant has no such step. So to find where a change came from, look first at the adult's side; but this is only where to look first, not a claim that it must be the adult who changed.*

</note>

Having no [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) does not mean having no effect. When B does not respond, that too is a result that falls out of B at this moment and [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at A when A notices it ([forced wager](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c11): not acting is also a result). So "no response" may not be written as "nothing happened". This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Not responding is also a way of acting, and it reaches the other party. One cannot say that having no reaction means nothing happened.*

</note>

Where the sequence goes after a [mismatch](concepts.md#c08) is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02): it may be a [repair](concepts.md#c09), and it may also be forgiveness, [conflict](concepts.md#c10), continued communication, and so on; if "forgiveness" is an attribution that some [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) makes about the sequence, then when spoken it is an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), not the compatibility of the sequence. So forgiveness does not imply a [repair](concepts.md#c09), and compatibility of the sequence does not imply that anyone forgave: a [repair](concepts.md#c09) looks only at the sequence, forgiveness is an attribution, and the two can occur independently. Whether forgiveness is genuine cannot be confirmed from outside by [unconfirmability of drives](concepts.md#c03), and feelings are not discussed in this paper. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*"I forgive you" is the speaker's view of that failure to match, and saying it is something done. Having said forgiveness, the exchange between the two does not necessarily fit together; and when the exchange fits together again, it is not necessarily because someone said forgiveness. These two things occur independently.*

</note>

5.1 in Section 5 uses the Still-Face paradigm as a calibration: that paradigm has B deliberately not respond for a period and then resume, which artificially changes the interaction sequence and may produce a [mismatch](concepts.md#c08) and the sequence after it, and then compares them using external records.

<note>

*The real study later on happens to have the mother pause her responses first and then resume, which amounts to artificially creating a "failure to match" and then seeing what happens next.*

</note>

### 3.5 Construction of the Self and the Sources of Material

This section uses Component 1 and Component 3 (Conclusion 2-11, Conclusion 2-13).

The [construction of the self](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c28) is the process by which a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) uses [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) to draw itself out, so the "self" is drawn out, not found. A has two sources of material for constructing itself, and both exist whether or not [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) is present. The first is A's own [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28): by Premise 1, each [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of A [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at A itself before the next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) (the self-feedback of Section 2). The second is B's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at A: it includes B's attribution about A, and also the part of the [exogenous background](concepts.md#c02) that reaches A by way of B, carrying B's errors and B's frame. The emergence of [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) does not add a source of material; it only adds one link that treats one of one's own [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) as a view and processes it again. The formulations that are excluded are: A discovers itself; the self is already there to begin with; B's saying "you are hungry" is a reading of A's inner state; A has no material of its own before having [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01). By the [wall of the blind spot](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c09), the very act of drawing that is now taking place cannot be seen, so what A sees is always only the self that has already been built. That both sources exist is a constraint-level conclusion; that B's attribution and frame, if they enter A's content by way of the [exogenous background](concepts.md#c02), carry B's possible errors, uses the [configuration assumption](concepts.md#c01) and the [exogenous background](concepts.md#c02) and is a conditional conclusion.

<note>

*An infant establishing itself has two kinds of material: one is what it itself has made returning to itself, and the other is the adults' reactions and the environment reaching it by way of the adults. Both kinds of material are there before it is able to step back and think about itself; being able to step back adds only another way of processing the material and not more material. The part given by adults carries the adults' errors.*

</note>

The material of a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can come from three places: [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of external things; the [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) and [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) of others' [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); and the further [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of one of its own [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), the last of which only those with [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) have. Whatever material exists from these places returns, by Premise 1, to that [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) itself and enters its [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) at the next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09). As the number of interactions increases, material increases, but there is no guarantee that all three of these places improve at the same time, no guarantee that the [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) approaches the other, and the other's true [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) does not become readable. Having the ability does not equal using it this time (Section 2). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*What a person does next draws on three sources: their judgment of things, their guesses and impressions of others, and their thoughts looking back at themselves (only those who can step back have this one). All three sources return to the person. More meetings means more material, but there is no guarantee that all three become more accurate, nor that your impression of the person becomes more like the person, and certainly one cannot see through the other because of it.*

</note>

The self-concept of an adult also has two sources, as in 3.5: one's own [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) return to oneself, and the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of others (including others' attributions about oneself) [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself. An adult has [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01); if it takes others' attributions about oneself as views and makes a further [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of them, that remains a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of its own, without having to adopt them directly; but having the ability does not imply being used this time, nor that what is seen is the whole of oneself (the [Law of Attrition](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c26), the [wall of the blind spot](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c09)). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*An adult building a view of themselves likewise has two kinds of material: what they have made themselves, and others' reactions to them. An adult has one more step than an infant and can think "this is only others' view"; but there is no guarantee of thinking this way every time, nor of reaching the whole of oneself.*

</note>

A child seeing a caregiver pick up a coat and walk to the door, and then walking to the door too, offers a short calibration: the past experience of going out is material in the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13), and walking to the door is the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28). Whether the child is excited or uneasy is not written, by [unconfirmability of drives](concepts.md#c03); whether the next time will be the same is also [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02) (the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25)). This is a calibration and is not a source of derivation.

<note>

*A child sees an adult pick up a coat and then walks to the door; one can say that earlier experiences of going out are affecting the child. But whether the child is happy or afraid cannot be seen from outside, and whether it will be the same next time is also not certain. This is only a comparison and not a basis for reasoning.*

</note>

### 3.6 The Boundary Where Second Order Emerges

This section does not use any component directly; it uses only the [configuration assumption](concepts.md#c01) and definitions.

This paper does not explain where [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) comes from, does not claim that it necessarily emerges at some moment, and certainly does not derive it from the quality of care. The emergence itself is an empirical description and cannot be derived. This section is a constraint-level conditional conclusion.

## 4. Theme Two: Coupling and Tacit Coordination among Selves with Second-Order Distinction

### 4.1 Symmetric Loops, the Second-Order Network, and Edges Involving an Infant

This section uses Component 2 (Conclusion 2-12).

If [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) and [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) emerge in A after the assumed stage, the [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) on both sides are symmetric in capability configuration: each [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) has its own small [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31), can treat one of its own [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) as a view and make a further [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of it, and can also make [view attributions](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) about the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of others. There may be two participants, or three, four, or five. This section is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Before, one side could step back and think and the other could not; now both can. Everyone can think "this is my view", and everyone can take what others say as "their view". There can be more people, several interweaving with one another.*

</note>

A [second-order network](concepts.md#c04) is a set formed by [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that have [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) and [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), together with the [couplings](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36) between them: one [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) makes a [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) about the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of another [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), and does not merely react to it as one [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); each participant, at the moment of participating, has both [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) and [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), and neither can be missing. These two acts differ: the object of [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) is one's own [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), the object of [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) is the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of another, and having one does not guarantee having the other. There are at least two participants, but they need not be present at the same time: the party who writes down content is a participant at the moment of writing, and later [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43) does not affect this. The regularities that the network forms in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13), including norms and institutions, are its patterns and not another kind of entity.

<note>

*This is what the earlier "society" means under a new name. There are three conditions: someone takes another's words as the other's view; each participant, at the moment of participating, can both step back and think about themselves and attribute views to others, and both are needed; the other need not be present, and a writer who has long since left still counts. The norms and institutions that grow out of long-term interaction between people are the shape of this network and not another kind of thing outside the network.*

</note>

Therefore, a person alone in a house, reading the words that others left behind and recalling what someone once said, as long as the person classifies that content as the other's view, can also take the person's own understanding of it as a view, and the person who left the words also had [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) and [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) when writing, is within a [second-order network](concepts.md#c04) at that moment. This paper does not draw a separate node for an absent other; the other's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) has already [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at this person and entered the person's [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13), and is covered by the person's own [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and the [exogenous background](concepts.md#c02).

<note>

*A person alone is also in this network: when reading what others wrote, the person takes it as "that person's view". The absent person is not drawn as a separate point, because what the person said has already become part of the reader's past.*

</note>

Structural symmetry does not imply symmetry of [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) or [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). Two [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that both have [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) each obtain only [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at themselves (Premise 4), and the content, moment, and error of what they obtain may differ. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Both sides being able to step back and think does not mean that what both sides obtain or think is the same. Each person obtains only the share that reaches themselves.*

</note>

The patterns of a [second-order network](concepts.md#c04), such as norms and institutions, are not causes. They are regularities accumulated in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13), and they can act on the [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) of a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) only by way of concrete [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10); to say that a pattern "brought about" an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), one must point out which [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Norms and institutions are the shape left by people's long-term exchanges; they do not push anyone to do anything by themselves. To say that a custom "causes" a behavior, one must make clear which occasion of what reached which person.*

</note>

When a [second-order network](concepts.md#c04) contains both A, who has only [first-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c24), and members who have [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01), each edge must be judged separately: the edge on which A sits is a [coupling](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36), but A's end does not have [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01), so this edge does not belong to the [second-order network](concepts.md#c04); only an edge whose two ends each have [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) and [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) at the moment of participating belongs. The judgment is based on the moment of participation, and no permanent label is attached. Whether a given [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) has both at a given moment cannot be confirmed from outside; by the [configuration assumption](concepts.md#c01) it is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02) and can only be assumed.

### 4.2 Multiple Selves Interweaving

This section uses Component 1, Component 2, and Component 3 (Conclusion 2-11, Conclusion 2-12, Conclusion 2-13).

Each [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) is at the same time part of the [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) of others: B's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) can [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at C and D at the same time, and B's attribution about C and B's attribution about D each return to B itself. Not every pair of nodes need have a [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05); which pairs have one is determined by data, and the structure says only that it is allowed. This section is a constraint-level conclusion, but which pairs actually have one is conditional.

<note>

*What one person says can be passed to several people at the same time, and the person's guesses about each of them return to that person individually. Not every pair of people has a shared past that belongs to just the two of them; which pairs have one depends on the actual situation.*

</note>

As the number of people increases, there is no guarantee that the proportion of the whole [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at each [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) rises. By Premise 4, the [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) of each [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) always has a part that has not [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others, so more [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) does not mean more transparent. The formulations that are excluded are: the more nodes, the necessarily more complex; familiarity makes the other transparent.

<note>

*More people does not mean that everyone understands others better. For what each person has lived through, some part never reaches others. Knowing more people also does not mean seeing through them.*

</note>

A [second-order network](concepts.md#c04) is not guaranteed to become a subject that grasps the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) of all its members merely because it has many people. By the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25), as long as one of the [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) in it is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), the next step of that [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) cannot be uniquely derived in advance; by Premise 4, no position can obtain the [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) of all members. So whoever makes predictions about the whole network obtains only part of what [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at each [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) in it and is subject to the same limits; this does not imply that aggregate quantities of the network are unpredictable. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*A group of people influencing one another does not thereby become a "brain" that knows everything and calculates everything precisely. Each person's next step still cannot be calculated precisely, and no single position can see everything that everyone has lived through. So predictions about a group are as limited as predictions about one person.*

</note>

### 4.3 Negotiation of Attribution

This section uses Component 5 (Conclusion 2-15).

By [unconfirmability of drives](concepts.md#c03), attributions among family members are likewise only attributions. What [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) brings as a new act is that a [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) can be brought out and negotiated. When A says to B "you misunderstood me", this example records this sentence as one [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of A; after it [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at B, B's [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) about it is still only a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of B (Premise 2). So negotiation does not guarantee successful correction, and it does not guarantee that either side can confirm the other's drives. The function of [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) is not to see through motives but to let an attribution be brought out as a view. This section is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Between adults one can say "you misunderstood me", which an infant cannot do. But this sentence is also just one more sentence, and what the listener takes in is still the listener's own understanding. Negotiation lets guesses be brought out and discussed; it cannot let a person see the other's real motive.*

</note>

```text
Step 4-3-1: An attribution can be brought out and negotiated (from definition of Second-Order Distinction + definition of Second-Order Network + Conclusion 2-5 + definition of Output)
Step 4-3-2: This example records A's saying "you misunderstood me" as one Output of A; after it Arrives at B, the View Attribution B makes about it is still a Distinction of B (from Premise 2 + definition of Arrival + definition of View Attribution + definition of Output)
Conclusion 4-3-3: Negotiation does not guarantee successful correction, and it does not guarantee that either side can confirm the other's drives (from Step 4-3-2 + Conclusion 2-15 + Conclusion 1-5)
```

### 4.4 Adding Nodes: Third Parties and New Members

This section uses Component 2, Component 3, Component 4, and Component 5 (Conclusion 2-12, Conclusion 2-13, Conclusion 2-14, Conclusion 2-15).

When D is added to the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) that has only B and C, each [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) still has its own small [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31). Between two participants there is at most one edge, so the number of edges does not exceed the number of nodes times the number of nodes minus one, divided by two. Knowing three people does not mean that the three know one another, so how many edges actually exist is determined by data. Under the setting that in a small-scale group of acquaintances everyone knows everyone (Premise 9), there is an edge between every two participants, and the number of edges grows faster than the number of nodes. Which edges actually carry a [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) is likewise determined by data. The upper bound on the number of edges is a constraint-level conclusion; that every two have an edge uses Premise 9, and whether an edge carries a [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) is conditional.

<note>

*With one more person, the maximum number of relationships is the number of people times the number of people minus one, divided by two. But if you know three people, it does not mean that the three know one another; which pairs actually exist depends on the situation. In a small circle where everyone knows everyone, the number of relationships grows faster than the number of people. Which pairs really have a shared past that belongs only to them also depends on the actual situation.*

</note>

B's same [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at C and [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at D constitutes two different [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10): C and D each carry their own [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and each make a [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), and the two may differ; B's attribution about C and B's attribution about D also each return to B itself. Certain [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) between B and C may never [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at D, so D is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02) to obtain the complete [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) between B and C. This is what can be written in the model about what is commonly called "being excluded": certain [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) did not [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10). Why they did not, by [unconfirmability of drives](concepts.md#c03), cannot be written as someone's deliberate doing. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*The same sentence heard by C and heard by D are two different things, each understood in its own way. What B and C said privately, D may never have heard, so D does not have that stretch of shared past. This is all that "being excluded" means; whether it was deliberate cannot be seen from outside.*

</note>

If C relays B's words to D and this relay is recorded as an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of C, then it is a new [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of C, not a copy of B's original [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); if C takes B's sentence as one of B's views, this [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) carries C's [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02). By Premise 2 and Premise 4, a relay cannot pass on B's own segment in full. So the person in the middle who passes words along changes the material that D obtains and does not make D and B [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at each other directly. Whether there is a person in the middle in a given network is conditional.

<note>

*What a go-between relays is new words that the go-between has understood and then said, not the original words. So after passing through a go-between, what D gets will not be the same as what B originally said; this is inevitable. Whether there is such a go-between depends on the actual situation.*

</note>

When a new member A enters a [second-order network](concepts.md#c04) in which B and C already have a [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05), A and B, and A and C, start from zero in the dimensions where there was no two-way [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) before (3.1); the existing [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) of B and C is not thereby reset to zero, nor does it thereby become A's (the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28)). After A joins, the subsequent [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) between B and C can [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at A, and the [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) and [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) of B and C are not a static extension of what they were before A joined (the [Law of Flux](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c24)). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*The newcomer starts from the beginning with each old member; the shared past already existing among the old members does not disappear, and the newcomer does not automatically have it. Once the newcomer arrives, the exchanges among the old members are also seen by the newcomer, and the whole cycle is no longer the same as before.*

</note>

Development changes the dimensions in which A can participate, for example movement, voice, and language. An increase in the dimensions available for participation does not mean that [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) in those dimensions have occurred, nor that a [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) already exists. This paragraph is a conditional conclusion, and the empirical antecedent it uses is that development changes the conditions of participation (Premise 8).

<note>

*As a child grows, there is more it can do, but "can do" does not mean "has done", nor that it has a shared past with anyone. What growing up changes about what can be taken part in comes from observation and is not derived from the rules.*

</note>

```text
Premise 8: Development changes the dimensions in which A can participate.
Note: Premise 8 is an empirical antecedent used as an input to derivation.
Premise 9: Under the setting of a small-scale group of acquaintances, every two participants know each other, that is, there is a Coupling between every two participants.
Note: Premise 9 is a scoping assumption and not a claim about the general case; knowing three people does not mean that the three know one another.
```

### 4.5 Removing Nodes: Exit, Termination of an Individual, and Continuation of Patterns

This section uses Component 2 and Component 4 (Conclusion 2-12, Conclusion 2-14).

After a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) makes its [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43), the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) it has already [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others remain in others' [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13); the part it did not [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others does not [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) because of the [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43); whether it can still [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02) (Premise 4, the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28)). When an individual terminates, if the individual's later position is no longer a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) at the next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09), it is an [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43); the part that has already occurred and [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others remains. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*When a person leaves, or dies, what the person has already said and done and passed on to others stays with those others; what the person did not pass on is not passed on just because the person has left, and whether it still can be is not guaranteed. Later things will no longer happen to the person.*

</note>

Even if a later person makes the same movements, it is a new occurrence at a new position (the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28)) and not a [restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c27) of that person's segment. If the same practice recurs in later people, one can study whether it shows [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37) or [stasis](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c52) in a dimension, but the dimension must be stated. To claim "the same pattern continues", one must list traceable participants, [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), records, or rules; the same name, or a similar appearance, is not enough. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion; which patterns actually continue is conditional.

<note>

*A later person doing as an earlier person did is not that earlier event happening again; it is a new occurrence. To say "this habit has been passed down", one must be able to show who, what they did, and what record was left; the same name or a similar look does not count.*

</note>

A [second-order network](concepts.md#c04) is composed of participants and their [couplings](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36). When participants are replaced, this definition gives no criterion for saying whether the network before and after the replacement is still the same network. So replacement of members implies neither that the network vanishes nor that it continues; to claim either, one must first list a criterion and traceable material. This paper does not give this criterion.

<note>

*When the members change, is this network still the original one? This paper draws no conclusion on this question. To say "still" or "no longer", one must first say clearly what standard is used and produce evidence.*

</note>

### 4.6 Misplaced Attribution: Conflict, Counteraction, and the Labels of Attribution

This section uses Component 3 and Component 5 (Conclusion 2-13, Conclusion 2-15).

Two [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) may attribute the same [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) inconsistently, as already seen in 4.2 and 4.3. After the attributions are misplaced, the sequence may move toward negotiation (4.3), toward [repair](concepts.md#c09) after a [mismatch](concepts.md#c08) (3.4), or toward [conflict](concepts.md#c10) (defined in 4.8). A failure to match is not the same as [conflict](concepts.md#c10): a [mismatch](concepts.md#c08) is that one side's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) did not connect, which can be on one side only and need not have opposite directions; [conflict](concepts.md#c10) requires that the directions of the two sides be opposite, that each target the other's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), and that they strengthen each other (4.8). Refusal, warning, and counteraction are all [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), and enter the same [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) together with other [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); [conflict](concepts.md#c10) is the structure formed when the two sides' [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of this kind target one another, and is not a further [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28). What can be recorded is the statements, the actions, and what follows; from these one cannot fill in the complete inner purpose of either side ([unconfirmability of drives](concepts.md#c03)), nor, because it is [conflict](concepts.md#c10), infer that there is no [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06) (see 4.7). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Things not matching up may be talked through, may later match up again, or may turn into a quarrel. Not matching up is not the same as quarreling: a child cries and you offer a bottle that it will not take, which is a failure to connect; a quarrel is you wanting me to eat salty and me wanting you to eat sweet, and the harder you push the harder I push back. Refusal, warning, and retaliation are things done, like other interactions, and are counted together in the shared past; a quarrel is the shape of these back-and-forths strengthening one another. We can see only what was said and done, not who is thinking what, and we also cannot say, because of a quarrel, that the two do not know each other well.*

</note>

[Conflict](concepts.md#c10) begins after [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10). Refusal, warning, and counteraction are all subsequent [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) made against [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that have already [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10); if between two [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) neither side's [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) have ever [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the other, and none have [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) by way of being relayed by someone else, there is no [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that they can target at one another, and so no [conflict](concepts.md#c10). Therefore, toward a stranger one has never seen and never heard of, [conflict](concepts.md#c10) is impossible; toward someone one has only heard about through relays, [conflict](concepts.md#c10) can occur, and its starting point is the relayed [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), not the other person. [Conflict](concepts.md#c10) is a pattern on the sequence after [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), not a property brought by one side. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion, writing only a necessary condition and not how [conflict](concepts.md#c10) arises.

<note>

*Without having come into contact with each other, quarreling is impossible. A quarrel grows only after two people (or, through someone passing words along) have already met each other's words and ways. Someone one has neither seen nor heard of cannot be in conflict with you; someone one has only heard about through others can. All this says is that contact is needed for it to be possible; it does not say that a quarrel must follow contact, nor why a quarrel happens.*

</note>

A sequence can be attributed by some [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) as "deception" or "coercion". If a sequence is taken as the other's view, these two words are the content of a [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), of the same structure as "he cries because he is hungry" in 3.2: what the speaker has is the other's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and the subsequent records, and "he knows the facts and deliberately misleads" or "he has no other choice" is the speaker's own [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). This does not say that reality contains no deception or coercion; it says that if these two words point to the inner state and drives of an other that is not in [lock in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), then by [unconfirmability of drives](concepts.md#c03) they cannot be confirmed from outside; what is judged by public behavior, procedure, or external constraints is excepted. This attribution, once voiced, is itself an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); the part that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others enters their [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and takes part in their subsequent [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), so it has an effect in the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31); but whether it is correct, the model does not adjudicate. The feeling of being deceived and the feeling of being coerced belong to inner states and are not discussed in this paper. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Saying "he is deceiving me" or "I was forced into it" is the speaker's interpretation of what was seen, of the same kind as "he cries because he is hungry". It is not that the world has no deception or coercion, but that these two words point to something in the other's mind, which cannot be confirmed from outside. Once spoken, this interpretation is itself a thing done and affects the interaction that follows; whether it is right, this paper does not judge. As for feelings, this paper does not discuss them.*

</note>

When a researcher uses these two words, it is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) that the researcher, as a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), makes about the sequence, and it can be a [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) and can also be an [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) of the other's [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); it is not read off from data, and the text must state who made the attribution. The outward descriptions a researcher can write include two: a certain [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) does not agree with the subsequent record; one side issues an instruction and the other side carries it out. These two descriptions do not guarantee that one can separate deception from misreporting, or coercion from voluntariness: a disagreement may come from misremembering, misunderstanding, or being misled by others (the [Law of Attrition](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c26), the [illusion](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c14)), and carrying out may be a choice made when not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) (the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25)). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*When a researcher says "this is deception" or "this is coercion", that is only the researcher's own interpretation, not something read from the data, so it must be stated who said it. What a researcher truly sees is mainly two things: the words and the later record do not match; one side gives an order and the other does as told. From these two, one cannot tell whether it is lying or misremembering, nor whether it is compulsion or willingness.*

</note>

The same approach applies to every word that points to the other's inner state or motive. "Trust" is an [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) that some [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) infers from the other's [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) about roughly how the other's [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) stands toward oneself; "sense of security" and "anxiety" are attributions that some [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) makes about the sequence after a [mismatch](concepts.md#c08) and about the pattern of its own subsequent [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), and the feeling itself is not discussed; "promise" is an attribution that one [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) makes about its own and the other's future [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), and it is spoken aloud, and speaking aloud is an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), not a guarantee; "fairness" is an attribution made about [impact](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c47) and the sequence, [impact](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c47) cannot be added up, and there is no externally available ledger; "emotional contagion and co-regulation" is an attribution that a researcher makes about the [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37) of the pattern of the two sides' [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), and the feeling itself is not discussed; "social support" is an attribution made about one side's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), and its effect can be seen only from the subsequent sequence. Whether what these words point to is true cannot, by [unconfirmability of drives](concepts.md#c03), be confirmed from outside, and the model does not adjudicate; the model asks only what they do after entering the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31): once voiced, the [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) is itself an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), and the part that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others enters their [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and takes part in their subsequent [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). Acting on such an attribution is a [forced wager](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c11), and the result after acting is not a test of the attribution. When a researcher uses these words, it is likewise a [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), and the text must state who made it. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*"I trust him", "I have no sense of security", "I promise you", "this is not fair", "he is soothing me" are all the speaker's own views, of the same kind as "he is deceiving me". It is not that the world has no trust or fairness, but that these words point to something in the other's mind or to a ledger, which cannot be confirmed from outside. Once said, the sentence is itself a thing done, which reaches others and affects the interaction that follows; acting on it is a gamble, and winning the gamble does not prove the view right. When a researcher uses these words, it is likewise only the researcher's view, and it must be stated who said it. As for what the person concerned feels inside, this paper does not discuss it.*

</note>

Whether the other's segment here is a [decision](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c06) can only be assumed from outside; what can overturn the assumption is finding a mechanism that proves that the other's segment here can only come out of a [locked-in round](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c25) or a [random round](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c26). In that case the other's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) in this segment is not a [decision](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c06), and this edge at that moment cannot be counted as a [decision](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c06) made by the other's [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) (4.1); it does not follow that the other lacks [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01), nor whether the edge belongs to the [second-order network](concepts.md#c04) at other moments. Merely seeing compliance does not constitute this proof. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*There is only one case in which one can say with grounds "he had no other choice": being able to point to a mechanism that proves he could only go this way at this step. Then his step is no longer his own judgment, and this relationship does not count as this network. Merely seeing him do as told is not enough.*

</note>

Calibration: in daily life, when one says "I trust him", what bystanders can see is how he entrusts and how he acts; whether he truly trusts is a guess on the bystanders' part. The model's wording agrees with this. This is only a comparison with daily life and not a source of derivation.

<note>

*It is the same in daily life: we can see only how a person acts, and "he trusts me" and "he is deceiving me" are both our guesses. This is only a check against daily life and not a basis for reasoning.*

</note>

### 4.7 Accumulation of History: Tacit Coordination

This section uses Component 2, Component 3, Component 4, and Component 5 (Conclusion 2-12, Conclusion 2-13, Conclusion 2-14, Conclusion 2-15).

[Tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06) is, in a specified type of situation and dimension, where two [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) have gone through a repeated [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05), a behavioral pattern that, in comparable situations, can still continue compatibly without having to be guided item by item each time. The criterion is [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and behavioral pattern, not prediction: it does not require either side to compute the other's next step in advance (the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25)), does not require friendliness, does not require mutual transparency, and does not require a stable [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45). A large number of interactions does not mean that [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06) already exists; more interactions only add comparable material. This paragraph is a constraint-level definition and exclusion; whether a given two people have [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06) in a given dimension is conditional.

<note>

*Tacit understanding means "being able to connect without explaining from the start each time". What it looks at is the record of actual coordination, not who predicted whom, nor how stable anyone's impression of anyone is. It does not equal good feelings: even between enemies there can be a fixed corresponding rhythm; and having spent a long time together does not necessarily bring tacit understanding.*

</note>

[Tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06) is also not classified by moral stance. If hostile parties, in a specified comparable situation, repeatedly show compatible continuations of the kind warning-and-yielding or probing-and-defending, without having to redo complete guidance, they meet [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06) within that scope; from this one does not infer friendliness, trust, common interest, or genuine understanding. Repeated hostility that does not meet the criterion cannot be registered as [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06) merely by the number of years of association. To compare who has more [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06) with whom, the comparison must be made under the same dimension, time window, and acceptance data; it cannot be inferred that enemies in general have more or less [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Even between enemies there can be a fixed rhythm of "once you warn, I back off". This also counts as tacit understanding, but it does not mean reconciliation, nor understanding. To compare who has more tacit understanding, one must use the same yardstick, the same period, and the same kind of matter.*

</note>

The number of counterparts with whom a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) newly acquires [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06) within a stated time window has an upper limit. Suppose the [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) it can receive within this window are limited, and a [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) needs repeated [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) in comparable situations; and the [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) with different counterparts each needs its own [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) (the same [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) arriving at different counterparts is a different arrival); then the number of counterparts cannot increase without limit. How large the upper limit is cannot be derived: [impact](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c47) cannot be added up, and the same [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) has different effects on different [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), so when this is used the assumptions about [frequency](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c48) and [discrepancy](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c49) must be written out. The more people one has contact with, the fewer repeated [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) each pair gets, and the harder it is for [impressions](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) to become stable; this is an empirical description. Without a sufficiently repeated [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) there will be no [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06); this is an [empirical prohibition](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c51), writing only a necessary condition, and can be modified by new cases; conversely, long-term coexistence does not guarantee [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06). This paragraph is a conditional conclusion, and the empirical antecedent it uses is that the [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) each [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can receive within a period of time are limited (Premise 10).

<note>

*The number of people a person can be close to is limited, because a person has only so much time and attention in a day. If one knows too many people and has met each only a few times, there is no time to form the kind of coordination that connects "without much being said". How long or how many counts as too much, this paper cannot calculate. "Long-term association is needed for it to be possible" is a necessary condition summarized from observation, not "long association certainly brings it".*

</note>

Once [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06) has formed, it is not guaranteed to continue. By the [Law of Flux](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c24) and the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25), past compatible continuations do not [lock in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) the next continuation; whether it continues depends on new [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Having coordinated well before does not guarantee doing so next time. Each time depends on what newly happens.*

</note>

A [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) does not imply that two [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) hold the same content. Each [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) obtains only [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at itself (Premise 4), so the same shared experience leaves different material on the two sides. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*What two people went through together leaves different things in the two people's minds; each gets only the share that reached themselves.*

</note>

Calibration: two people who have worked together for a long time can connect without explanation; with someone who has never worked with them, the same matter must be explained again. This is only a comparison with daily life and not a source of derivation.

<note>

*After working together for long, little needs to be said; with a new person, it must be explained again. This is only a comparison with daily life.*

</note>

### 4.8 Weight Trajectories, Friendliness and Conflict, the Compatibility Threshold

This section uses Component 2, Component 3, Component 4, and Component 5 (Conclusion 2-12, Conclusion 2-13, Conclusion 2-14, Conclusion 2-15).

This section is not a seventh operation on the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31); it reads the direction in which the [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) of two [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) are heading. Two [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) with [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) each have their own [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). When the same [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at different [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), its [impact](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c47) and [discrepancy](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c49) may differ according to their respective [histories](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13), and so may the movement it causes in their respective [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). [Convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37), [divergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c38), and [stasis](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c52) under a [coupling](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36) are, along a dimension stated by the researcher, the distance between the patterns of the two sides' [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) becoming shorter, longer, or unchanged; the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) of the two sides affect later [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and thereby this distance, but do not alone determine it, and neither does either side alone. What the researcher obtains from outside is the change in the pattern of [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and in the distance, not the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) of the two sides: each [Self's](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) always has a part that has not [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the other (Premise 4); an increase in the number of interactions does not change this either (3.5). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*The same sentence has different effects on different people, because each person has a different past in hand. Whether two people are becoming more alike, less alike, or staying the same depends on the inner state of each side, not on what either side says alone. What a researcher can see is only what the two sides did and the distance between them, not the inner states.*

</note>

"Friendliness" and "[conflict](concepts.md#c10)" are labels that a researcher attaches to outward patterns. [Conflict](concepts.md#c10) is the [Taiji](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c39) formed between two [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) within a stated dimension and time window: the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of both sides target the other's [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) in that dimension, with opposite directions, and every time one side's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the other, it strengthens the other's own direction, so the firmer one side is, the firmer the other becomes. It can form only if the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of at least one side has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the other (including through relay). Each going its own way in opposite directions, with no [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that target the other, is not [conflict](concepts.md#c10). "Friendliness" in this paper refers only to an outward pattern within the same dimension, criterion, and time window in which the distance shows [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37) or [stasis](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c52) and the sequence is compatible, and no separate concept is set up. Both words are descriptions of the sequence and not of the inner state or drives of either side ([unconfirmability of drives](concepts.md#c03)). When a researcher uses them, the dimension and time window must be stated, and for "friendliness" the criterion must be stated as well; the same pair of [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can be given both labels at the same time in different dimensions, and the same sequence can receive a different label in a different time window. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*"Getting along well" and "quarreling" are outward patterns the researcher sees: two sides drawing closer, or staying put, and still connecting is called friendly; quarreling (conflict) is you wanting me to eat salty and me wanting you to eat sweet, the harder you push the harder I push back, opposite directions strengthening each other. Each eating their own and not interfering does not count as quarreling. These are only names of patterns and do not say what they feel inside. When using them, one must say which matter is being looked at, by what standard, and over how long a stretch.*

</note>

For any pair of [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), including acquaintances with a [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05), compatibility is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02). For [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) to be compatible, one side's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) must fall within the range under the other's [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) that it can connect with; this range belongs to the other's [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17), which does not fully [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) (Premise 4) and cannot be derived in advance from the other's next [decision](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c06) (the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25)). So the framework provides no procedure that can guarantee that the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) is compatible, and every attempt is a [forced wager](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c11). A [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) adds material (3.5) and may also consist entirely of [mismatches](concepts.md#c08), so a lot of accumulation does not imply compatibility. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Even for people who know each other very well, there is no guarantee of getting along. To get along, one must say what the other side can connect with, but one cannot see the other's inner state fully and cannot calculate their next move exactly, so each time is a gamble. Knowing someone for a long time only means more material in hand, and the material can be entirely quarrels.*

</note>

Having material does not mean cooperating. Even if a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) holds enough material to estimate the other's compatible region, having the ability does not imply using it this time (Section 2); even if it is used this time, the tendency of its next [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) is still given by its whole [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) (the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25)), and being compatible with the other in this dimension is only one dimension among them. So even if both sides hold material about each other's compatible region, being in [conflict](concepts.md#c10) is not excluded. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*I have worked with you and have a lot of information about you in hand, and roughly know what kind of wording you like. But knowing your preference does not mean I will speak according to your preference this time: what I say is the result of my whole state, and your preference is only one consideration. So two people who understand each other well can also fall into a quarrel.*

</note>

The absence of a [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) does not imply that compatibility is impossible. By the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25), the next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) of a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) cannot be uniquely derived in advance, so from "there is no [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05)" one cannot infer that the next [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) must be incompatible with the other. "[Not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02)" is not "guaranteed not". Possible sources of compatibility that this paper does not exclude: overlap in the material that the two sides each obtained from the [exogenous background](concepts.md#c02); [specified coordination](concepts.md#c07) narrowing the criterion to a narrow dimension; or a [random round](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c26). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion. The "outsider" here refers only to a counterpart with no [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) in the stated dimension; the more general problem of strangers is left to later papers.

<note>

*People who are not close can also get along: they may have been shaped by the same environment, may have agreed on only one very narrow thing, or it may just happen to be so. There is simply no guarantee. "Not close" here only means having no shared past on this matter.*

</note>

To go further and speak about how high or low the threshold is, two empirical antecedents are needed. Premise 12: the dimensionality of each [Self's](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) in a dimension is very high, and the region that is compatible under the stated criterion occupies only a very small part of the combinations of possible [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of the two sides. Premise 13: in the same dimension, a [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) is material by which a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) estimates the other's compatible region. The "threshold" in this section refers to the amount of new material needed, under that dimension and criterion, to estimate the other's compatible region rather than fall into it by luck. Since the compatible region occupies only a very small part (Premise 12), without material, the two sides falling into the small overlapping piece is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02). For a counterpart with a [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05), this source is already in hand (Premise 13); for one without, this source is zero. ### 4.9 Positive Feedback between Two People: Three Loops, Attraction, Reciprocity, Escalation of Conflict, and Dissipation

This section uses Component 1, Component 2, Component 3, Component 4, and Component 5 (Conclusion 2-11, Conclusion 2-12, Conclusion 2-13, Conclusion 2-14, Conclusion 2-15).

Between two [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) with [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01), within the same specified time window, if each is still a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), their [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at each other, and each one's subsequent [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) are influenced by the other's, there are three [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) in that window: A's small self-feedback [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31), B's small self-feedback [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31), and the large [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) between A and B (Section 2). [Positive feedback](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c32) is a [loop's](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) own [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) coming back around and pushing the [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) further in the direction that produced this [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28). The [positive feedback](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c32) between two people can occur on these three [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) at the same time: A's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at B's [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), B's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at A's [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), and each is in turn pushed toward the direction that produced that [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), so the relation between [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) grows tighter and tighter. This process has no directional preference: along some dimension, if the two sides show [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37) and reinforce each other, the outward appearance is "attraction"; along some dimension, if the two sides show [divergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c38), with their outputs targeting each other and reinforcing each other, the outward appearance is [conflict](concepts.md#c10) ([conflict](concepts.md#c10) is precisely the [Taiji](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c39) formed when two [positive feedbacks](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c32) showing [divergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c38) are subject to mutual [coupling](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36), and the phase in which the intensity rises is called escalation of [conflict](concepts.md#c10); the [Taiji](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c39) must form a dynamic balance, and a mutual reinforcement of Divergence in which no dynamic balance forms is not called [conflict](concepts.md#c10)). "Attraction" is a label the researcher attaches to the former outward pattern; "reciprocity" is one pattern that the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) sequence of [positive feedback](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c32) can show: if, under a stated criterion, one side's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of a certain kind is followed by the other side's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of the same kind, and the reverse also holds, the sequence presents the outward pattern of reciprocity ("reciprocity" is a label attached to the sequence); the framework does not guarantee that such a sequence appears. The two are both labels attached by the researcher and may be names of the same process at different levels of description; no separate concept is set up, and neither is a description of an inner state ([unconfirmability of drives](concepts.md#c03)). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Between two people, three cycles are in fact turning at the same time: mine, yours, and the one between us. My words enter your mind and your words enter mine, and each of us is pushed further in the same direction by what we ourselves have said and done, tighter and tighter; from outside this is "mutual attraction", and seeing them go back and forth is "reciprocity". This force itself is neither good nor bad: the same mutual reinforcement, when heading in a similar direction, is attraction, and when heading in opposite directions, is quarreling ever more fiercely. "Attraction" and "reciprocity" are only names we give to the outward appearance and do not say what they feel inside.*

</note>

Reciprocity does not require simultaneity, because the [rounds](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c04) of the two ends are not assumed to be synchronized (Section 2). Reciprocity also does not require equivalence: [impact](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c47) cannot be added up, [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02) to be fully readable, so it is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02) that one can calculate from them who gave more; each [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) obtains only [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at itself (Premise 4), and the two sides each have a ledger, which may be inconsistent; this is the misplaced attribution of 4.6. The interruption of a reciprocal sequence is not the same as a [mismatch](concepts.md#c08); the criteria of the two differ, and they must be stated separately when used. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*The back-and-forth need not be simultaneous, nor equal in amount. "Who gave more" has no outside ledger to check against; each person has one in their own mind, and the two may be inconsistent. If the back-and-forth breaks off, it is not necessarily "failing to connect"; the standards of the two things differ and should be stated separately.*

</note>

[Positive feedback](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c32) is not guaranteed to continue. By the [Law of Flux](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c24), the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that the two sides' [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) reach differs at every [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09); by the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25), the next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) of a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) cannot be uniquely derived in advance. The reinforcement of this round does not imply the next round. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Getting better and better or worse and worse in this round does not guarantee that the next round will be the same.*

</note>

The [positive feedback](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c32) between two people also cannot be strengthened by an unlimited increase in the amount of [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10). The [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) each [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can receive within a period of time are limited (Premise 10), so when the reinforcement reaches some point, it will be crowded out by other [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) or limited by other conditions; where the limit lies, this paper does not enumerate, and it is decided by data. This paragraph is a conditional conclusion, and uses Premise 10.

<note>

*What a person can take in is limited, so mutual reinforcement cannot rise without end; at some point other things will crowd in, or other conditions will block it. Where it will meet them, this paper cannot calculate.*

</note>

The [positive feedback](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c32) between two people turns only if [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) keep [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the other side. Distance and fixed conditions (living together, the same workplace, shared settings) affect the [frequency](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c48) of [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), and in this paper belong to the [exogenous background](concepts.md#c02) (Premise 6), entering the model only by way of [frequency](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c48). If the [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) of this pair stop altogether, the large [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) stops while the small [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) on both sides do not necessarily stop as a result (Section 2); the [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) that has already [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) remains, and renewed contact is a new occurrence and not a restoration of the original [positive feedback](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c32) (the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28), 4.5). If the [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) merely decrease, one cannot say that the large [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) must dissipate, because other [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) are also entering the [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) (the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25)). So it is written as "without supply it is not sustained", not as "it must dissipate". The conclusions on stopping and decreasing are constraint-level; the distance and fixed conditions that cause [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) to decrease are conditional and use the [exogenous background](concepts.md#c02). A typhoon relies on warm seawater for its supply and dissipates when it leaves the supply; this comparison only helps imagination and is not a source of derivation.

<note>

*The mutual reinforcement between two people turns only if each other's words and actions keep reaching the other, like a typhoon that relies on warm seawater. Distance, whether they live together, and whether they have shared settings determine how much gets across. If it stops altogether, the cycle between the two stops, but the cycle in each person's own mind does not stop, the shared past is still there, and meeting again later counts as a new occurrence. If less gets across, one can say only that the pushing force is smaller, not that it must fall apart, because other things are also having an influence. The typhoon is only to help you imagine and is not the basis of the reasoning.*

</note>

When the large [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) is weak, the small [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) can continue. Every attribution A makes about B and every [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) A produces return to A's own [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) (Premise 1), without needing B's response. So unrequited love (B does not feed back in the way of responding) and long distance (the two sides' [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) are limited by exogenous conditions) are both cases where the large [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) is weak and the small [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) are present. In this case A still has feedback from the other: B's [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that do not respond, [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) relayed by a third party, and the [exogenous background](concepts.md#c02) are all material that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at A. At the constraint level one can write only that "the small [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) can sustain", not that "this persistence is sustained by the small [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31)": from the outward sequence alone it is not guaranteed that one can distinguish whether self-feedback, relays by a third party, or past [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) is holding it up, because [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) is unreadable (4.8). The three [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) also need not be symmetric, and structural symmetry does not imply symmetry of [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) or [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) (4.1). "Liking" is an attribution, not a fact, and the feeling is not discussed in this paper. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Even if the other does not respond, a person can keep liking them, because what the person has said and done, and the person's own view of that other, are still coming back to the person. Long distance is the same: without meeting, the cycle in each person's mind is still turning. But from outside, one cannot tell whether the persistence relies on the cycle in one's own mind, on words passed on by others, or on earlier experience. "Liking" is the view this person gives to themselves and is not a fact; feelings this paper does not discuss.*

</note>

A change in the outward [frequency](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c48) of contact does not imply a change in the two sides' attributions about each other, nor that there is no change. What the researcher can see is the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) on the large [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31), not the small [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) on the two sides. The continuation of a small [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) also does not imply that the attribution stays unchanged: by the [Law of Flux](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c24) and the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25), one's own [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) changes at every [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09). When [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) decrease, the other's changes that do not [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) do not directly enter the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself (Premise 4, the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28)); one's own small [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) processes old material plus one's own [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), and one's [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) of the other may come apart from the other's actual changes. These items are constraint-level conclusions. At a reunion, how large the other's new [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) is in [discrepancy](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c49) for oneself, and whether it triggers a [mismatch](concepts.md#c08), is decided by data and is conditional. This paper does not write "distance does not affect feelings": the model says only that the outward [frequency](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c48) and the inner attribution are two layers and cannot be inferred from one another.

<note>

*Seeing each other less cannot be taken to mean that feelings have faded, nor that nothing has changed. From outside one sees only the meetings and the back-and-forth, not the cycle in each person's mind. That the cycle in the mind keeps turning also does not mean that the thinking is unchanged. While apart, the other's changes did not reach you, so the other in your mind stays in the old shape, and the longer it goes, the more likely it fails to match how the other is now. Whether there will be a mismatch after reuniting depends on the actual data. This does not say that distance does not affect feelings; it says only that the number of meetings and what is thought in the mind are two layers and cannot be inferred from one another.*

</note>

Calibration: two people who grow more congenial the more they talk, or quarrel more fiercely the more they quarrel, are both found in daily life, with opposite directions and the same mechanism. The case in which one keeps liking the other though the other does not respond is also found in daily life. When two people at a distance meet less, the longing in their hearts does not necessarily decrease with it, and at a reunion some feel that the other has changed. This is only a comparison with daily life and not a source of derivation.

<note>

*Growing more congenial the more you talk and quarreling more fiercely the more you quarrel are the same kind of mutual reinforcement, only in different directions; unrequited love and long distance can also find their shadows in daily life. This is only a comparison with daily life and not a basis for reasoning.*

</note>

## 5. Cases

This entire section is calibration and is not a source of any derivation. Each case unfolds in seven steps: the [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23), the initial state, the new [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), the change in [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), the outcome, what it shows, and what can be said versus what cannot be inferred.

<note>

*This section only sets real studies against the model above to see how closely they fit; it is not used to prove any conclusion. Each example is explained in the same seven steps.*

</note>

### 5.1 The Caregiver Suddenly Stops Responding: The Still-Face Paradigm

**Data note.** This case is taken from the study published in 2015 in Frontiers in Psychology by Aureli, Grazia, Cardone, and Merla, which I have read in full. That study uses the paradigm published in 1978 in the Journal of the American Academy of Child Psychiatry by Tronick, Als, Adamson, Wise, and Brazelton; I did not obtain the original 1978 paper. The facts below are all taken from the 2015 study, and it is marked which parts are the researchers' interpretations.

<note>

*The data for this example come from a follow-up study of 2015, and the full text was read. The original 1978 paper was not read, so none of its details are used. The researchers' own interpretations are marked separately.*

</note>

**1. [Cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23).** The scope is the face-to-face interaction of a mother and her infant in the laboratory; the time is one experiment, in four phases. There are two nodes: the infant is A, and the mother is B. The phase instructions the researchers gave to B are treated as one thing [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at B from outside, and no separate node is set up.

<note>

*We look only at one mother and one infant, and only at that short period of the experiment. The instructions from the researchers to the mother count as one thing that reached the mother from outside.*

</note>

**2. Initial state.** By the [configuration assumption](concepts.md#c01), A has only [first-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c24), and B has [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) and [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02). The final sample is twelve mother-infant pairs, with infants three to four months old, born at term, and the mother the primary caregiver, from Italy; originally thirty-one pairs agreed to take part, and the rest were excluded for technical and procedural reasons or because the infant was too fussy from the start. A and B already have an everyday [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) at home, and the experiment takes only one situation from it.

<note>

*In the end there were twelve mother-infant pairs, with infants about three to four months old; the others were excluded because of broken equipment, because the mother could not keep a blank face throughout, because the infant was too fussy, and so on. Here it is assumed that the infant cannot yet look back on its own view and the adult can; this is an assumption and not something the study measured.*

</note>

**3. New [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10).** The experiment has four phases: the mother plays with the infant as usual (three minutes); the mother keeps a neutral expression and gazes steadily at the infant, without touching or speaking (one minute); the mother resumes interaction (three minutes); the fourth phase is play with toys (five minutes), added by the researchers for their own study and not part of the classic paradigm. What changes in this segment is B's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28): B is still present, and "not responding" is also an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that B produces in this phase and that keeps [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at A, of the same structure as "not moving is also a result" in the [forced wager](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c11).

<note>

*Four phases: normal play for three minutes; the mother with a blank face for one minute; interaction resumed for three minutes; and finally play with toys for five minutes (this segment was added by this study itself). The mother's blank face is not "nothing happening"; it is itself what she gives the infant at that moment.*

</note>

**4. Change in [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06).** Only the outward record is written. The researchers coded the infant's state once per second, defining it by expression, gaze, and vocalization; a second coder re-coded twenty-five percent of the sessions, with an agreement coefficient of 0.76. The results: the proportion of looking at the mother with a positive expression was significantly lower in the still-face phase than in the normal interaction phase, and in the resumption phase significantly higher than in the still-face phase but still lower than in the normal interaction phase; the proportion of looking around at the environment was higher in the still-face phase, the overall test did not reach significance, and the pairwise comparison of the two phases did; protest behavior showed no statistically significant difference, and self-soothing behavior did not increase significantly; withdrawal, turning away, and indicators of autonomic stress did not appear at all in the data. In the language of [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) one can write only: in the interaction dimension, after B's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) changed, the [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) of A's [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of the kind "looking at B and showing a positive expression" became lower, and that of "looking around at the environment" became higher.

<note>

*The researchers recorded second by second what the infant was doing, and then computed what proportion each phase took. The results: looking at the mother and smiling clearly decreased, came back somewhat after resumption but not to the original level; looking around increased; fussing and unease did not clearly increase.*

</note>

**5. Outcome.** The experiment ended in the fourth phase. The researchers also recorded the surface temperature of the nose tip and forehead; this case does not use that part of the data, because the link between body temperature and motive is the researchers' interpretation, which this paper does not adopt.

<note>

*The researchers also measured the infant's facial temperature and used it to infer the infant's neural state. That is an interpretation of an inner state from outside, and this paper does not use it.*

</note>

**6. What this case shows.** Two things. First, in an asymmetric [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31), B's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) is an input to A's [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), which can be seen in outward behavior. Second, this paradigm can proceed on outward records alone, without first knowing what the infant wants, which is compatible with [unconfirmability of drives](concepts.md#c03). The still-face phase plus the resumption phase artificially change the interaction sequence and may produce a [mismatch](concepts.md#c08) and the sequence after it (3.4); the proportion of looking at the mother with a positive expression rose again in the resumption phase but remained lower than in the normal phase, but this case specifies no compatibility criterion, so it is not called [repair](concepts.md#c09). In addition, six mother-infant pairs were excluded because the mother could not keep a blank face throughout, which shows that B's own [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) cannot be fully fixed just by following instructions; this is an outward fact, and this paper does not look for a reason for it.

<note>

*Two points: first, when the adult's behavior changes, the infant's behavior changes with it, and this can be said from the behavioral records alone; second, the whole experiment does not need to guess the infant's mind first. One more thing: six mothers could not keep a blank face throughout and were excluded, which shows that even adults' own reactions cannot be controlled just by wanting to; but why, this paper does not say.*

</note>

**7. What can be said and what cannot be inferred.** What can be said: under this design and this sample, a change in B's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) appeared together with changes in certain outward behaviors of A. What cannot be inferred: why the infants changed in this way; the researchers' statements that "the infant perceives that the interaction has been interrupted" and "an expectation is violated" are the researchers' interpretations and not outward records, and this paper does not adopt them; the authors themselves speculate that the still-face phase lasting only one minute may be why the infants showed no clear distress, which is also only speculation and was not tested; the sample of twelve pairs is small, and the authors themselves list this as a limitation; whether this result extends to other durations, other ages in months, other cultures, or everyday family interaction; whether A's behavioral change was caused by B's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) alone; whether an individual infant has [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01).

<note>

*All that can be said is: in this small sample, the adult's reaction changed, and certain infant behaviors changed at the same time. Why, and whether it would be the same in another situation, cannot be said; the researchers' "violated expectation" is an explanation and not something seen. The sample is only twelve pairs, and the authors themselves admit it is too small.*

</note>

### 5.2 The Same Partner versus a Changed Partner: A Dyadic Precision Manipulation Task

**Data note.** This case is taken from the study published in 2025 in Scientific Reports, volume 15, by Heidersberger, Kaiser, Jadav, Mihić Zidar, Curioni, Johannsen, and Lee, which I have read in full. I did not obtain the supplementary tables, so the test results below use only the numbers reported in the main text. The researchers' interpretations are marked separately from the measurements.

<note>

*This example comes from an experimental study of 2025, and the full text was read, but the supplementary tables attached on the website were not read. The researchers' own interpretations are marked separately and not mixed in with the measured results.*

</note>

**1. [Cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23).** The scope is two participants in the same experimental task, standing face to face, each holding with the right hand a grip at one of the two ends of the same handle; the time is one experiment, in five phases. There are two nodes: the two partners. The task rules, the apparatus, and the score displayed after each trial are treated as the [exogenous background](concepts.md#c02) shared by the two, and within this time window do not update with the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) of this case.

<note>

*We look only at two people operating the same handle together. The rules of the experiment, the apparatus, and the score of each trial count as a background shared by the two, which does not change during this period.*

</note>

**2. Initial state.** There were forty-four participants, none of whom had done this task before, in groups of four. By the [configuration assumption](concepts.md#c01), both sides have [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) and [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02); this is an assumption, which the paper did not measure. The task is a "hot wire game": the two work together to move a metal ring on the handle along a curved copper wire to a turning point and back, trying not to touch the wire, with a target time of twenty seconds, and after each trial a score is displayed.

<note>

*Forty-four adults who had not done this task, in groups of four. The task is like the "don't touch the wire" game: two people hold one handle together and follow a curved copper wire without touching it, and a score is shown at the end of each attempt. Here it is assumed that they are all people who can look back on their own view and can take others' behavior as the other's view; the paper did not measure this.*

</note>

**3. New [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10).** The experiment has five phases: first each does one session alone (five trials); then they cooperate with a fixed partner (fifteen trials, called training); then each does one session alone again (five trials); the last two phases have fifteen trials each, one returning to the same partner and one changing to another member of the same group who had just had a different partner in the training phase, with the order randomly assigned across groups. At the start of these two cooperative phases, every participant has the same accumulated amount of task experience. The two exert force on each other through the same handle, and each one's force and movement [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the other by way of the handle. The paper does not say whether the participants knew each other beforehand, so this paper does not call the changed partner a "stranger" and says only "having no [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) in this task".

<note>

*First alone, then fifteen trials with a fixed partner, then alone again; in the last two segments, one returns to the same partner and one changes to another person, in random order. The two people push and pull each other through the same handle, so each person's effort is transmitted to the other's hand. The paper does not say whether these people knew each other before, so here we say only "having no shared past in this task".*

</note>

**4. Change in [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06).** Only outward records and statistical results are written, not causes. According to the paper: on returning to the same partner, the score of the first trial was higher than that of the first trial of the training phase; with a changed partner, the score of the first trial was lower than that of the first trial with the same partner, and did not differ significantly from the first trial of the training phase; from the fourth trial on, the difference between the two was no longer significant. When returning to the same partner, the position and direction of the handle, the trajectory of the hands, and the applied force were all closer to those of the last training trial than with a changed partner, and the wrist trajectory showed no significant difference; in the phase of returning to the same partner, the score did not rise significantly within the fifteen trials. In the language of [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) one can write only: in this task dimension, when cooperating with a partner with a [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05), the [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) of high-scoring [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) is higher in the first few trials; when cooperating with a partner without a [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05), this gap shrinks within a few trials.

<note>

*All that can be said are the changes in the numbers: returning to the old partner, the score was higher from the start and the way of applying force was similar to before; with a new partner, the score started lower and caught up after a few trials. In the old-partner segment, the score did not keep improving.*

</note>

**5. Outcome.** The experiment ended with the last cooperative phase. The paper also reports two results unrelated to this paper, which this case does not adopt: pairs with different handedness performed lower, and the authors themselves state that a larger and more balanced sample is needed; when the difference in the two individuals' own performance was large, cooperative performance was lower.

<note>

*The experiment ended with the last cooperative segment. The paper also found that pairs with different handedness scored lower, and that cooperation was worse when the two people's original performance differed a lot; these are not relevant to what this paper wants to say and are not used.*

</note>

**6. What this case shows.** Three things. First, a [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) is specific to a partner: experience of cooperating with someone else brought no clear benefit in the first trial with a changed partner (no significant difference, which is not proof of no difference). Second, a [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) provides a starting point and not a long-term advantage: the gap disappears within a few trials, that is, two people lacking a [dedicated history](concepts.md#c05) can reach the same score through the task rules and a few trials of adjustment, which is a calibration for "coordination does not need prior [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06)". Third, this study relies throughout on outward measurement alone: scores, trajectories, and forces, without needing anyone to compute the other's next step, which is compatible with "not requiring prediction" in the definition of [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06).

<note>

*Three points: first, having cooperated with someone else does not carry over directly to a new partner; second, a shared past only makes the beginning smoother, and a new partner can catch up in a few trials; third, the whole experiment looks only at visible behavior and does not need anyone to guess the other correctly.*

</note>

**7. What can be said and what cannot be inferred.** What can be said: under this task and this sample, in the first few trials after returning to the same partner, the score was higher and the force and trajectory were closer to before; after changing partner, the score caught up within a few trials. What cannot be inferred: whether this familiarity between partners is [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06) (the shared [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) here is only fifteen trials, about ten minutes, in a laboratory, far shorter than everyday relationships); whether the same holds in other tasks, over longer times, or in other age groups; the researchers' statements of "learning a predictive model of the other's movements" and "negotiating a joint action strategy" are the researchers' interpretations and not measurements; the participants' subjectively reported "more rushed, lower sense of control" is self-report and cannot be taken as a cause; the authors speculate that fatigue is why the score did not continue to rise in the old-partner phase, which is also only speculation; whether the participants knew each other beforehand; whether an individual participant has [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01).

<note>

*There is also much that cannot be said: whether these ten minutes of coordination count as real tacit understanding; whether it would be the same with another task or a longer time; the researchers' "predicting the other's movements" is only an explanation and was not measured; the participants' "felt more rushed" is only their own account; in the old-partner segment the score no longer improved, and the authors guess they were tired, which is also only a guess.*

</note>

## 6. Grading of Conclusions and Falsifiable Conditions

The conclusions of this paper are of two grades. Constraint-level conclusions are derived only from the [Law of Flux](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c24), the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25), the [Law of Attrition](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c26), the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28), and Premise 1 through Premise 4, together with Premise 20 through Premise 22 added by the author. Conditional conclusions additionally use the [configuration assumption](concepts.md#c01), the [exogenous background](concepts.md#c02), or some empirical antecedent. The cases in Section 5 are calibration, belong to neither grade, and are not used to support conclusions of either grade.

</note>

## Appendix 1: Tacit Coordination Is Not Transmitted, and Coordination Does Not Require Every Pair to Have It

What follows is a finite construction that holds only for this synthetic world and is not a real sample. Suppose three [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), B, C, and E, in the same task and the same dimension: between B and C there are three rounds of two-way [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) ordered in time, each round continuing compatibly without needing to be guided item by item again; between C and E there are likewise three rounds; between B and E there are no [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of this kind at all. Under the criterion of this construction, B and C have [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06), C and E have [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06), and B and E do not. Therefore the antecedents of two segments of [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06) do not sufficiently imply a third.

<note>

*An illustration with a small example: B and C coordinate well, and C and E coordinate well, but B and E have never had direct dealings. In that case one cannot say that B and E also have tacit understanding. This example holds only in this made-up small world and does not represent reality.*

</note>

Now suppose the task is that B hands data to C, and C then hands it to E, the order occurring as prescribed and sufficient to pass the acceptance check of this task. [Specified coordination](concepts.md#c07) is a criterion of this kind: for a defined task, according to explicitly listed acceptance conditions, one examines a sequence of actions that actually occurred, and if the sequence meets the acceptance conditions, the sequence is said to exhibit coordination on that task. After this task is completed, there is still no [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06) at all between B and E, so coordination does not require every pair of members to have [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06). Success also does not equal an improved relationship or mutual understanding. For contrast: if three rounds of compatible continuation of the same kind were added for B and E, B and E would also have [tacit coordination](concepts.md#c06) under this criterion, and the earlier difference would disappear. This construction holds only for this synthetic world and cannot be carried over to reality to prove that "such a situation does not exist in reality".

<note>

*Consider one more thing: a handover can be completed by procedure, without everyone being familiar with everyone. B gives the data to C, C gives it to E, the task is completed, but B and E still have no tacit understanding. If three smooth rounds of cooperation were also added for B and E, the difference would disappear. This is still only a check in a small world.*

</note>

Now consider what kind of discrimination coordination requires. Suppose the sets of qualifying responses for two situations have no intersection, and the material and decoding conditions that B obtains in the two situations are the same. If the same fixed response is used, it cannot fall into two mutually disjoint sets at the same time, so the same fixed response cannot guarantee qualifying in both situations. This item does not exclude other material that distinguishes the two situations. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*If the qualifying reactions required by two situations are completely different, and the clues B gets cannot tell which situation it is, then using the same reaction cannot be right on both sides. To answer correctly, one needs clues that can tell the two situations apart.*

</note>
