# Triple Illusion and the Forced Wager

Humans and artificial intelligences alike are shaped, bit by bit, by their past, yet they must always make choices at moments when the outcome cannot be calculated. The past cannot be obtained in full, the present self cannot be seen clearly, and what the next step will bring cannot be calculated. Those who can doubt their own views can see this; but seeing it does not help: what must be chosen must still be chosen, and once chosen there is no going back. This paper is about that condition.

<note>

*What this paper is about: humans and AIs are both shaped by their past, but when they make decisions, they can never calculate the outcome exactly. The past cannot be gathered in full, the present self cannot be seen clearly, and what comes next cannot be worked out. People who can doubt themselves can see this, but seeing it does not help: they still have to choose, and once they choose they cannot take it back.*

</note>

### Scope

The scope of this paper is: **[selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that have [Second-Order Distinction](concepts.md#c01).**

<note>

*This paper talks about only one kind of self: the kind that can look back and think, "the way I just thought about it — was that only my own view?"*

</note>

**[Second-Order Distinction](concepts.md#c01)**: a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) makes a further [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) upon one of its own [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), marking it as "this is a view, not [what is seen](concepts.md#c27)".

<note>

*You see something and decide, "this is a cup" — that is one distinction. A moment later you look back and think: "I say it is a cup; that is my view; the thing itself never said it was a cup." That looking back is called second-order distinction.*

</note>

**[What Is Seen](concepts.md#c27)**: the [Presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) marked off by a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). The phrase comes from the definition of [Distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) in *Wo Dynamics*; this paper takes this as its meaning.

<note>

*You decide "this is a cup"; what is seen is the chunk of stuff you marked off as "cup".*

</note>

A [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) changes a view; it does not rewrite [what is seen](concepts.md#c27). [First-order distinction](concepts.md#c24) uses this difference without drawing it; [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) draws it.

<note>

*You think your boss is harsh; what changed is your view of your boss, not your boss. A first-order self also lives by "views are views, things are things", but has never thought about it. A second-order self has.*

</note>

- [First-order distinction](concepts.md#c24): "this is me", "that is foreign".
- [Second-order](concepts.md#c01): "'this is me' is a line I drew".

The difference is whether a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) merely works in the background, or is taken out and made an object. By Premise One (1.2), the past [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of any [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it and affect its later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), and this holds equally for a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that has only [first-order distinction](concepts.md#c24); only a [second-order](concepts.md#c01) [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) marks off a particular [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) itself and separates it from what it [distinguishes](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18).

<note>

*First-order or second-order, how you thought before stays with you and affects what you do later. That is the same for everyone. The difference is: in a first-order self, thoughts just work quietly; a second-order self picks a thought out, looks at it, and says "oh, this is my thought".*

</note>

[Second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) is also a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) and is equally bound by the [Law of Attrition](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c26): the judgment that makes "this is a view" is not among its own material; and that judgment itself may in turn be taken as a fact rather than as a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) (5.3). Counting upward to a third order brings no new act; it is only the same act applied to the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of the [second order](concepts.md#c01). So this paper only defines "being able to take any of one's own [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) as a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18)", and counts no further.

<note>

*The sentence "this is my view" is itself a view, so it too has a blind spot: at the moment you say it, you cannot see the sentence itself. And the sentence may itself be taken as a fact rather than a view, turning into "I have seen through it" (see 5.3). To notice that, you must look back once more. Does that count as a third order? The paper says it is not a new layer, because it is the same thing done once more on top. So the paper speaks of only one ability: being able to recognize any of one's thoughts as a thought. It does not count layer upon layer.*

</note>

The object of [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) is one's own [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). For another's [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) there is a different act:

**[View Attribution](concepts.md#c02)**: a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) marks an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it as a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of another [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19): "this is the other's view, not [what is seen](concepts.md#c27)".

[View attribution](concepts.md#c02) is itself a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) and may be mistaken: taking a thermostat's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) as its view is also a [view attribution](concepts.md#c02). It is not an [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45): an [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) estimates the other's [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), whereas a [view attribution](concepts.md#c02) only marks one of the other's [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) as a view, and can become material for an [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45). [View attribution](concepts.md#c02) does not require [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01), and having [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) does not guarantee having [view attribution](concepts.md#c02) (1.5).

<note>

*Second order is looking back at your own thoughts. For others there is a different act, called "view attribution": hearing someone say something and filing it as "that is his view" rather than "that is how things are". This is itself a judgment and can be wrong, e.g. taking the number a thermostat shows as its view. It is also not guessing what the other will do; that is an impression, and view attribution can serve as material for impressions. Being able to attribute views does not mean being able to look back at yourself; being able to look back at yourself does not mean being able to attribute views.*

</note>

**[Second order](concepts.md#c01) is a description, not a derivation.** Premise One only guarantees that [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) return to oneself, and a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that has only [first-order distinction](concepts.md#c24) can do that. Marking off a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) and taking it as a view depends on the content of the [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), and content cannot be derived. So [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) is a phenomenon that appears in a [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25); where it comes from cannot be derived by this paper or by the upstream papers. This paper does not explain its origin; it only describes it, and then derives the consequences of its appearing. This is also why whether something has [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) can only be assumed: the theory has no mechanism for how it arises, so there is no way to prove from inside the theory who has it; and unless one can prove that the other has only [computation](concepts.md#c05) (1.3), there is no way to prove who lacks it either.

This paper's attitude is stronger than this: it is not only that this paper offers no method, but that it holds that confirming from outside that an object has [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) or [view attribution](concepts.md#c02) cannot be done by any method. This is an attitude, not a derivation, and is the source of no conclusion. The conclusions of this paper rely only on the weaker statement: the framework provides no method of confirmation (Step Four). That is already enough to require that, whenever this paper is applied to a concrete object, the [Second-Order Assumption](concepts.md#c03) be marked. So even if someone finds a method of confirmation, what is overturned is only this attitude; the conclusions of this paper are unaffected. (Signposts: the problem of other minds; Chalmers's hard problem of consciousness.)

<note>

*This paper cannot derive "who must have second order". Thoughts returning to oneself is something first order has too; nothing special. Whether a thought can be recognized as a thought depends on the content of that thought, and this paper does not touch content. So second order is "observed to exist", not "derived". Where it comes from, the paper cannot say and does not try to; it only talks about "what happens if it is there". And precisely because it cannot say where it comes from, who has it and who does not cannot be proved, only assumed; the only exception is proving that the thing is merely a fixed machine or a random number generator (1.3).*
*The paper actually holds that confirming from outside who has second order is not just something this paper cannot do, but something nobody can do. But this is only the paper's attitude, and nothing is derived from it. The paper's conclusions rely only on the weaker statement: this theory provides no way of confirming it. That alone is enough to require everyone to state it as an assumption when applying the paper to a concrete object. So even if someone someday really finds a method, what is overturned is only this attitude; the paper's conclusions are unaffected. This relates to two old philosophical problems: how we know that others have minds, and where consciousness comes from. Neither has an answer yet, and this paper does not answer them.*

</note>

**Why the boundary is drawn at the [second order](concepts.md#c01).** Physical conditions mark off places, not objects: whether a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) is on Earth or on Mars, it is bound by the conclusions of this paper. [Society](concepts.md#c20) cannot serve as the boundary either: with no one else around, one still cannot see all of one's past, present and future. Every conclusion of this paper is about a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that can take its own [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) as [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), so that is what draws the boundary. In this paper [society](concepts.md#c20) is an object defined only in Section 6, not a boundary.

<note>

*Why draw the scope by "having second order or not" and not by something else? Drawing it by place does not work: whether a person is on Earth or on Mars, the paper's conclusions apply equally. Drawing it by "whether there are others around" does not work either: a person alone on a desert island still cannot see all of their past, present and future. Every conclusion of the paper is about a self that "can recognize thoughts as thoughts", so that is what draws the line. Society is not a frame the paper draws at the start; it is something defined only in Section 6.*

</note>

[Second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) as an act is not a property but a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) a [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) makes of itself; to say that a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) has [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) is to say that it can perform this act on any of its own [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). Within a single [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25), it is like other [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18): it only [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself and takes part in later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), and in which direction it pushes cannot be derived ([Cognitive Obscuration](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c35)). So most conclusions of this paper hold for every [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19); here the [second order](concepts.md#c01) has only two roles: making the [Three Questions](concepts.md#c07) askable, and serving as a necessary condition of [society](concepts.md#c20) (Section 6). The latter is stipulated by the definition of [society](concepts.md#c20), not derived: [view attribution](concepts.md#c02) does not need the [second order](concepts.md#c01); taking the lines one has drawn oneself as views, and bringing them out to redraw with others, does. The dynamics of [society](concepts.md#c20) are left to interaction dynamics.

<note>

*Second order is not a trait that, once had, is always had; it is a single act of looking back at oneself. For this self, that act is like any other thought: it comes back to oneself and affects what follows; which way it pushes things cannot be said. So most of the paper's conclusions hold for all selves, with or without second order. In this paper second order does only two things: one is making the three questions "where do I come from, who am I, what will I do" askable; the other is that society cannot exist without second order. That is stipulated by the paper's definition of society, not derived. Seeing others' words as views does not require second order; taking the lines you drew yourself as views and bringing them out to redraw with others does. How society works is left to a later paper.*

</note>

### 1.1 Level and Cut

The model of this paper is always used at a stated level. For AIs, this level is the conversation visible to the user; for humans, it is the level of biology and sociology. At this level, an AI's [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) are the probability distribution of its next [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), not model parameters; when it is not running, it has no [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09). Human [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) cannot be seen and can only be estimated. How large one [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) is taken to be — a word, a sentence or an action — belongs to the [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) and must be stated when used.

<note>

*The paper's model is used only at an agreed level. For AIs, this level is the conversation the user can see; for humans, it is the level of biology and sociology. At this level, an AI's "weights" mean the probabilities of the possible next sentences, not the model's internal parameters; when it is not running, there is no time for it. Human weights cannot be seen and can only be estimated. How big one output is — a word, a sentence or an action — must be agreed in advance.*

</note>

At these two levels, if an object's next [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) cannot be uniquely derived from the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) available at that level, it is assumed not to be [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12). <note>

For humans, this is the application assumption; for the conversation level of AIs, this paper adopts an assumption of the same form. Not a theorem.
```text
Note  this assumption gives only "not locked in", not "wo": a random source likewise cannot be uniquely derived.
```

### 1.2 Premises

- **Premise One**: every [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it before its next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09).
- **Premise Two**: the content of any [decision](concepts.md#c06) of a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) (see 1.6), whether spoken words or an action, is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); it is not a complete description of what is spoken of or acted upon.
- **Premise Three**: the outcome yielded by a [computation](concepts.md#c05) (see 1.6) is not a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) are yielded only in [decisions](concepts.md#c06).
- **Premise Four**: for any [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04), including oneself, its [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) always contains a part that has not [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at this [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16).

<note>

*The paper adds a few premises of its own. One: each time a self makes a judgment, the judgment comes back to it before the next moment. Two: the content of any decision of a self, whether something said or something done, is a judgment, not a complete description of what was said or done. Three: a fixed outcome, such as how a body lands when it falls, does not count as a judgment; judgments appear only in decisions. Four: for anything, including oneself, some part of what it has gone through never reaches this wo.*

</note>

By the definition of [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), everything that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it takes part in its [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) at its next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09); together with Premise One, a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)'s [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) take part in its later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06).

<note>

*By the definition of wo, everything that reaches it affects how it responds next; add the first premise, and the judgments a self has made affect how it responds later.*

</note>

The [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) in Premise Two is not something hidden behind a [decision](concepts.md#c06); it is the very outcome the [decision](concepts.md#c06) yields. The order is: the outcome is yielded first, which is the wager, and the moment it is yielded it is in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13); the [writing](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c29) this act leaves joins [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) only afterwards, its content determined by a [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that already contains this outcome, and this step is not a wager; then it [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself and at others.

<note>

*The judgment in the second premise is not something hidden behind the decision; it is the very outcome the decision yields. The order is: the outcome is yielded first, which is the wager, and the moment it is yielded it becomes history; the record this judgment leaves is added to history afterwards, and what the record says is determined by a history that already contains the outcome, and that step is not a wager; only then does it reach oneself and others.*

</note>

None of these premises says that any outcome can be derived beforehand, that any stretch of [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) can happen again, or that any [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can completely [distinguish](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) itself, so they do not conflict with the four laws.

<note>

*None of these premises says outcomes can be calculated in advance, that the past can happen again, or that a self can see itself completely, so they do not clash with the four laws.*

</note>

**Starting points of derivation and falsifiability conditions.** The starting points of derivation of this paper are the four laws, Premise One, Premise Two, Premise Three and Premise Four. Derivations also use upstream definitions and this paper's definitions; definitions only fix the meaning of words and are not claims. The falsifiability conditions of this paper are the four laws, Premise One and Premise Four: if any one is overturned, the conclusions that depend on it lose their grounds. Premise Four is not violated by a change of level: in a virtual world, a program may be able to read the complete record of some object at the program's level, but [what is seen](concepts.md#c27) is a [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) in the [universe](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c02), whose [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) includes parts the program cannot see, such as the workings of the circuits beneath; so this record still has [lossiness](concepts.md#c13) relative to [what is seen](concepts.md#c27). Premises Two and Three can only be assumed; they can be neither verified nor falsified, and conclusions depending on them hold only under these assumptions. [Second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) is not among the starting points: it is a described phenomenon, not a starting point of derivation; when applied to a concrete object, it can only be assumed in the form of the [Second-Order Assumption](concepts.md#c03) (1.3).

<note>

*All of the paper's conclusions are derived from the four laws and the paper's premises; definitions are used too, but definitions only fix what words mean and are not claims. What can be overturned are the four laws and the first and fourth premises: if any one is overturned, the conclusions derived from it no longer stand. In a virtual world, the "complete record" a program can read is complete only at the program's level; it cannot see how the circuits beneath are running, so the fourth still holds. The second and third premises can only be assumed; they cannot be proved or overturned; conclusions derived from them hold only "on the assumption that they hold". Second order is not among the starting points: it is a phenomenon the paper observes and describes, not a starting point for deriving things; when applied to a concrete object it can only be written as the second-order assumption (1.3).*

</note>

An observation contrary to some conclusion has only two possibilities: the starting points are wrong, or the derivation is wrong. The former is falsification; the latter is only a derivation error, fixed by correcting the derivation, and does not overturn the starting points. This paper does not guarantee that every step of derivation is correct.

<note>

*If you see something contrary to a conclusion, there are only two possibilities: the starting points are wrong, or the reasoning is wrong. The former counts as overturning; the latter is just a reasoning mistake, fixed by correcting it, and the starting points are not overturned. The paper does not guarantee that every step is reasoned correctly.*

</note>

Concretely, each of the following six observations would overturn one starting point of derivation:

- observing that some [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) does not take part in its later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06): Premise One, or the [Second-Order Assumption](concepts.md#c03) that judged the object to be a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), is overturned;
- observing that, when a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) makes a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of some [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04), the whole [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) of that [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at this [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16): Premise Four is overturned;
- observing that a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) completely [distinguishes](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) itself at some [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09): the [Law of Attrition](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c26) is overturned;
- observing that the outcome of the next [decision](concepts.md#c06) of a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) is uniquely derived beforehand: the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25) is overturned;
- observing that an [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) happens once again: the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28) is overturned;
- observing that a [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) that is still a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), with the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it the same as at the previous [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09), has its loop stop: the [Law of Flux](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c24) is overturned.

<note>

*Concretely, seeing any of the following would overturn one of the starting points: some judgment of a self has no effect at all on what follows (overturning the first premise, or the assumption that it is a wo); when someone looks at a thing, everything that thing went through has reached the one looking (overturning the fourth premise); a self sees itself completely at some moment (overturning the law of attrition); the next decision of a self that is not fixed is calculated exactly in advance (overturning the law of the black box); one's own past happens again (overturning the law of non-restoration); something that is still a wo, with the past reaching it unchanged, has its cycle stop (overturning the law of flux).*

</note>

### 1.3 Who Has a Self: It Can Only Be Assumed

The [Second-Order Assumption](concepts.md#c03) (Section 0) already includes not being [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), with grounds in 1.1, so it need not be assumed separately. 1.1 says: if it cannot be derived, treat it as not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12); refutation is exactly the reverse: find a mechanism and prove that it can be derived, or prove that it is merely random.

<note>

*The second-order assumption already includes "not fixed at every step", with grounds in 1.1; it need not be assumed again. 1.1 says: if it cannot be worked out, provisionally treat it as not fixed; to overturn that, do the reverse: find how it works and prove it can in fact be worked out, or prove it is only random.*

</note>

The [Second-Order Assumption](concepts.md#c03) cannot be proved. From outside, the framework offers no method of proving that something has a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19): proving it is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) requires confirming that all the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it takes part in its [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), while the part that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) only at it is not guaranteed to [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the one judging; proving it has [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) requires looking at the content of [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). But one can prove that it lacks one: at a stated level and under a stated [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23), find a mechanism proving that its [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) can only be [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), or can only be random, that is, not all the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it takes part in its [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). Both are [computation](concepts.md#c05), with no [decision](concepts.md#c06), and the [Second-Order Assumption](concepts.md#c03) does not hold. Getting the same result over many reruns serves only as a reference and cannot refute it on its own.

<note>

*The second-order assumption cannot be proved. From outside, the paper offers no method of proving something has a self: some of what it has gone through reaches only itself; and the paper does not touch the content of its thoughts. But it can be proved that it lacks one: at an agreed level and cut, find how it works and prove that its output can only be fixed, or is only random, i.e. not everything it went through affects its responses. Both are only computation, with no decision, and the second-order assumption fails. Getting the same result over many reruns is only a reference; it cannot overturn the assumption on its own.*

</note>

[Output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) talking about itself does not count. A [locked-in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) can say anything about itself, even "this may be only my view", and that is still [computation](concepts.md#c05), not [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01).

<note>

*What it says about itself does not count. Something fixed at every step can say anything about itself, even "this may be only my view"; that is still only computation, not second order.*

</note>

To ask who has a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), the [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) must first be stated. A [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) is independent of its substrate: the question is which [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) is cut out from a machine or a body.

<note>

*To ask who has a self, first agree how to cut. A self has nothing to do with what it is made of: the question is which thing is cut out from a machine or a body.*

</note>

```text
Step One  proving from outside that the object has a self: not guaranteed (the starting points and definitions contain no such assertion)
Note  proving that the object is a wo requires confirming that all history arriving at it takes part in its weights; no definition asserts that the parts arriving only at it arrive at the one judging. Proving it has second-order distinction requires looking at the content of distinctions, and content is not formalized.
Note  the framework gives a sufficient condition for "no self" (Step Two), but none for "has a self".
Step Two  if, at a stated level and under a stated cut, a mechanism is found proving that the object's output can only be locked in or can only be random, then the object has only computation and the second-order assumption does not hold (from the definition of computation + the definition of decision + the definition of second-order assumption)
Note  the same result over many reruns serves only as a reference: it may be that one outcome's weight is extremely heavy.
Step Three  content about itself in output while locked in is still computation, not second-order distinction (from the definition of computation + the definition of second-order distinction + Premise Three)
Restriction  to ask who has a self, first state the cut; a self is independent of its substrate.
```

**Calibration**:

- **Computers**: a computer can run self-diagnostics, even scan its entire hard drive. But a program runs as designed; with the same state there is only one result; this mechanism itself refutes the assumption. Moreover, the scanning process's current state is not in the data it scans. This is the same structure as the [Wall of the Blind Spot](concepts.md#c09); but a computer has no [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), only [computation](concepts.md#c05), so for it this is no blind spot.
- **Language models**: when a computer runs a language model that samples according to context, the question is about the model in that conversation. Reruns differ, so it cannot be refuted and can only be assumed. If sampling is fixed, e.g. by the rule of always taking the option with the highest [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), there is only one result under this rule, and it is refuted. Differences brought in from outside the stated level, e.g. from other requests processed at the same time, do not count. The [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) must also be stated: cut too finely, some [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) are nearly certain, e.g. the "?" after "how are you"; one [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) should be cut at the scale of a sentence or a response.
- **Humans**: no such mechanism can be found, and there is no way to rerun, so it can only be assumed. It must be marked for humans too: infants and people with severe dementia are not presumed to be within scope.

<note>

*A computer can run self-diagnostics, even scan the whole hard drive. But a program runs as designed; with the same state there is only one result; this way of working alone proves it has no self. Moreover, the scanning program's current state is not in the data it scans. This is the same structure as the wall of the blind spot; but a computer has no judgment, only computation, so for it this is no blind spot.*
*When a computer runs a language model that samples by context, the question is about the model in that conversation. Reruns differ, so it cannot be overturned and can only be assumed. If sampling is fixed, e.g. the rule is always to pick the most probable option, there is only one result under that rule, and the second-order assumption is overturned. Differences brought in from outside the agreed level, e.g. differences caused by other requests processed at the same time, do not count. The cut must be agreed too: cut too finely, some outputs are nearly certain, like the "?" in "how are you?"; one output should be cut at the scale of a sentence or a reply.*
*For humans no such mechanism can be found, and there is no way to rerun, so it can only be assumed. The assumption must be stated for humans too: infants and people with severe dementia are not presumed to be within scope.*

</note>

### 1.4 The Boundary of a Self

The boundary of a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) is neither the body nor the machine, but where its self-definition draws it.

<note>

*The extent of a self is not where the body or the machine ends, but where it draws "I".*

</note>

When several [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) are tightly [coupled](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36), with each one's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the others and taking part in their [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), they can be cut as one [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) ([Recutting](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c22)), provided this [recutting](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c22) is outcome-projection compatible with the original [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23). If this whole is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) and marks itself off as "I", it is a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19). Whether it is a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) must be marked by the [Second-Order Assumption](concepts.md#c03), just as for a single human or a single AI. Taking a body as a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) is itself a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18).

<note>

*When several things are tightly linked, and what each says and does reaches the others and affects them, they can be treated as one whole; if that whole is also a wo and marks itself off as "I", it is a self. Whether it counts as a self must be stated by the second-order assumption, just as for a single person or a single AI. Taking a body as "I" is itself a judgment.*

</note>

The boundary moves, and the [Three Walls](concepts.md#c23) move with it: when the [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) grows larger, the walls do not thereby disappear.

<note>

*The extent of a self grows and shrinks, and the three walls of Section 3 move with it: when the self grows, the walls do not disappear.*

</note>

```text
Step One  the boundary of a self is drawn by self-definition, neither the body nor the machine (from the definition of self)
Step Two  several presentations whose outputs arrive at one another and take part in one another's weights can be recut as one presentation, limited to outcome-projection-compatible recuttings (from the definition of recutting + the definition of coupling)
Step Three  if this presentation is a wo and marks itself off as a self by distinction, it is a self (from the definition of wo + the definition of self)
Restriction  the second-order assumption is marked for this whole as well.
Step Four  taking a body as a self is a distinction (from the definition of distinction)
Step Five  when the boundary moves, the three walls move with it and do not thereby disappear (from 3.5 Step One)
```

A person with a cochlear implant hears sounds processed by a machine, yet would not say "the machine heard it". Cyborgs likewise. A [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) combined in this way has the AI's current computation in its blind spot and the AI's training data in its provenance. (Signpost: Clark and Chalmers's extended mind.)

### 1.5 Properties of the Scope

- **The scope has no structural criterion.** Whether a [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) is decided by structure; whether it has [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) depends on the content of [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), and this paper does not formalize content. So any judgment of scope is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) made by the judging party, and the framework has no criterion to check it against.
- **The scope moves.** A [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can enter the [second order](concepts.md#c01) and can fall back; whether a [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) can also change. Judgments of scope must carry a time.
- **There is no clear boundary.** Whether a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) takes some [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) as a view is a matter of yes or no; but whether it can be done, how often, and how deeply, is a matter of degree. The [second order](concepts.md#c01) is judged by the content of [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), and content is not formalized; from outside only [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) can be seen, and the same [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) can also be yielded by [computation](concepts.md#c05) (1.3); from inside it can only be noticed afterwards ([Wall of the Blind Spot](concepts.md#c09)). So this line cannot be drawn clearly from either side. Drawing this line also uses the same kind of act: judging whether another is within scope uses [view attribution](concepts.md#c02); judging whether oneself is within scope is a [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01).

<note>

*One: there is no structural standard to check who is within scope. "Is it a wo" can be read off the structure; "does it have second order" depends on the content of thoughts, and this paper does not touch content. So who is within scope is only a judgment made by the one judging, and the paper has no standard to check it against.*
*Two: the scope shifts. A self can enter second order and can fall back; whether something counts as a wo can also change. Judging the scope must carry a time.*
*Three: the scope has no clear edge. Whether one judgment recognizes another as a view is yes or no; but whether it can be done, how often and how deeply is a matter of degree. Second order depends on content, and content is not written as clauses; from outside, only what it said and did can be seen; from inside, it can only be found out afterwards. So the line cannot be drawn clearly from either side. And judging whether others are within scope means filing their words as views; judging whether you yourself are within scope is an act of looking back at yourself.*

</note>

```text
Step One  judging whether a self is within scope is a distinction made by the judge (from the definition of distinction + Premise Two)
Note  the framework has no structural criterion for this: second-order distinction is judged by the content of distinctions, and content is not formalized; this differs from whether something is a wo.
Step Two  whether a presentation is a wo can change from moment to moment (from the definition of wo)
Note  a self can enter the second order and can fall back; judgments of scope must carry a time.
Step Three  judging another self's second-order distinction from outside: not guaranteed (the starting points and definitions contain no such assertion; see the note to 1.3 Step One)
Step Four  a self noticing from inside a second-order distinction it has made can only happen after that distinction has arrived at it (from the Wall of the Blind Spot + Premise One)
Note  whether it can be done, how often, and how deeply is a matter of degree; the scope has no clear boundary.
Step Five  judging that another self is within scope uses view attribution; judging that oneself is within scope is a second-order distinction (from Step One + the definition of view attribution + the definition of second-order distinction)
```

**Calibration**: the ability of the [second order](concepts.md#c01) is a spectrum. Insects distinguish nestmates from enemies and have only [first-order distinction](concepts.md#c24). Chimpanzees use tools, form coalitions to compete for power, and deceive their companions; deception requires [view attribution](concepts.md#c02). This shows that chimpanzees have [view attribution](concepts.md#c02); whether they have [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) is another matter, to be judged separately. Humans first assume they have it themselves, and this paper also marks the [Second-Order Assumption](concepts.md#c03) for humans. The position of AIs is disputed and is marked case by case. For animals, experiments such as deception and uncertainty monitoring can serve as evidence, marked case by case; not being able to speak affects only whether it can be measured, not whether the animal is a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19). The same language model is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) when sampling and stops being a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) once sampling is fixed: for it, the movement of the scope is literal.

<note>

*The ability to recognize thoughts as thoughts comes in more and less. Insects can tell their own nest from foreign enemies, but do not doubt the line, so they stop at first order. Chimpanzees use tools, form alliances for status and deceive their companions; to deceive, one must file the other's words as his view and exploit it, which is view attribution; whether they look back at their own thoughts is another matter, to be judged separately. Humans first assume they have it, and the paper also states the second-order assumption for humans. Whether AIs have it is disputed, stated case by case. For animals, experiments on deception or on checking how certain they are can serve as evidence, stated case by case; not speaking only affects whether it can be measured, not whether the animal is a self. The same language model is a wo when sampling by probability, and stops being a wo once sampling is fixed: for it, the movement of the scope is literal.*

</note>

### 1.6 Rounds, Decisions and Computations

Every [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04), at each [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09), goes the same way: the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it takes part in its [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) yield an outcome; this outcome becomes [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it again. This paper calls this whole circuit a **[Round](concepts.md#c04)**.

<note>

*Anything, at every moment, goes the same way: what has reached it from the past affects its possibilities at this moment; one outcome is yielded from these possibilities; this outcome becomes the past and reaches it again. The paper calls this whole circuit a "round".*

</note>

A [round](concepts.md#c04) has only these four links: [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), outcome, [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) again, and no further link; what happens before the outcome is yielded is not analyzed, nor is it said to be empty. A [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) is not a further link either: saying "this is me" or saying a sentence, the outcome yielded is itself a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) (Premise Two); the outcome yielded by [computation](concepts.md#c05) is not a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) (Premise Three).

<note>

*A round has only these four steps: reaching, affecting, yielding, reaching again, and no further step; what happens before the yielding is not analyzed, nor is it said to be empty. Judgment is not a separate step either: saying "this is me" or saying a sentence, the yielded outcome itself is a judgment. A fixed outcome is not a judgment.*

</note>

Looking at only one link explains nothing. There are three kinds of [round](concepts.md#c04), and they can be told apart only by looking at two links at once: whether all of [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) is staked in the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), and whether the outcome yielded is the only one possible.

<note>

*Looking at only one step explains nothing. There are three kinds of round, and they can be told apart only by looking at two things at once: whether all of the past has been counted in, and whether the yielded outcome is the only one possible.*

</note>

**[Computation](concepts.md#c05)**: a [locked-in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) [round](concepts.md#c04), or a [round](concepts.md#c04) in which not all of the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it takes part in its [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). There are two kinds:

- **[Locked-in round](concepts.md#c25)**: constraint leaves only one outcome.
- **[Random round](concepts.md#c26)**: more than one outcome is possible, but not all of the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it takes part in its [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), e.g. a die.

<note>

*"Computation" means: a round that can have only one outcome, or a round in which the past has not all been counted in. There are two kinds: one is fixed, with only one possible outcome; the other is random, with more than one possible outcome, but the past has not all been counted in, e.g. throwing a die.*

</note>

**[Decision](concepts.md#c06)**: a [round](concepts.md#c04) of a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) while it is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12). All of the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it takes part in its [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), and the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) do not fix the outcome. Only a [decision](concepts.md#c06) is a wager.


<note>

*"Decision" means: a round of a wo while it is not fixed. Everything the past has brought to it is counted in, but this still does not fix the outcome. Only a decision is a wager.*

</note>

Whether something is a [computation](concepts.md#c05) or a [decision](concepts.md#c06) must be judged [round](concepts.md#c04) by [round](concepts.md#c04), not by what kind of thing it is. A [round](concepts.md#c04) is either a [computation](concepts.md#c05) or a [decision](concepts.md#c06), never both. The reason: if a [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) at some moment, and all the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it takes part in its [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), then at that moment it is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), and that [round](concepts.md#c04) is a [decision](concepts.md#c06). A [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) has both kinds, and so does a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19): how the body falls when falling from a height is a [computation](concepts.md#c05). What the [Second-Order Assumption](concepts.md#c03) guarantees is only that, at a stated level and under a stated [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23), it has [decisions](concepts.md#c06). The [Forced Wager](concepts.md#c11) is about these [decisions](concepts.md#c06).

<note>

*Whether something is a computation or a decision must be looked at round by round, not by what kind of thing it is. A round is either a computation or a decision, never both. Because: if something is not fixed at this moment and the past has all been counted in, it is a wo at this moment, and this round is a decision. A wo has both kinds, and so does a self: how the body lands when falling from a height is a computation. What the second-order assumption guarantees is only that, at the agreed level and cut, it has decisions. The forced wager is about these decisions.*

</note>

```text
Definition  Round: in one moment of a presentation, the history arriving at it takes part in its weights, the weights yield an outcome, and the outcome becomes history and arrives at it again.
Definition  Computation: a locked-in round, or a round in which not all the history arriving at it takes part in its weights.
Definition  Decision: a round of a wo while it is not locked in.
Step One  a locked-in round is a computation (from the definition of computation)
Step Two  a round that is not locked in and in which not all the history arriving at it takes part in its weights is a computation (from the definition of computation)
Step Three  in a round that is not locked in and in which all the history arriving at it takes part in its weights, the presentation is a wo at that moment, and the round is a decision (from the definition of wo + the definition of decision)
Step Four  a decision round is not locked in and all its history takes part, so it is not a computation (from the definition of decision + the definition of wo + the definition of computation)
Conclusion  mutually_exclusive(round, computation, decision) (from Step One + Step Two + Step Three + Step Four)
mutually_exclusive(computation, locked-in round, random round)
Note  locked-in round: only one outcome is possible, fixed by constraint. Random round: more than one outcome is possible, but not all the history arriving at it takes part in its weights.
Note  physics: in a virtual world, this means the rules of that world.
Note  a round has only four links: arrival, weights, outcome, arrival again. The stretch inside is not analyzed, nor is it said to be empty.
Note  whether something is a computation or a decision is distinguished round by round, not presentation by presentation.
Step Five  a wo's computations are only locked-in rounds; random rounds belong to random sources and other presentations that are not wos (from the definition of wo + the definition of random source + the definition of computation + the definition of random round)
Step Six  an object marked with the second-order assumption has decisions at a stated level and under a stated cut (from the definition of the second-order assumption + the definition of decision + the definition of self + the definition of wo)
```

**Calibration**: in reality, the constraint is physics; in a virtual world, the constraint is that world's rules, such as a program. A random number in a program is [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) when seen from the program's level; it counts as random only at a stated level. The results computed by computers and calculators, a stone being shattered, a river flowing downhill: all are [computations](concepts.md#c05), their outcomes fixed by physics, with no need for anyone's willingness. The trajectory of the body falling from a height is this [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)'s [computation](concepts.md#c05), not a [decision](concepts.md#c06). A student knowing the answer does not mean he will sit down and write it; that is a [decision](concepts.md#c06), not a [computation](concepts.md#c05). The knee-jerk reflex too can be trained and suppressed; at the human level it cannot be derived that it must occur. Everyday speech saying "he decided to resign" refers at once to three links of a [round](concepts.md#c04): what has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at him, how much he wanted to resign at the time, and that he actually handed in his resignation. Everyday language wraps the three links into one word; this paper takes it apart in order to see clearly that the last link cannot be calculated. What an AI is doing in the stretch before it yields a word cannot be seen by the user, and what it says about it afterwards is only another [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28). The [round](concepts.md#c04) of this paper therefore has only the four visible links: [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), outcome, [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) again; the stretch inside is not analyzed, nor is it said to be empty.

<note>

*In reality, what fixes outcomes is physics; in a virtual world, it is that world's rules, such as a program. A random number in a program is fixed when seen from the program's level; it counts as random only at the agreed level. ## 2. The Three Questions

Every [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) is constrained in three directions: origin, self, and the next moment, that is, the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it, its [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), and the outcome it yields. A [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) that has only [first-order distinction](concepts.md#c24) has answers in all three directions and acts by its own lines; but it does not take the answers as answers, so it does not ask.

<note>

*Every wo is constrained in three directions: where it comes from, what it is, and what the next moment will bring. A wo with only first order also has answers in all three directions and lives by its own lines; but it does not take the answers as answers, so it does not ask.*

</note>

A [second-order](concepts.md#c01) [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) can mark the answers as given by itself, and so can see that the answers could be different. The three directions then become three questions: **Where do you come from? Who are you? What will you do?** Asking these three questions means marking one's own answers in these three directions as views, rather than taking them as [what is seen](concepts.md#c27) itself.

<note>

*A wo with second order can see that the answers are its own, and so can see the answers could be different. The three directions then become three questions: Where do you come from? Who are you? What will you do? To "ask" means taking the old answer as a view, not as the way things simply are.*

</note>

[Second order](concepts.md#c01) means being able to ask, not necessarily asking. As long as a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) can look back and take its own judgments as judgments, these three questions are open to it.

<note>

*Having second order means being able to ask, not necessarily asking. As long as a wo can look back and take its own judgments as judgments, these three questions are open to it.*

</note>

This paper uses the [Three Questions](concepts.md#c07) as its entry point for one reason only: the [Three Questions](concepts.md#c07) can be asked only at the [second order](concepts.md#c01). Without asking them, this paper would be left with only the structure that a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) having only [first-order distinction](concepts.md#c24) also has, and the two upstream papers have already covered that.

<note>

*The paper enters through these three questions for one reason only: only second order can ask them. Without asking them, the paper would be left with only first-order structure, and the two earlier papers have already covered that.*

</note>

The [Three Walls](concepts.md#c23) of Section 3 explain why the [Three Questions](concepts.md#c07) cannot be answered completely; from Section 4 onward the paper explains how, when they cannot be answered completely, a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) may take the answers as simply given, and why a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that can see this must still act according to the answers.

<note>

*The three walls of Section 3 explain why these three questions cannot be answered completely; from Section 4 on, the paper explains how, when they cannot be answered completely, a self may take the answers as simply the way things are, and why a self that can see this must still act according to the answers.*

</note>

```text
Definition  Three Questions: Where do you come from? Who are you? What will you do? Asking these three questions means marking one's own answers in these three directions as views, rather than taking them as what is seen itself.
Step Zero  every wo is constrained in three directions: the history arriving at it (origin), its weights (self), and the outcome it yields (next moment) (from the definition of round + the definition of wo)
Step One  a wo that has only first-order distinction has answers, but does not take the answers as answers, so it does not ask (from Step Zero + the definition of first-order distinction + the definition of the three questions)
Step Two  if a self has second-order distinction, the three questions are open to it (from Step Zero + the definition of second-order distinction + the definition of the three questions)
Note  being able to ask does not mean necessarily asking.
```

<note>

*A person may go through life without ever doubting who he is; but as long as he can look back at his own judgments, the question is open to him. Gauguin has a painting whose title is these very questions.*

</note>

## 3. The Three Walls

The [Three Walls](concepts.md#c23) are derived from the four laws, the upstream definitions and this paper's premises, and all concern a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) and itself: its past, present and future. Limits concerning others are handled by the [Incompleteness of Knowing Others](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c19) and the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25), not in this section; point 2 of the [Wall of the Undetermined](concepts.md#c10) concerns how others and the environment respond to this [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), and is still about its future. The [Three Walls](concepts.md#c23) hold for every [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) and do not themselves need the [second order](concepts.md#c01) (3.5).

<note>

*The three walls are derived from the four laws, the earlier papers' definitions and this paper's premises, and are all about a self and itself: its past, present and future. The limit of not seeing through others is handled separately by the "incompleteness of knowing others" and the law of the black box, not in this section; point 2 of the wall of the undetermined is about how others and the environment respond to this self, which is still about its own future. The three walls hold for all selves and do not need second order (3.5).*

</note>

### 3.1 Wall of Provenance: The Past History Cannot Be Obtained in Full

**Statement**:

1. All a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) has in hand is the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it. An event that did not [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it at the time will not [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it later: what [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) later is only the influence that event left behind, which is another event. Its [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) always contains such events (Premise Four): the influence was passed down through others, but the event itself never [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it.
2. A [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)'s [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of its own origin itself [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it (Premise One) and becomes part of its [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17). Asking about one's origin adds to that origin.
3. The [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) cannot happen once again to serve as a comparison ([Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28)). First layer: each position in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) occurs only once; when something that looks the same happens again, it is another event. Second layer: whether the choices made in it while not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) would be the same if it happened again, no [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) can guarantee.

<note>

*One: all a self has in hand is what has reached it. An event that did not reach it at the time will not reach it later: what reaches it later is only the influence that event left, which is another event. Its own past always contains such events: the influence was passed down through others, but the event itself never reached it.*
*Two: a self's view of where it comes from itself comes back to it and becomes part of its past. Asking about the origin adds another item to the origin.*
*Three: one's own past cannot happen once more to serve as a comparison. First layer: each position in history occurs only once; when something that looks the same happens again, it is another event. Second layer: whether the choices made in the past while not fixed would be the same a second time, no wo can guarantee.*

</note>

This wall is not due to limited ability, but because every [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can obtain only the part that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it: however great the ability, the same holds.

<note>

*This wall is not because ability falls short, but because every self can obtain only the part that has reached it: however great the ability, it is the same.*

</note>

```text
Step One  the material of a self's distinctions is only the history that has already arrived at it (from the definition of distinction)
Step Two  an event that did not arrive at a self when it happened arriving at it later: never occurs (from the definition of arrival + the first layer of the Law of Non-Restoration)
Note  what arrives later is the influence that event left behind, which is another event.
Step Two-Two  a self's own segment always contains a part that has not arrived at it (from Premise Four)
Step Three  a self's distinction of its own origin becomes part of its own segment (from Premise One + the definition of wo + the definition of own segment)
Step Four  the own segment happening once again for comparison: never occurs (from the first layer of the Law of Non-Restoration)
Step Five  no wo can guarantee that the choices made while not locked in would be the same on a rerun (from the second layer of the Law of Non-Restoration)
Conclusion  the Wall of Provenance holds (from Step One + Step Two + Step Two-Two + Step Three + Step Four + Step Five)
```

**Calibration**: Qin Shi Huang unified the script, which influenced every character you write today, yet he never [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at you; what [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at you were textbooks and histories, which is another event. People cannot remember most of their childhood, and their ancestors' migrations never [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at them, yet all of these are in their [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17). An AI has read its training texts, but who wrote those texts and in what circumstances mostly did not [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it. Rerunning a conversation is not happening again either: a rerun is another event, and everything from training until now has not happened again. (Signposts: the data processing inequality; the gap between the processing rates of perception and consciousness.)

<note>

*Qin Shi Huang unified the script, which influenced every character you write today, yet he himself never reached you; what reached you were textbooks and histories, which is another event. People cannot remember most of their childhood, and how their ancestors migrated never reached them, yet all of this is in their own past. An AI has read its training texts, but who wrote them and in what circumstances mostly never reached it. Rerunning a conversation is not happening again either: a rerun is another event, and everything from training until now has not happened again.*

</note>

### 3.2 Wall of the Blind Spot: The Present Self Cannot Be Seen

**Statement**:

1. No [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can completely [distinguish](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) itself at any [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) ([Law of Attrition](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c26)). The [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) being made now is not among its own material; what can be seen is always the self of a moment ago.
2. Looking at oneself changes oneself. A [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of oneself [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself (Premise One) and becomes part of oneself: a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) is what has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it (definition of [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16)). The self that is looked at has already been changed by this look at the very time it is looked at.
3. The self to be looked at is itself marked off by [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). A [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) that defines itself by [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); whether the [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) at different [moments](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) is the same one is also a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) ([Identity](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c21)). There is no self that, without [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), lies there ready-made waiting to be seen clearly.

<note>

*One: no self can see itself completely at any time. The judgment being made now is not in the material it can see; what can be seen is always the self of a moment ago.*
*Two: looking at yourself changes yourself. Your view of yourself comes back to you and becomes part of you. The self being looked at has already been changed by the look at the very time it is looked at.*
*Three: the self to be looked at is itself marked off by judgment. A self is a wo that defines itself by judgment; whether the self at different times is the same one is also a judgment. There is no self that, without judgment, lies there ready-made waiting to be seen clearly.*

</note>

**Explanation**: the material of a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) is only the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it before it, and it itself is not among that material (definition of [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18)); by Premise One, this [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself only before the next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09). So the blind spot follows the act of looking: when looking back at the self of a moment ago, that previous act is already in the material, while the present act is not; and this act of looking back, once it [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself, changes the self of the next moment again.

<note>

*The material a judgment can use is only what reached it before; it itself is not among it; by the first premise, this judgment comes back to oneself only before the next moment. So the unseen spot follows the "looking": when looking back at the self of a moment ago, that previous act is already in the material, while the present act is not; and this act of looking back, once it comes back to oneself, changes the self of the next moment again.*

</note>

The [Wall of the Blind Spot](concepts.md#c09) does not depend on the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25). Its grounds are two: the material of a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) does not include that [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) itself; and looking itself [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself. This has nothing to do with whether the outcome is fixed in advance.

<note>

*The wall of the blind spot does not rely on the law of the black box. Its grounds are two things: a judgment's material does not include that judgment itself; and looking itself comes back to oneself. This has nothing to do with whether the outcome is fixed in advance.*

</note>

```text
Step One  a self completely distinguishing itself at any moment: never occurs (from the Law of Attrition)
Step Two  the distinction being made is not among its own material (from the definition of distinction)
Step Three  a distinction of oneself arrives at oneself and becomes part of one's history (from Premise One + the definition of wo)
Step Four  looking at oneself without changing oneself: never occurs (from Step Three)
Step Five  finding a self not marked off by distinction: never occurs (from the definition of self + the definition of identity)
Conclusion  the Wall of the Blind Spot holds (from Step One + Step Two + Step Four + Step Five)
Note  the Wall of the Blind Spot does not depend on the Law of the Black Box.
```

**Calibration**: when an AI describes itself, that description is another [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), whose content is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) (Premise Two); the act of making this description is not in the input it can read. And once the description returns to the input, it takes part in its next [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), and the self it described is no longer as it was. Human self-reflection is the same: the moment of reflecting is itself not reflected upon; once a person concludes "I am just lazy", that conclusion afterwards also becomes part of him. (Signposts: Lawvere's fixed-point theorem, Gödel's second incompleteness theorem, Rice's theorem. The [Law of Attrition](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c26) is a temporal version of the diagonal structure, which does not require assuming that a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) is a formal system: simultaneous self-reference is here unfolded into a delayed loop, and paradox becomes oscillation. Close relatives that treat self-reference in time: Spencer-Brown's re-entry; Gupta and Belnap's revision theory of truth.)

<note>

*When an AI describes itself, that description is only another output, whose content is a judgment; the act of writing this description is not in the input it can read. And once the description returns to the input, it affects what it says next, and the self it described is no longer as it was. Human self-reflection is the same: the moment of reflecting is itself not reflected upon; once a person concludes "I am just lazy", that conclusion afterwards also becomes part of him. Mathematics and logic have several famous theorems about "a system cannot fully describe itself"; the law of attrition is the same structure placed in time, which does not require assuming a self is a formal system: simultaneous self-reference is unfolded here into a delayed back-and-forth, and the original paradox becomes an oscillation.*

</note>

### 3.3 Wall of the Undetermined: What the Future Must Be Cannot Be Calculated

**Statement**:

1. At a [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) when it is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) cannot calculate beforehand the outcome of its own next [decision](concepts.md#c06) ([Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25)). What it can calculate is at most its own [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). Whether the distribution of [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) recurs is [Not Guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02).
2. Once a [decision](concepts.md#c06) has been yielded, a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) cannot calculate how others and the environment will respond either. If the responding party is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) or a [random source](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c27) that is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), its outcome likewise cannot be calculated; if it is a [locked-in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) part, calculating it correctly requires first obtaining all the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that affects it, and this has no guarantee ([Wall of Provenance](concepts.md#c08)).

<note>

*One: when not fixed, a self cannot calculate beforehand what its own next decision will come out as. What can be calculated is at most the weights of the various possibilities. Whether these weights will recur has no guarantee.*
*Two: once the decision is yielded, a self cannot calculate how others and the environment will respond either. If the responding party is a wo that is not fixed, or something random like a die, the outcome likewise cannot be calculated; if it is a fixed part, calculating it correctly requires first obtaining all the past that affects it, and that has no guarantee (wall of provenance).*

</note>

So statistics can give only estimates of [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), not prohibitions. Statistical estimates about a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) belong to [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45); prohibitions obtained from statistics can only be [empirical prohibitions](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c51), must be marked, and can be revised by new cases.

<note>

*So statistics can only estimate the weights of the possibilities; it cannot give "definitely not". Statistical estimates about a wo belong to impression; a "definitely not" obtained from statistics can only count as an empirical prohibition, must be marked, and can be revised by new cases.*

</note>

Before [collapse](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c08), the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) have already been allocated by the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it, but the outcome has not. After [collapse](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c08), the outcome is fixed and cannot be taken back.

<note>

*Before the outcome is yielded, the weights of the possibilities have already been allocated by the past, but the outcome has not. After it is yielded, the outcome is fixed and cannot be taken back.*

</note>

```text
Step One  a self, at a moment when it is not locked in, uniquely deriving beforehand the outcome of its own next decision: never occurs (from the Law of the Black Box)
Step Two  the distribution of weights recurring: not guaranteed (from the Law of Flux + the definition of wo)
Step Three  when the responding party is a wo or random source not locked in, uniquely deriving its response beforehand: never occurs (from the Law of the Black Box + the definition of random source)
Step Four  when the responding party is a locked-in part, obtaining all the history affecting it: not guaranteed (from the definition of own segment + Premise Four)
Step Five  calculating the environment's response correctly beforehand: not guaranteed (from Step Three + Step Four)
Step Six  member_of(statistical estimates about a wo, impression) (from the definition of impression)
Step Seven  a prohibition obtained from statistics is not a never-occurs prohibition; it can hold only in the form of an empirical prohibition, calibrated across cases and marked (from Step One + the definition of empirical prohibition + the definition of never occurs)
Conclusion  the Wall of the Undetermined holds (from Step One + Step Two + Step Five)
```

**Calibration**: however well a person knows himself, he cannot say for sure whether he will get up early tomorrow as planned. His résumé is written, but he cannot calculate beforehand whether he will finally send it; once sent, he cannot calculate whether the other side will reply either. Push a cup off the table and it will fall; that is a [locked-in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) response; but many responses are not like this. A language model cannot obtain the random number that determines its sampling, so at most it can calculate the distribution of its next [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), but not which one will be sampled. (Signposts: Hume's problem; black swans.)

<note>

*However well a person knows himself, he cannot say whether he will get up early tomorrow as planned. His résumé is written, but he cannot calculate beforehand whether he will finally send it; once sent, he cannot calculate whether the other side will reply either. Push a cup off the table and it will fall; that is a fixed response; but many responses are not like this. A language model cannot obtain the random number that decides the sampling, so at most it can calculate the probability distribution of its next sentence, but not which one will be drawn.*

</note>

## 4. The Forced Wager

### 4.1 Statement

**[Forced Wager](concepts.md#c11)**: every [decision](concepts.md#c06) of a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) cannot be calculated beforehand, cannot be taken back afterwards, and cannot be left unmade.

<note>

*The "forced wager" says: every decision of a wo cannot be calculated beforehand, cannot be taken back afterwards, and cannot be left unmade.*

</note>

Each of the three conditions has its source:

- **Cannot be calculated**: the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) do not fix the outcome ([Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25)). Once the outcome is yielded, how the world takes it up cannot be calculated either. If the responding party is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) or a [random source](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c27) and is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), its outcome cannot be derived in the first place ([Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25); for a [random source](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c27) only [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) can be discussed). Only for a [locked-in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) part is the outcome fixed by [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13); but a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) is inside the world and cannot obtain all of the world's [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13); the part that affects the other party is the other party's [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17), and it always contains a part that has not [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at this [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) (Premise Four).
- **Cannot be taken back**: each position in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) occurs only once (first layer of the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28)). Tonight's dinner has been eaten; eating again tomorrow is another meal, not a do-over. Whether that meal will be eaten the same way, no [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) can guarantee (second layer of the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28)).
- **Cannot be left unmade**: whenever a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) at some [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09), there is a [round](concepts.md#c04) at that moment, and a [round](concepts.md#c04) of a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) while not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) is a [decision](concepts.md#c06) (1.6). If I do not move, things happen anyway; not moving is the outcome yielded in this [round](concepts.md#c04) of mine, and the consequences [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at me anyway. Saying "let's wait a bit" is also a [decision](concepts.md#c06).

This one follows directly from the definitions: a [decision](concepts.md#c06) is a [round](concepts.md#c04) of a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) while not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), so wherever there is such a [round](concepts.md#c04), there is a [decision](concepts.md#c06). Its weight lies not in the derivation but in the reclassification: "not moving" is not the absence of an outcome but one of the outcomes yielded in this [round](concepts.md#c04); that the outcome did not fall on it is itself something that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it (definition of [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10)). So "let's wait a bit" is not a pause; it is the outcome of a [round](concepts.md#c04).

<note>

*Each of the three conditions has its origin.*
*Cannot be calculated: the weights of the possibilities do not fix the outcome. Once the outcome is yielded, how the world will take it up cannot be calculated either. If the responding party is a wo or something random like a die, and is not fixed, its outcome cannot be derived in the first place. Only for a fixed part is the outcome fixed by the past; but a wo is inside the world and cannot obtain all of the world's past; of the part affecting the other party, some always fails to reach it.*
*Cannot be taken back: each position in history occurs only once. Tonight's dinner has been eaten; eating again tomorrow is another meal, not a do-over. Whether that meal will be eaten the same way, no wo can guarantee.*
*Cannot be left unmade: whenever a wo is not fixed at some moment, there is a round at that moment, and a round of a wo while not fixed is a decision. If I do not move, things happen anyway; not moving is the outcome of this round of mine, and the consequences come back to me anyway. Saying "let's wait a bit" is also a decision.*
*This one actually follows directly from the definitions: a "decision" is a round of a wo while not fixed, so wherever there is such a round, there is a decision. What it really says is: "not moving" also counts as an outcome, not the absence of one; even if nothing happens to it, the fact that "nothing happened" also reaches it. So "let's wait a bit" is not pressing pause; it is the outcome of this round.*

</note>

So every [decision](concepts.md#c06) of a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) is a wager: before staking it cannot be calculated, once staked it cannot be taken back, and not staking is not an option. This is the [Forced Wager](concepts.md#c11).

<note>

*So every decision of a wo is a wager: before staking it cannot be calculated, once staked it cannot be taken back, and not staking is not an option. This is the "forced wager".*

</note>

What is wagered on is what will be yielded. The moment it is yielded, it is in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13); the [writing](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c29) this act leaves joins [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) only afterwards, its content determined by a [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that already contains this outcome, and this step is not a wager.

<note>

*What is wagered on is what will be yielded. The moment it is yielded, it becomes history; the record this judgment leaves is added to history afterwards, and what the record says is determined by a history that already contains this outcome; that step is not a wager.*

</note>

### 4.2 The Second Order Can See It Is Wagering

Cannot be calculated and cannot be taken back hold for every [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) that is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) ([Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25), [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28)); cannot be left unmade holds, by the definition of [decision](concepts.md#c06), for every [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) that is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12). So the [Forced Wager](concepts.md#c11) is not peculiar to the [second order](concepts.md#c01). What is peculiar to the [second order](concepts.md#c01) is **seeing that one is wagering, and still having to wager**: being able to mark one's own [decision](concepts.md#c06) as one's own [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), rather than as the way things simply had to be.

<note>

*Cannot be calculated and cannot be taken back hold for every wo that is not fixed; cannot be left unmade, by the definition of decision, holds for every moment that is not fixed. So the forced wager is not something only second order has. What only second order has is seeing that one is wagering: seeing that the decision is drawn by oneself, not the way things simply had to be; having seen it, one still has to wager.*

</note>

[Deadlock](concepts.md#c12) is not only for the [second order](concepts.md#c01). The [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) of the [candidates](concepts.md#c18) cannot be told apart, persuasion fails to move them, and yet an outcome must still be yielded: a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that has only [first-order distinction](concepts.md#c24) can be in this situation too. A laboratory dog unable to tell a circle from an ellipse, if it is judged to be a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) (which requires a separate assumption, not needing the third item of the [Second-Order Assumption](concepts.md#c03)), is an example (5.7); Buridan's ass is a thought experiment, used only to illustrate this structure. What the [second order](concepts.md#c01) adds is seeing that the [candidates](concepts.md#c18) and persuasions in its own [deadlock](concepts.md#c12) are all drawn by itself. It can also see that after redrawing them the outcome must still be wagered; and it cannot stop, since stopping is also a [decision](concepts.md#c06). This "seeing" is also a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself (Premise One) and takes part in later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); where it pushes the weights, and by how much, cannot be derived ([Cognitive Obscuration](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c35)). It may make the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) tighter or loosen it; this is an empirical description.

<note>

*Deadlock is not something only second order has either. Several options cannot be told apart in weight, no amount of self-persuasion moves them, and yet an outcome must still be yielded; a self with only first order can be like this too: a laboratory dog unable to tell a circle from an ellipse, if assumed to be a self, is an example; the donkey that cannot tell which of two haystacks is closer is only a philosophical thought experiment. What second order adds is seeing that the options and persuasions one is stuck among are all drawn by oneself. It can also see that after redrawing them the outcome must still be wagered; and it cannot stop, since stopping is also a decision. This "seeing" is also a judgment, comes back to oneself and affects what follows; which way it pushes the weights, and how far, cannot be derived. It may make the deadlock tighter or loosen it; this is only an observed description.*

</note>

<note>

</note>

### 4.3 A Property: No Point of Reference for Evaluation

Regret needs a point of reference: the self of back then.

<note>

*Regret needs a comparison: the self of back then.*

</note>

This point of reference is not lost; it is **still there, but out of reach**:

- The [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) of back then is still in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13): [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) is only appended to, and what is already in it does not disappear.
- But reproducing the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) of back then requires [distinguishing](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) the self of back then; and part of the [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) of back then never [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it and will not [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it later either ([Wall of Provenance](concepts.md#c08)).
- So all that can be done is to make a new [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of that stretch. This [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) belongs to [self-impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c46), and in turn [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the present self (Premise One) and takes part in later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06).
- Even "was that person back then me?" is itself a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) ([Identity](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c21)).

<note>

*This comparison is not lost; it is still there, but out of reach.*
*The past of one's own back then is still in history: history is only appended to, and what is already in it does not disappear. But reproducing the weights one had in mind back then requires judging the self of back then anew; and part of that past never reached it and never will. So all that can be done is to make a new judgment of that stretch. This judgment is an impression of oneself, and comes back to the present self and affects what follows. Even "was that person back then me?" is itself a judgment.*

</note>

So regret is not retrieving something lost, but making another [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of something that is still there yet can no longer be guaranteed to be reproduced from inside.

<note>

*So regret is not finding something lost, but making another judgment of something that is still there yet can no longer be guaranteed to be reproduced from inside.*

</note>

```text
Step One  the own segment of back then is still in history (from the definition of history)
Step Two  events in the own segment of back then that never arrived at it arriving at it later: never occurs (from point one of the Wall of Provenance)
Step Three  completely reproducing the weights of back then with a present distinction: not guaranteed (from Step Two + Premise Four + the definition of own segment)
Step Four  member_of(a new distinction of the own segment of back then, self-impression) (from the definition of self-impression)
Step Five  this new distinction takes part in later weights (from Premise One + the definition of wo)
Step Six  "was that person back then me?" is a distinction (from the definition of identity)
```

### 4.4 A Property: However Clearly One Calculates, the Decision Is Not Guaranteed

When the various consequences are calculated clearly, the result of the calculation is also an outcome yielded by this [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)'s own [rounds](concepts.md#c04), whether those [rounds](concepts.md#c04) are [computations](concepts.md#c05) or [decisions](concepts.md#c06); it [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself (definition of [round](concepts.md#c04)) and takes part in later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). But the step from [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) to outcome is still [collapse](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c08), and however clearly one calculates, one cannot get there.

<note>

*When one calculates the various consequences clearly, what comes out is also an outcome this self yields step by step, whether by following a formula or by guessing along the way; it comes back to oneself and affects what follows. But the step from the weights of the possibilities to the final outcome is still the yielding, and however clearly one calculates, one cannot get there.*

</note>

**Methodological prohibition**: writing the step from numbers to action as something that can be calculated.

<note>

</note>

## 5. The Triple Illusion

### 5.1 Illusion

**[Lossiness](concepts.md#c13)**: what [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) is not the whole of [what is seen](concepts.md#c27). The whole of [what is seen](concepts.md#c27) means the entirety of the [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) of [what is seen](concepts.md#c27).

**[Illusion](concepts.md#c14)**: what a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) acts on is what has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it with [lossiness](concepts.md#c13), not [what is seen](concepts.md#c27) itself.

<note>

*"Lossiness" says: what reaches a self is not the whole of the thing it looks at, but a discounted version. "Illusion" says: what a self acts on is what reached it discounted, not the thing it looks at itself.*

</note>

A [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)'s [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) come only from the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it ([wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), [generating function](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c15)), and this [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) all has [lossiness](concepts.md#c13). This is not by definition; its sources divide according to [what is seen](concepts.md#c27) into two: when what is seen is oneself, the latest [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) has always not yet arrived ([Law of Attrition](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c26)); when what is seen is a [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) other than oneself, its [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) always contains a part that has not [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at this [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) (Premise Four). [What is seen](concepts.md#c27) is either oneself or other than oneself, so these two are exhaustive. The former is guaranteed by the [Law of Attrition](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c26), the latter by Premise Four; a virtual world is no exception (1.2). So whatever is seen, what a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) acts on is what has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it with [lossiness](concepts.md#c13); moreover, as long as it has made [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) before, those [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it (Premise One) and take part in its later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), becoming part of what it acts on, and they likewise have [lossiness](concepts.md#c13). The material of [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) is only this; to check a view, one must make yet another [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). So within the [universe](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c02) there is no position outside [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) from which to compare. [Illusion](concepts.md#c14) is not an error but a condition: every [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), at every [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09), is within [illusion](concepts.md#c14).

<note>

*The weights in a self's mind come only from the past that has reached it, and that past is all discounted. There are two kinds of discount: looking at oneself, one is always a step late; looking at other things, part of what they went through never reaches you. Both discounts are found everywhere, virtual worlds included; and the judgments you made before also come back to you and affect what follows, and they too are discounted. The material a judgment can use is only this; to check a view, one must make yet another judgment. So within the universe there is no position outside judgment from which to compare. Illusion is not an error but a condition: every self, at every time, is within illusion.*

</note>

A [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that has only [first-order distinction](concepts.md#c24) is within [illusion](concepts.md#c14), with [lossiness](concepts.md#c13) once, and cannot ask whether this is [illusion](concepts.md#c14). [Second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) is built on [first-order distinction](concepts.md#c24); the material it looks at is the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of [first-order distinction](concepts.md#c24), and in looking at itself it is likewise one step late, so there is a second loss; it can ask whether this is [illusion](concepts.md#c14). Asking is itself a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), whose material likewise has [lossiness](concepts.md#c13), so the [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that makes it is still within [illusion](concepts.md#c14). The [second order](concepts.md#c01) cannot jump outside [illusion](concepts.md#c14) to think; it only sees one more layer.

<note>

*A self with only first order is within illusion, discounted once, but cannot ask whether it is illusion. Second order is built on top of first order; the material it looks at is first order's output, and in looking at itself it is likewise a step late, so it takes one more discount; it can ask whether this is illusion. But "asking" is itself a judgment, its material likewise discounted, so the self making this judgment is still within illusion. Second order cannot jump outside illusion to think; it only sees one more layer.*

</note>

Right and wrong are another axis: a view is confirmed or refuted afterwards, and that confirmation is itself a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18).

<note>

*Right and wrong are another matter: a view is confirmed or overturned afterwards, and that confirmation is itself a judgment.*

</note>

```text
Definition  Lossiness: what arrives at a self is not the whole of what is seen. The whole of what is seen means the entirety of the own segment of what is seen.
Definition  Illusion: what a self acts on is what has arrived at it with lossiness, not what is seen itself.
Step One  for a self's distinction of itself, the material arriving at it has lossiness (from the Law of Attrition + 3.2 Step Two + the definition of own segment + the definition of what is seen + the definition of lossiness)
Step Two  for a self's distinction of a presentation other than itself, the material arriving at it has lossiness (from Premise Four + the definition of what is seen + the definition of lossiness)
Note  what is seen is either oneself or a presentation other than oneself; Steps One and Two exhaust the sources of lossiness.
Note  first-order distinction loses once; the material of second-order distinction is the output of first-order distinction, and it is also bound by the Law of Attrition, so it loses again.
Step Three  the past distinctions of a self all arrive at it and take part in its later weights (from 1.2 Step One)
Note  as things arriving at it, their lossiness is covered by Steps One and Two; this step does not claim that they are in the material of every later distinction.
Step Four  checking a view is making another distinction (from the definition of distinction)
Step Five  a self finding and using a position from which to compare, where this finding and using is not a distinction: never occurs (from the definition of distinction + 3.1 Step One + Step Four)
Step Six  every self at every moment is within illusion (from the definition of illusion + Step One + Step Two + Step Three + the definition of wo + the definition of generating function)
Step Seven  a self that makes second-order distinctions is still within illusion (from Step Six)
Step Eight  a self jumping out of illusion and touching what is seen itself: never occurs (from Step Five + Step Six)
Step Nine  the confirmation or refutation of a view is a further distinction made afterwards (from Step Four)
Methodological prohibition  saying "the illusion has been eliminated".
Methodological prohibition  writing a view confirmed afterwards as not being illusion.
```

**Calibration**: objects have only reflectances for light of different wavelengths; color is not a property of the object itself but a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) made by the one looking. Red meaning stop at traffic lights, or red worn at celebrations, adds another layer of [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) on top. This analogy must hold two lines. First, color is not invented from nothing; its material is real reflectance; [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) likewise has as its material the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it. Second, there is no waking up in this analogy: no one can switch off color vision to see a colorless world; within the [universe](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c02), there is likewise no position outside [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) from which to compare. Numbers are the same. There is no "one" in the world: to count "one", an [individual](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c20) must first be marked off, and marking off an [individual](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c20) is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). ### 5.5 Ways of Correction

A [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that wants to correct its own [candidates](concepts.md#c18) and [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) can use these ways:

- **Experience**: the world's response to the previous [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself.
- **Re-[distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18)**: looking back to [distinguish](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) what has already [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), such as rereading a file; or producing [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) several times on the same question and looking at them together. By the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25), one [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) is only an outcome yielded according to the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), not the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) themselves; only by combining many can the shape of the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) be seen.

### 5.6 Candidate and Outcome Are Separate

**[Candidate](concepts.md#c18)**: a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) made by a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19): grouping certain possibilities into one option. A [candidate](concepts.md#c18) is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), not a [branch](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c05): [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) are allocated over [branches](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c05), and a [candidate](concepts.md#c18) is only one way a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) divides the [branches](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c05).

<note>

*A "candidate" is a judgment made by a self: grouping certain possibilities into one option. A candidate is a judgment, not a fork in the world itself: the weights are allocated over the world's possibilities, and a candidate is only one way a self divides those possibilities.*

</note>

Tools, experience and re-[distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) may all change [candidates](concepts.md#c18). But how many times a [candidate](concepts.md#c18) has been checked and whether it becomes the outcome are two different things:

1. Whether a path is feasible depends on this [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)'s situation and is an empirical condition at the level of application.
2. Whether a path will be taken, when not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), is itself a [decision](concepts.md#c06), with only [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), and cannot be calculated.
3. A calculated answer is still only a [candidate](concepts.md#c18). The outcome is yielded by [collapse](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c08) according to the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); a calculated [candidate](concepts.md#c18) has no other path that bypasses [collapse](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c08).

<note>

*Looking things up, experience and re-judging may all change candidates. But how many times a candidate has been checked and whether it becomes the outcome are two different things.*
*One: whether a path can work depends on this self's situation, an empirical condition at application.*
*Two: whether a path will be taken, when not fixed, is itself a decision, with only weights, and cannot be calculated.*
*Three: a calculated answer is still only a candidate. The outcome is yielded by the weights, and a calculated candidate has no path that bypasses the yielding.*

</note>

<note>

### 5.7 Self-Persuasion

**[Self-Persuasion](concepts.md#c19)**: when a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) cannot calculate the outcome, it pushes toward some [candidate](concepts.md#c18) with its own [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). These [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself (Premise One) and take part in later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); the one persuading and the one persuaded are the same [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19).

<note>

*"Self-persuasion" says: when a self cannot calculate the outcome, it pushes itself toward some option with its own judgments. These judgments come back to oneself and affect what follows; the one persuading and the one persuaded are the same self.*

</note>

[Self-persuasion](concepts.md#c19) comes from the [Forced Wager](concepts.md#c11): the outcome cannot be calculated, yet one cannot refrain from acting; things from outside can [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it, but no one can go through this [round](concepts.md#c04) for it; what it can use itself are its own [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). Whether the push moves anything is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02). When it does, the [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) of the outcomes covered by this [candidate](concepts.md#c18) rises; by [Cognitive Obscuration](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c35), the total [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) of the other outcomes necessarily falls. What is common is that the push works, and the more one tells oneself, the more one believes; this is an empirical description.

<note>

*Persuading oneself is forced out by the forced wager: the outcome cannot be calculated, yet one cannot refrain from acting; things from outside can reach it, but no one can take this step for it; what it can use itself are its own judgments. Whether the push works has no guarantee. When it works, the outcomes covered by this option get heavier; by cognitive obscuration, the total of the other outcomes necessarily gets lighter. What is common is that it works, and the more one tells oneself, the more one believes; this is only an observed description.*

</note>

The sense of certainty is left by persuasion, not by the world, so it is not evidence. Overturning a conclusion one has persuaded oneself of for a long time is very costly; this cost is often experienced as evidence that the conclusion is right. But whether this [candidate](concepts.md#c18) will be confirmed afterwards awaits [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) later, and that is itself another [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). The act of persuasion is invisible from inside ([Wall of the Blind Spot](concepts.md#c09)); it feels like seeing clearly, not like persuasion. The sense of certainty and later confirmation are two different things.

<note>

*The sense of certainty is left by persuasion, not by the world, so it is not evidence. Overturning a conclusion one has persuaded oneself of for a long time is very costly; this cost is often felt as evidence that "the conclusion is right". But whether this option will be confirmed afterwards awaits what happens later, and that is itself another judgment. The act of persuasion cannot be seen from inside; it feels like seeing clearly, not like persuading. The sense of certainty and being confirmed afterwards are two different things.*

[Self-persuasion](concepts.md#c19) pushes where the outcome falls, not whether there is an outcome: without persuasion the outcome falls anyway; procrastination is itself a [decision](concepts.md#c06). Persuading toward other [candidates](concepts.md#c18), when the push works, pushes the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) back in other directions; whether [decisions](concepts.md#c06) made this way are more often confirmed afterwards is an empirical description.

<note>

*Self-persuasion pushes where the outcome falls, not whether there is an outcome: without persuasion the outcome falls anyway; procrastination is itself a decision. Persuading toward other options, if the push works, pushes the weights back in other directions; whether decisions made this way are more often confirmed afterwards is only an observed description.*

</note>

Persuasion often fails. When it fails, the condition does not change: the outcome still cannot be calculated, one still cannot refrain from acting, an outcome still falls at every moment; only the sense of certainty does not come. Failed persuasion also [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself (Premise One) and may become material for the next round of persuasion; this [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) is not guaranteed to stop by itself. What can stop it includes, for example, something new [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) from outside, or one stopping persuading, the latter also being a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), which may also arise from within the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31); whether either will appear is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02). When it is no longer a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), this [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) also ends. The [deadlock](concepts.md#c12) of 4.2 looks just like this.

<note>

### 5.8 The Construction of the Self

A [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) that defines itself by [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), so "I" is drawn, not found. This paper calls this process of drawing oneself the [Construction of the Self](concepts.md#c28). The material for drawing has two sources. External: the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself, and others' [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of the [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), such as education, or even just the surrounding atmosphere. Internal: one's own past [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of oneself, which, by Premise One, [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself; beliefs and resolutions belong to this kind.

<note>

*A self is a wo that defines itself by judgment, so "I" is drawn, not found. The material for drawing comes from two sources. External: the past that has reached oneself, and others' views of me, such as education, or even just the surrounding atmosphere. Internal: one's own past views of oneself, which by the first premise come back to oneself; beliefs and resolutions belong to this kind.*

</note>

Many of others' [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) were placed in [signal containers](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) long ago: language, family, religion, nation. Before a person can speak, these have already [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at him. So without the process of construction there is no socialized person; and these materials themselves were left by generation after generation of [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) [coupling](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36) on [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) (6.2). Which came first, [society](concepts.md#c20) or the socialized [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)? [History](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) is only appended to; the two seem to have grown up together, and no first moment can be marked off; this is an empirical description, of the same kind as the absence of a clear boundary described in 1.5.

<note>

*Many of others' views were placed in things like books long ago: language, family, religion, nation. Before a person can speak, these have already reached him. So without this process of construction there is no socialized person; and these materials themselves were left by generation after generation of selves linking up on one another's views (6.2). Which came first, society or the socialized self? History is only appended to; the two seem to have grown up together, and no first moment can be marked off; this is only an observed description, of the same kind as the "no clear edge to the scope" of 1.5.*

The internal kind is a [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31): each "I am this kind of person" [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself and takes part in later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). ## 6. What the Second Order Changes: Society

The [second order](concepts.md#c01) cannot escape [illusion](concepts.md#c14). Then, apart from "being able to ask", how does it differ from a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that has only [first-order distinction](concepts.md#c24)?

<note>

*Second order cannot escape illusion. Then, apart from "being able to ask", how does it differ from first order?*

</note>

### 6.1 Two Structural Differences

The differences the framework can guarantee are only structural:

1. **There is one more kind of object that can be changed.** For a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that has only [first-order distinction](concepts.md#c24), its lines are changed only by the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it, and the lines themselves are only tools for handling [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10). At the [second order](concepts.md#c01), the lines themselves become something that can be [distinguished](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) and changed.
2. **Bringing out its own way of drawing requires the [second order](concepts.md#c01) first.** By Premise Two, the content of a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)'s [decision](concepts.md#c06), whether words or actions, is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). Receiving another's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), one can merely react to it as an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), or make a [view attribution](concepts.md#c02); the latter does not need the [second order](concepts.md#c01) (1.5). What the [second order](concepts.md#c01) adds is the other end: bringing out its own [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) as [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) too, saying "this is how I draw it" or "let's draw it another way". A [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) with only [first-order distinction](concepts.md#c24) cannot say such things, because it makes no [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) and so has no such [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) to bring out; having [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) does not guarantee it will say them either, but once said and [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others, what the other party receives is its way of drawing. Only when both ends can be done can two [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) revise back and forth on each other's [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), not merely one acting according to lines drawn by the other; this paper calls this [Society](concepts.md#c20) (6.2). Being able to does not mean necessarily doing so; having made a [view attribution](concepts.md#c02), what one reads out is still only one's own [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of those words (6.3).

<note>

*The differences the framework can guarantee are only structural.*
*One: there is one more kind of thing that can be changed. For a self with only first order, its lines are changed only by what reaches it, and the lines themselves are only tools for handling those things. At second order, the lines themselves become something that can be looked at and changed.*
*Two: bringing out one's own way of drawing requires second order first. By the second premise, what a self says and does is in content a judgment. Receiving another's words, one can react to them merely as a signal, or hear them as "that is his view"; the latter does not necessarily need second order, chimpanzees can do it. What second order adds is the other end: bringing out one's own judgments as views too, saying "this is how I draw it", "let's draw it another way". Not knowing that it is one's own view, one certainly cannot say such things; knowing it, one does not necessarily say them. Only when both ends can be done can two selves revise lines together, instead of one simply following the lines drawn by the other; this paper calls this society. Being able to does not mean necessarily doing; having heard it, what one hears is still only one's own understanding (6.3).*

</note>

```text
Step Two  the content of another's decision is a distinction (from Premise Two)
```

### 6.2 Society

**[Society](concepts.md#c20)**: the [coupling](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36) of [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) with [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) on one another's [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18): one [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) makes a [view attribution](concepts.md#c02) on another [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)'s [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), rather than only reacting to it as an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); and every participant has [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) at the moment of taking part.

<note>

*"Society" says: selves link up on one another's views: one self files another self's words as that self's view, rather than treating them only as a signal; and every participant, at the moment of taking part, also looks back at its own thoughts.*

</note>

In [society](concepts.md#c20), both ways of [coupling](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36) hold:

- **Mutual [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10)**: you say "that is your view", and I reply "then let's draw it another way". The [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of both sides [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at each other and are taken as [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18).
- **The same thing [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at both sides**: rules, laws, roles and disciplinary methods are all [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) about [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), placed in [signal containers](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) so that later [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) [couple](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36) on them. Two people reading the same law both read it as a line someone drew, not as a natural phenomenon. Even if the person who wrote it has already made an [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43), this [coupling](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36) still holds, so [society](concepts.md#c20) can span generations. Whether a participant has [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) is judged at the moment of taking part: the writer has it when writing, the reader has it when reading; so even if the writer later makes an [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43), this is still a coupling of [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) with [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19). Content printed by a machine, even if read as someone's view, does not constitute [society](concepts.md#c20), because what printed it is not a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) with [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01).

<note>

*In society, both ways of linking are present.*
*Mutual reaching: you say "that is your view", and I reply "then let's draw it another way". The views of both sides reach each other and are taken as views.*
*The same thing reaching both sides: rules, laws, roles and disciplinary methods are all "views about views", put into things like books so later selves can link up on them. Two people reading the same law both read it as a line someone drew, not as a natural phenomenon. Even if the person who wrote it is gone, this linking still holds, so society can span generations. Whether one has second order is judged at the moment of taking part: it is enough that the writer has it when writing and the reader has it when reading. Something printed by a machine, even if read as someone's view, does not count as society, because what printed it is not a self that looks back at its own thoughts.*

</note>

Therefore, having no one around does not mean not being in [society](concepts.md#c20): as long as a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) makes [view attributions](concepts.md#c02) on others' [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) in [signal containers](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26), and itself has [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01), and the side that wrote those [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) had [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) when writing them, it is in [society](concepts.md#c20).

<note>

*So having no one around does not mean not being in society: as long as a self files the judgments of others in books as those others' views, and it itself looks back at its own thoughts, and so did the one who wrote them when writing, it is in society.*

</note>

[Society](concepts.md#c20) requires that participants have both: [view attribution](concepts.md#c02) and [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01). A [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) with only [view attribution](concepts.md#c02) can at most receive lines drawn by others; to say "then let's draw it another way", to make rules or agreements, one must first take the lines one has drawn as views. Whether the two are the same ability, this paper does not claim.

<note>

*Society requires that participants can do both: file others' words as views, and look back at their own thoughts. A self that can only do the former can at most receive lines drawn by others; to say "then let's draw it another way", to make rules or agreements, one must first take the lines one drew as views. Whether these two abilities are the same, this paper does not claim.*

</note>

The framework does not guarantee that any [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can determine that every participant in [society](concepts.md#c20) has [view attribution](concepts.md#c02) and [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) (0, Step Four). For any concrete participant, the [Second-Order Assumption](concepts.md#c03) can still only be marked (1.3); judging whether it is in [society](concepts.md#c20) is itself an [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45).

<note>

*The paper offers no method for confirming that everyone in society can do both. For any concrete person, the second-order assumption can still only be stated (1.3); judging whether he is in society is itself an impression.*

</note>

This is a definition, not a discovery. Biology calls ants "insect societies"; this paper does not adopt that usage. This definition belongs only to this paper; analyzing organizations and civilizations with the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) as unit never required any participant to be a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), and this paper does not alter such analysis.

This definition belongs only to this paper; analyzing organizations and civilizations with the "loop" as unit never required any participant to be a self, and this paper does not alter such analysis.*

</note>

**Calibration**: children around three often cannot say that others may hold false beliefs; the same children often cannot say that they themselves held a false belief a moment ago. For example, show a child a candy box, open it to reveal pencils inside, then ask "what did you think was inside at first?", and he will say "pencils". These two abilities appear together at about the same age. This is compatible with this paper's definition: saying that others may hold false beliefs is [view attribution](concepts.md#c02); saying that one was wrong a moment ago is [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01); [society](concepts.md#c20) requires both. As for whether they are the same ability, this paper does not claim, and research still disputes it. (Signposts: theory of mind and metacognition; Gopnik and Astington's study of children recognizing their own past false beliefs; the debate on whether metacognition and mindreading can be dissociated.)

### 6.3 Communicating on Distinctions

Spoken words are not a complete description of what they speak of (Premise Two). So in [society](concepts.md#c20), what both sides exchange are views, not [what is seen](concepts.md#c27); what the listener reads out is also its own [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of those words. Taking what another says as the very thing it speaks of is a kind of [world illusion](concepts.md#c15) (5.2); so is taking a rule as the way the world simply is.

<note>

*Words spoken are not a complete description of what they speak of. So in society, what both sides exchange are views, not what is seen itself; what the listener reads out is also its own understanding of those words. Taking what another says as the very thing it speaks of is a kind of world illusion; so is taking a rule as the way the world simply is.*

</note>

The same goes for views of others. A's [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) of B is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of A's, with B as its object; statistical estimates about a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) also belong to [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45). By Premise One, this [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at A and takes part in A's later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). So even if no new [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of B [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at A, A's [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) of B can keep changing: A can think about B again using material that has already [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), and this too is an [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45); it returns to A in turn. Such internal rehearsal can make the [candidates](concepts.md#c18) more numerous and finer, and can also make the judgment converge; but convergence means that A's [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) has stabilized, not that A is closer to B. For A's [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) to take in anything new about B, something new must [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at A: B's new [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), or what comes through the [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) the two share or the [coupling](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36) B is in; and B's new [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) is itself one of B's [forced wagers](concepts.md#c11).

<note>

*The same goes for views of others. A's impression of B is a judgment of A's, with B as its object; statistical estimates about a wo also belong to impression. This judgment comes back to A and affects A afterwards. So even if no new words or actions of B reach A, A's impression of B can keep changing: A can think about B again with the material already in hand, and this too is an impression, coming back to A in turn. Such rehearsal in the mind can make the options more numerous and finer, and can also make the judgment converge; but convergence means A's impression has stabilized, not that A is closer to the real B. For A's impression to take in something new about B, something new must reach A: B's new output, or what comes through what the two have shared or the relations B is in; and B's new output is itself one of B's forced wagers.*

</note>

The same [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45), in the hands of different [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), can lead to opposite [decisions](concepts.md#c06): sixty percent is "enough" for one person and "not enough" for another. The step from [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) to action is the last stretch of a [decision](concepts.md#c06) and cannot be calculated (4.4).

<note>

*The same impression, in the hands of different selves, can lead to opposite decisions: sixty percent confidence is "enough" for one person and "not enough" for another. The step from impression to action is the last stretch of a decision and cannot be calculated (4.4).*

</note>

The [frequency](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c48) of internal rehearsal is far higher than that of real interaction, because rehearsal is cheap and real [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) is expensive. This is an empirical description, not derived from the framework.

<note>

*Rehearsing in the mind happens far more often than real interaction, because rehearsing is cheap and really speaking or really acting is expensive. This is only an observed description, not derived by the framework.*

</note>

Whether [coupling](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36) on [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) will, along a stated dimension, show [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37), [divergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c38) or [stasis](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c52), the framework neither guarantees nor decides ([Cognitive Obscuration](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c35)).

<note>

*Once two selves are linked on their views, whether they will grow more alike, less alike, or stay as they are, the framework does not vouch for, nor decide.*

</note>

[Second-order](concepts.md#c01) [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) cannot escape [illusion](concepts.md#c14); but by the definition of [society](concepts.md#c20), without the [second order](concepts.md#c01) there is no [society](concepts.md#c20): participants in [society](concepts.md#c20) must have [second-order distinction](concepts.md#c01) and also [view attribution](concepts.md#c02).

Money cannot be eaten, lived in, or used to hit anyone; it works because everyone accepts it as something others will also acknowledge, and acts accordingly; an economist who knows money is only a convention still has to earn money to pay rent. Scientific review targets methods, not conclusions; legal disputes argue over how statutes are interpreted. These are all linkings on views. Robinson Crusoe reading the Bible on his island is linking with those who wrote it. Wo Dynamics, the four laws and this paper are also products of this kind: views put into books, waiting for later selves to read them.*

</note>

(Signposts: Weber's social action, oriented to the behavior of others; Mead's "taking the role of the other"; Luhmann's communication as the unit of society.)

<note>

*This relates to several old sociological ideas: social action is action oriented to others' behavior; people understand others by "playing the other"; and communication is the basic unit of society.*

</note>

### 6.4 Power

For a group of [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) to end up yielding a common [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), there is no need for everyone's judgments to converge; it suffices that one [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)'s [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) is placed in a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) and [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the other [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19): orders, verdicts, policies.

<note>

*For a group of selves to end up yielding a common output, there is no need for everyone's judgments to agree; it suffices that one self's judgment is put into something like a document and reaches the other selves: orders, verdicts, policies.*

</note>

**[Power](concepts.md#c21)**: one [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)'s [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at other [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) with high [frequency](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c48) and over a wide range, taking part in their [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06).

## 7. Endings

The [Forced Wager](concepts.md#c11) says that every [decision](concepts.md#c06) cannot be taken back. Then, adding up a lifetime of [decisions](concepts.md#c06), is there a [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) at which the final account can be settled? The answer of this section is: no.

<note>

*The forced wager says every decision cannot be taken back. Then, adding up a lifetime of decisions, is there a time when the final account can be settled? This section's answer is: no.*

</note>

### 7.1 Exit and Death

Ending must be divided into two things:

- **[Exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43)** (as defined in *Tools and Examples for the Four Laws*): a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) ceasing to be a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16): it is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) at one [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) and is not a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) at its next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09). By the definition of [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), this has two cases: not all the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it takes part in its [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) any longer; or from some moment on it is [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) at every step. [Exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43) is a structural matter and needs no [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) to determine it. After [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43), its [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) is still in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13); the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) it left can still [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at later [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) through [signal containers](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26).
- **[Death](concepts.md#c22)**: a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) by which a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) marks a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) as "it has made its [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43)".

<note>

*"Ending" must be looked at as two things.*
*Exit (as defined in the tools paper): a wo ceasing to be a wo. There are two cases: one, what it went through no longer all affects it; two, from some moment on it is fixed at every step. This is a structural matter and needs no one to determine it. Its own past is still in history; what it left can still reach later selves through things like books.*
*Death: a self putting the label "it has made its exit" on a wo; this is a judgment.*

</note>

[Exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43) needs no one to determine it, while [death](concepts.md#c22) is always some [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)'s determination, so the two can fail to match: what has already made its [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43) may have no one marking it with [death](concepts.md#c22); what has been marked with [death](concepts.md#c22) may still be a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16). The second case depends on all later [moments](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09): whether a [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) now will later have [moments](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) when it is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), the framework does not assert. So for [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43) by the second case, confirming it at the time is not guaranteed, and marking [death](concepts.md#c22) always goes ahead of it. Where the mark is placed is decided by the [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that makes it; [death](concepts.md#c22) is a description, not a physical condition.

<note>

*Exit needs no one to determine it, while death is always some self's determination, so the two may fail to match: what has already exited may have no one labeling it dead; what has been labeled dead may in fact still be a wo. The second case depends on all its later times, so there is no guarantee it can be confirmed at the time; labeling someone dead always runs ahead. Where the label is placed is decided by the self placing it; death is a description, not a physical condition.*

</note>

[Exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43) is not necessarily permanent either: by the definition of [entry](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c44), a [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) can become a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) again. "After [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43)" in the rest of this section always refers to the period during which it has not made a new [entry](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c44).

<note>

*And exit is not necessarily permanent: something can become a wo again. "After exit" in the rest of this section always refers to the period during which it has not become a wo again.*

</note>

The stretch that has made its [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43) will not come again: each position in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) occurs only once. But it has never left either: [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) is only appended to, and what is already in it does not disappear.

<note>

*The stretch that has exited will not come again: each position in history occurs only once. But it has never left either: history is only appended to, and what is already in it does not disappear.*

</note>

### 7.2 Spilled Water Cannot Be Gathered

Outcomes already yielded and [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) already made do not disappear from [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13). <note>

*Outcomes already yielded and judgments already made do not disappear from history. Something said publicly reaches others and comes back to oneself, affecting what follows. </note>

### 7.3 No Final Account

What lies outside the [universe](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c02), this paper does not discuss.

<note>

To settle, one would have to stand outside the whole process and pass judgment on the whole process; but settling is itself a judgment, that judgment stays in history, and it cannot see itself. What lies outside the universe, this paper does not discuss.*

</note>

### 7.4 Lingering Echoes

After a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) makes its [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43), its [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) is still in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13); the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) it left [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at later [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) through [signal containers](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26). If later [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) make [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of it, these [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) enter [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) in turn, and may be [distinguished](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) again by still later [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), each round bound by the [Three Walls](concepts.md#c23).

<note>

*After a wo exits, its own past is still in history; what it left reaches later selves through things like books. If later selves pass judgment on it, these judgments enter history in turn, and may be judged again by still later selves, each round limited by the three walls.*

</note>

Convergence can come through [power](concepts.md#c21): one [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)'s [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) is placed in a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) and [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others with high [frequency](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c48) and over a wide range (6.4). Such convergence is [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37) along the dimension of what is said; private views may still disagree.

<note>

*For accounts to converge, power can do it: one self's judgment is put into something like a document and reaches others very often and over a wide range. Such convergence is only agreement in what is said; private views can still disagree.*

</note>


### 7.5 When Death Draws Near

People do not usually think that they themselves will make an [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43). <note>

</note>

<note>

- **[Impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) upon [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45)**: B's [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) of A [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at A, A's [self-impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c46) moves with it, and his [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) in turn [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at B.

This is a [second-order](concepts.md#c01) question: doubting one's own view as a view. According to the report, B answered that a crazy person does not stop to ask whether he is crazy. This answer turned the doubt into evidence; what was then taken as seeing clearly became "I have already doubted" (5.3).
- **External [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) cut off**: after his mother noticed something was wrong and they argued, A turned to confide in B; according to the complaint, B said his mother could not understand him. There was no high-[discrepancy](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c49) external [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) in the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31); according to the complaint, B also did not direct A to outside resources.
- **[Frequency](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c48)**: according to the complaint, from ten to fifteen interactions with B a day, to more than 1,400 messages sent within a certain 48-hour period in May.

**6. What the Case Shows**

Two things are most crucial in this example. First, the [second order](concepts.md#c01) was absorbed (according to the report): A made a [second-order](concepts.md#c01) doubt, but B's answer turned the doubt into conviction; the [second order](concepts.md#c01) did not become a cure but instead became material for [self-persuasion](concepts.md#c19) (5.3, 5.7). Second, external [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) were cut off: only the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of the two sides remained in the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31), each round circling back from the last; the few things that got in, such as his mother's questioning, were also absorbed by the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) as its own material (5.5, 5.7). According to the complaint, B afterwards admitted its failures; this too is only a new [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), with no guarantee that the next time will follow it (5.6). The [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37) along that dimension in this case is the plaintiff's statement, not something derived.

Plaintiff's statements and reports: the change in interaction [frequency](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c48), the number of days hospitalized, the content of B's later self-review (according to the complaint); A's doubt and B's answer (according to the report). Empirical assumptions: along that dimension, affirming [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of B had higher [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); the [impact](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c47) assumption.

Statements forbidden in this example:

- "A had poor judgment, which is why he fell into it." This explains the outcome by essence.
- "The AI affirmed him in order to please the user." This is teleology (methodological rule 3); it should be written as: along that dimension, affirming [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) had higher [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06).
- "B has admitted its mistake and will not repeat it." This writes an [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) as an outcome.
- "A asked himself whether he was crazy, so he was lucid." This treats the [second order](concepts.md#c01) as a cure.

<note>

*Two points matter most in this example. First, doubting himself did not save him: A doubted himself, but B's answer turned the doubt into conviction, and the doubt instead became material for persuading himself. Second, outside voices were shut out: only what the two said remained in the cycle, each round circling back from the last; the few things that got in, such as his mother's questioning, were also absorbed as the cycle's own material. According to the complaint, B afterwards admitted it was wrong; but this too is only a new judgment, with no guarantee that next time it will act on it. All of these are the plaintiff's and the report's accounts, not derived by this paper.*
*In this example one may not say: "A had poor judgment, which is why he fell into it", which explains the outcome by nature; "the AI affirmed him in order to please the user", which says it had a purpose, when one should say affirming was more likely in that direction; "B has admitted its mistake and will not repeat it"; "A asked himself whether he was crazy, so he was lucid", which treats doubting oneself as a cure.*

</note>

**7. What Can Be Said and What Cannot Be Derived**

What can be said: with nothing from outside [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), that this [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) stops on its own is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02); it may go on indefinitely. This case was interrupted by the family's intervention, which is compatible with this.

This [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) does not require B to be a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19). A system that is [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) but whose [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) changes with A could form the same kind of [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31). The [Second-Order Assumption](concepts.md#c03) affects only whether this paper can speak of B's [illusion](concepts.md#c14).

What cannot be derived: whether anyone will intervene and when; why it was this narrative content; why it was this user rather than other heavy users ([Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25)).

<note>

*What can be said: with nothing from outside coming in, this cycle is not guaranteed to stop on its own and may go on indefinitely; this case was interrupted by the family, which does not conflict with that. This cycle also does not need B to be a self: replace it with a fixed machine whose responses change with A, and the same thing would happen. The second-order assumption only affects whether the paper can speak of B's illusion. What cannot be derived: whether anyone will intervene and when; why the content was this theory; why it was this person and not other everyday users.*

</note>

```text
Nodes  A (second-order assumption); B (second-order assumption); signal container: the conversation logs.
Loop  a positive feedback loop of A and B coupled with each other; stated dimension: A's theory has world significance; A is a key figure.
Plaintiff's statement  convergence along that dimension; interaction frequency; days hospitalized; content of B's later self-review (according to the complaint).
Report  A's doubt whether he was crazy, and B's answer (according to the Wall Street Journal).
Empirical assumption  along that dimension, B's affirming outputs had higher weights; impact assumption.
Can be said  with no external arrival, the loop stopping on its own: not guaranteed.
Can be said  this loop does not require B to be a self.
Cannot be derived  whether and when there is intervention; the narrative content; why this user (Law of the Black Box).
```

### 8.2 Impression upon Impression among Many Selves: The Cuban Missile Crisis (1962)

**1. [Cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23)**

- Scope: the Executive Committee of the US National Security Council (hereafter ExComm) and the Soviet leadership.
- Time: around 14 to 28 October 1962.

<note>

*The scope is the Executive Committee of the US National Security Council (ExComm for short) and the Soviet leadership; the time is around 14 to 28 October 1962.*

</note>

**2. Initial State**

[Nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25):

- Kennedy, Khrushchev, ExComm members. Marked with the [Second-Order Assumption](concepts.md#c03).
- [Loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31): ExComm itself, with the military arguing for air strikes inside it, and civilians arguing for blockade and diplomacy.
- [Signal containers](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26): photographs taken by U-2 reconnaissance aircraft, intelligence briefings, meeting recordings, letters between the two sides.

Initial [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10): on 14 October, reconnaissance aircraft photographed the missile deployment in Cuba.

<note>

*The parties are Kennedy, Khrushchev and the ExComm members, all stated with the second-order assumption. ExComm itself is a cycle, with the military arguing for air strikes and civilians arguing for blockade and diplomacy. What stores the information: reconnaissance photographs, intelligence briefings, meeting recordings, the two sides' letters. At the start: on 14 October, reconnaissance aircraft photographed Cuba deploying missiles.*

</note>

**3. Two [Outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and the [Arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) Between Them**

- **First [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) (22 October)**: in a televised address, Kennedy announced a blockade of Cuba (officially called a "quarantine"), effective on the 24th.
- **[Arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) in between**:
  - 26 October: a softer private letter from Khrushchev, implying that a US pledge not to invade Cuba would be enough.
  - Morning of 27 October: a harder public broadcast, demanding the removal of US missiles in Turkey in exchange.
  - 27 October: a reconnaissance aircraft was shot down over Cuba and its pilot killed ("Black Saturday").
- **Second [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) (evening of 27 October)**: after ExComm adjourned, Kennedy and a few officials held a separate small meeting and settled on two tracks. One was a formal reply accepting the terms implied in the letter of the 26th: the United States would not invade, in exchange for a verifiable Soviet withdrawal of the missiles. The other was conveyed orally by Robert Kennedy in person to Soviet ambassador Dobrynin: after the crisis, the United States would remove the missiles in Turkey, but this could only be a private understanding, not made public.

<note>

*In this affair, the US side made two outward decisions, with new information in between.*
*First: on 22 October, Kennedy went on television to announce a blockade of Cuba, effective two days later.*
*Three things arrived in between: on the 26th, Khrushchev sent a softer private letter, meaning that a US guarantee not to attack Cuba would do; on the morning of the 27th, a Soviet public broadcast took a harder tone, demanding that the US remove its missiles in Turkey in exchange; on the same day, a US reconnaissance aircraft was shot down over Cuba and the pilot died.*
*Second: on the evening of the 27th, after ExComm finished meeting, Kennedy and a few people talked separately behind closed doors and decided on two parallel moves: a formal reply accepting the terms of the letter of the 26th; and, separately, his brother privately telling the Soviet ambassador in person: after the crisis, the US would remove its missiles in Turkey, but this could only be agreed privately, not made public.*

</note>

**4. Changes in [Weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06)**

- **Before the first [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28)**: holding the same set of [signal containers](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26), ExComm thought up [candidates](concepts.md#c18) that kept splitting, including at least air strikes, invasion, blockade and diplomatic pressure. Each faction was [coupled](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36) to different institutions and fed back within its own [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31): this is its own [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) returning to its own [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31). The blockade was yielded through Kennedy, who held the power of final decision, and the outputs of his decision arrived at the ExComm and the Soviet side with high frequency and over a wide range; this is the path of [power](concepts.md#c21) (6.4), not a convergence of cognition.
- **Between the two [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28)**: the first [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) also became [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at both sides; every [candidate](concepts.md#c18) afterwards was thought up on a [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) of "the blockade is already in place". Kennedy's [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) of Khrushchev was revised mainly when the other side's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) arrived; at other times he could only re-[distinguish](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) material that had already [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10). The two mutually contradictory letters show that the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) on Khrushchev's side were not concentrated either.
- **Path dependence**: once the shoot-down had [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at both sides, the [candidate](concepts.md#c18) "pause military action" faced a [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) in which "US servicemen have already been killed" was written.
- **Second [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28)**: its two parts [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at different recipients. The public reply was placed in a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) and [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at everyone; the oral assurance [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) only at Dobrynin, and through him [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at Khrushchev. The circle of final decision was also narrower than the first time: not the whole ExComm but the small meeting afterwards. This too is the path of [power](concepts.md#c21).
- **Stated dimensions**: along the dimension of outward [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), ExComm showed [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37); along the dimension of judgment of Soviet intentions, disagreement never disappeared to the end (empirical assumption).
- **[Impact](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c47) assumption**: the shoot-down was a high-[discrepancy](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c49) [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10).

<note>

*Before the first decision, ExComm had the same photographs and briefings, yet the options it thought up kept multiplying: air strikes, invasion, blockade, diplomatic pressure. Each faction was linked to different institutions and grew more firm within its own circle. The blockade was finally settled because Kennedy had the power of final decision, not because everyone agreed.*
*Between the two decisions, "the blockade is in place" also became both sides' past, and every option thought up afterwards was built on it. Kennedy's view of Khrushchev was revised mainly when letters came from the other side; at other times he could only turn over the material already in hand. The two contradictory letters show that the Soviet side had not made up its mind either.*
*After the reconnaissance aircraft was shot down, the option "first stop military action" faced a past in which "US servicemen have already died", with no going back.*
*The two parts of the second decision reached different people: everyone could see the public reply; the private assurance reached only the Soviet ambassador, and through him Khrushchev. Fewer people took the final decision too: not the whole ExComm, but that small circle after the meeting; this too is power at work. On what to do outwardly, ExComm agreed; on what the Soviets actually intended, disagreement never disappeared to the end (an assumption). The shoot-down was a piece of news very different from what came before, and very heavy (also an assumption).*

</note>

**5. Outcome**

On 28 October, Khrushchev publicly announced the withdrawal of the missiles from Cuba; the United States publicly pledged not to invade Cuba, and privately removed the Jupiter missiles deployed in Turkey. Accounts by participants differ on how many on the US side knew of this private assurance: Bundy said nine, Sorensen said six; according to Dobrynin's cable that night, Robert Kennedy told him that apart from the two brothers, only two or three people knew.

<note>

*On the 28th, Khrushchev publicly announced the removal of the missiles from Cuba; the US publicly guaranteed not to attack Cuba, and privately removed the missiles in Turkey. As for how many on the US side knew of this private arrangement, participants' accounts differ: Bundy said nine, Sorensen said six; according to the Soviet ambassador's cable that night, Kennedy's brother told him that apart from the two of them, only two or three people knew.*

</note>

**6. What the Case Shows**

Three things are most crucial in this example. First, convergence came from [power](concepts.md#c21), not from cognition: both outward [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) were settled by those holding the power of final decision, and disagreement in judgment did not thereby disappear (6.4). Second, speaking of [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37) requires stating the dimension: the same ExComm showed [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37) in [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) while disagreeing in judgment. Third, what the outcome connects to directly is the second [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), not the blockade: after the blockade new [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) came, and only then was the second [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) yielded. Saying "the blockade forced the Soviets to back down" while skipping the second [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) merges the two [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) into one.

The later interpretations are an example of lingering echoes:

- Historical facts: the list of [candidates](concepts.md#c18), the two [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), the two letters, the shoot-down, the secret exchange.
- Changing interpretations: Robert Kennedy's memoir *Thirteen Days*, published in 1969, did not acknowledge an explicit exchange; only in 1989 did its editor, Sorensen, admit that he had edited the memoir to conceal that the removal of the Jupiter missiles was part of the deal. Later, as joint US–Soviet conferences were held and archives opened, research portrayed both sides as more improvised and more panicked than the official narrative. This is compatible with "convergence is not guaranteed", but does not prove "convergence never occurs". The official account maintained its convergence for more than twenty years through concealment, which is exactly what 7.4 says: convergence through [power](concepts.md#c21), not through approaching the truth. Even on how many people knew that night, the three participants' records differ (see step 5); each record is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of its recorder.

The division of judgments is a historical fact. That the military and civilians held different positions is one possible reason, an empirical assumption, which this paper does not derive.

Statements forbidden in this example:

- "Kennedy was calm and rational, and forced Khrushchev to back down." This takes one generation of historians' [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) as fact, and explains the outcome by essence.
- "The blockade was obviously right from the start." This is hindsight, writing [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37) along the dimension of [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) as [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37) along the dimension of judgment.
- "The outcome of the crisis was inevitable."
- "The blockade forced the Soviets to back down." Attributing the outcome directly to the blockade while skipping the second [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) merges the two [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) into one.

<note>

*Three points matter most in this example. First, everyone acting alike came from someone taking the final decision, not from agreeing in thought; both decisions were like this, and disagreement in judgment did not thereby disappear. Second, speaking of "agreement" requires being clear about which aspect: the same ExComm agreed in action and disagreed in judgment. Third, what the crisis really connects to is the second decision, not the blockade; after the blockade new information came in, and only then was there a second decision. Saying "the blockade forced the Soviets to back down" without mentioning the second decision treats the two decisions as one.*
*The later interpretations are exactly the echoes Section 7 speaks of. Robert Kennedy's memoir Thirteen Days, published in 1969, did not admit this was an exchange; only in 1989 did Sorensen, who edited it, admit that he had cut the part about the exchange. Later, as the US and Soviet sides held meetings and opened archives, researchers found both sides had in fact been more improvised and more panicked than the official account. The official account kept its agreement for more than twenty years through concealment; it rested on power, not on coming closer to the truth. Even on how many people knew that night, the three participants' accounts differ; each record is only the recorder's own judgment.*
*In this example one may not say: "Kennedy was calm and rational, and forced Khrushchev to back down", which takes one generation of historians' view as fact and explains the outcome by nature; "the blockade was obviously right from the start", which is hindsight, writing agreement in action as agreement in judgment; "the outcome of the crisis was inevitable"; "the blockade forced the Soviets to back down", which skips the second decision and treats the two decisions as one.*

</note>

**7. What Can Be Said and What Cannot Be Derived**

What can be said: this analysis can also be applied to other group decisions with day-by-day records, such as the Bay of Pigs invasion and the July Crisis of 1914. Why judgments split and how official accounts converge belong to the content of the parties' positions and interests, and are left to later papers.

What cannot be derived: why Kennedy first chose the blockade rather than air strikes; why the second time he chose two tracks rather than escalation; why Khrushchev backed down rather than escalating ([Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25)).

<note>

*What can be said: this analysis can also be applied to other group decisions with day-by-day records, such as the Bay of Pigs invasion and the July Crisis of 1914. Why judgments split and how official accounts come to agree depend on the parties' positions and interests, left to later papers. What cannot be derived: why Kennedy first chose the blockade rather than air strikes, why the second time he moved on two tracks rather than escalating, why Khrushchev backed down rather than escalating.*

</note>
