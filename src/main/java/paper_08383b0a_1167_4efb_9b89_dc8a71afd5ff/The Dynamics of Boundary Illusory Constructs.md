# The Dynamics of Boundary Illusory Constructs

---

## 0. Position and Method

### 0.1 Position

This paper builds on *The Dynamics of Biological Humans' Output Patterns* (hereafter the Pattern paper). It asks: on an [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) that can already stand, when biological humans draw [we](concepts.md#c04) and [plural you](concepts.md#c05) about people, and make [belonging distinctions](concepts.md#c07) classing things as "mine", "yours", "ours", or "yours (plural)", how can these [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), through [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), make an [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) appear in the economic mode in the record; and how can the [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) that the researcher draws from spoken and written classifications, if registered, diverge from the inside outside difference in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06)? The Pattern paper treats the [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) structure among the outputs of many biological humans and does not register distinctions; this paper uses [illusory constructs](concepts.md#c01) such as [second-order capacity](concepts.md#c02), the [social person](concepts.md#c03), and we (0.5) to describe how these distinctions, through output, take part in the later operation and mutual shaping of existing economic modes.

The conclusions derived in the Pattern paper still hold here; this paper only adds and changes nothing.

<note>

*This paper follows the Pattern paper: without "we", an economic mode still runs; with constructs such as "we" and "ours" and the drawings that use them, people draw further margins of people and things within the same mode. These margins belong to each person's own distinctions and can affect others only through output; once what is done to those inside differs from what is done to those outside, the mode may show an inside and an outside. Boundaries spoken or written may also differ from boundaries actually acted out. Everything the Pattern paper said is used here unchanged.*

</note>

### 0.2 Method

This paper is deductive, in the same format as the Pattern paper: each conclusion states the definitions, premises, or earlier conclusions it is derived from; the cases serve only as calibration and bear no argument.

### 0.3 Scope of the Record

This paper records as follows: only the physical changes of [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) are registered, not the content of [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). A [social person](concepts.md#c03)'s distinctions are in its [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13), not in this paper's [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06).

**Notation convention**: saying that some [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) is in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) always means one the researcher has registered, and saying that some difference is in the record always means one the researcher draws from registered outputs under a stated [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23); saying that a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) becomes part of [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) means that the [writing](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c29) it leaves becomes part of history; saying that [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) does not fix the result always means when not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), under the [application assumption](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c50); words or names within quotation marks are written as given, and the "we", "you", and similar words in them are not linked; this paper links these concepts only when it uses them.

<note>

*The record registers only what people did. Who counts whom as one of their own is not written into the record. When this paper says "the record has", it means what the researcher wrote down; when it says "weight does not fix the result", it means when not locked in; words or names within quotation marks are written as given, without links.*

</note>

### 0.4 Scope of Claims

This paper states three kinds of things: relations that hold of structural necessity; which things cannot be derived from which others; and things that can be registered in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06), such as the gap between a [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) and an [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09). Conclusions of the first two kinds that depend on an [illusory construct](concepts.md#c01) hold under that construct (0.5 Note 6). This paper does not predict who will draw whom into a [we](concepts.md#c04) or who will class which thing as "ours", nor in which direction [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) or [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) will change. What this paper calls "ours" is not a rule of ownership, use, disposal, or exclusion of others.

### 0.5 Illusory Construct

**[Illusory construct](concepts.md#c01)**: a construct whose object is not given by the physical world, yet which is used to describe or organize [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), such as [we](concepts.md#c04), husband, and [second-order capacity](concepts.md#c02).

**Notation**: "physical world" is used in this paper as an ordinary term and is not set up as a concept.

Notes:
1. **Two ways of appearing**: first, a researcher uses it to describe complex first-order [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), sorting many outputs into a few [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); second, it appears in outputs as a name, an [avowal](concepts.md#c06), or a classification written into a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26); if this output [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others, it takes part in their [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), and through output may make later [records](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) take a corresponding shape (Conclusions 5.1, 5.5, 7.11). When the second way has a classification as its content, then if this output is registered in the record, it is material from which the researcher draws a [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) (3.9).
2. **Not a distinction**: a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) is an act of a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) that leaves a [writing](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c29) in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) (definition of distinction); an [illusory construct](concepts.md#c01) is the content of a distinction, or a construct used in a researcher's description or in [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), whose object is not given by the physical world. A distinction may use an illusory construct or may not. Drawing a [we](concepts.md#c04) is a distinction; the we it uses is an illusory construct (3.3).
3. **Not an illusion**: an [illusion](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c14) is a situation of a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), describing only what it acts on (definition of illusion); when an [illusory construct](concepts.md#c01) appears in [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), it may take part in later [records](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06).
4. **No claim of existence**: this paper neither claims nor denies that the object of an [illusory construct](concepts.md#c01) exists in any [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04). [Second-order capacity](concepts.md#c02) itself is an illusory construct: this paper uses it as shorthand for complex first-order [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); this is compatible with the [configuration assumption](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c01) (Section 2).
5. **Reason for adoption**: without [illusory constructs](concepts.md#c01), explaining one [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) would require tracing the whole [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at that biological human (definition of [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16)); with them, many outputs can be described as a few [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). Such sorting is a distinction of the researcher; what it does to each output that arrives at the researcher is [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02); either may be wrong.
6. **Traceable**: conclusions of this paper that depend on an [illusory construct](concepts.md#c01) hold under that construct; the registration of [claimed boundaries](concepts.md#c10), [inside outside differences](concepts.md#c09), and the gap between them depends only on the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06).
7. **The researcher is also inside**: when a researcher's description appears in its [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), the [illusory constructs](concepts.md#c01) in it may likewise [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others and take part in their [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) (Note 1, second way).

<note>

*Words like "we", "husband", and "second-order capacity" refer to things not given by the physical world; this paper calls them illusory constructs. They have two uses: researchers use them to state a mass of behavior simply; people also say them aloud and write them into documents; if passed to others, others' later actions may change accordingly. So illusory constructs are not only descriptions; they may also change later records. This paper does not say these things really exist, nor that they do not. Even "second-order capacity" is such a word: it may be only shorthand for complex first-order behavior. The same goes for the papers researchers write: once concepts are passed on, they may change others.*

</note>

Calibration: the sentence "In his heart he does not treat her as his wife" sorts years of [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) into one [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); this is the first way of appearing. "You are my wife", said at a wedding, and the marriage certificate are the second. (Signpost: Vaihinger's "philosophy of as if"; Hacking's looping effect of human kinds. Where they meet: concepts known not to correspond to real things are used anyway; a classification that reaches the classified changes them. Where they part: this paper sets up no division between fiction and reality, and asks only whether a construct's object is given by the physical world and whether the construct appears in output.)

### 0.6 Notation

"Appear", "involve", "speed", "one side", and "select"; "same" in uses such as the same thing or the same side; the verb "decide"; and the question "will it happen" are used in this paper as ordinary terms and do not refer to the upstream concepts of the same name. "Conflict" in this paper means that two [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) are incompatible; it is an ordinary term and does not refer to the [conflict](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c10) of the acquaintance paper. Names of others' theories in signposts, such as Coser's external conflict and Luhmann's system and environment, are kept as in the original and do not refer to upstream concepts.

"Cannot be derived" in this paper means: from the sources and [records](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) listed in this paper, a unique result cannot be derived; it does not claim that it cannot be derived under any condition or from any material. "Not uniquely derived" is used in the same sense.

<note>

*These words each have a special meaning upstream; this paper uses only their everyday meaning, as in "appear", "the same thing", or "who decides". "Conflict" here means two drawings that do not match, not the acquaintance paper's conflict, in which two people fight ever harder. And when this paper says "cannot be derived", it means a definite answer cannot be derived from the material this paper has, not that no one could ever derive it.*

</note>

**Premise**: this paper assumes that a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) and an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of it [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at that wo itself before its next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) and take part in its later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). <note>

*This paper assumes: each time a person draws something or does something, it comes back to that person and affects them afterwards. Upstream said this only of "looking at oneself"; this paper takes it as a general assumption.*

</note>

## 2. Scope

The object of this paper is the [social person](concepts.md#c03) (3.2). Biological humans who are not social persons are outside its scope; the conclusions of the Pattern paper still hold for them.

When this paper applies its conclusions to specific biological humans, it marks them with the [configuration assumption](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c01). By the definition of the configuration assumption, confirming from outside whether an object has [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) at some moment is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02); so whenever this paper's conclusions are applied to specific people, they hold under this assumption. The configuration assumption is made for a stated stage, so a biological human, taken as one by [identity](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c21), can be a [social person](concepts.md#c03) in one period and not in another.

<note>

*This paper speaks of one kind of person only: the social person. Those who are not are still treated by the Pattern paper; infants and the like are not presumed to be within scope. To say a specific person is a social person can only be assumed, because who has this capacity cannot be guaranteed to be confirmed from outside. And the same person may count during one period and not during another.*

</note>

Calibration: law divides legal capacity by marks such as age and guardianship orders, replacing a capacity that cannot be guaranteed to be confirmed directly with marks that can be [recorded](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06); this paper's marking of specific people with the [configuration assumption](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c01) is the same practice. This paper does not presume infants or people with severe dementia to be within scope.

<note>

*The law cannot confirm one by one who is of sound mind either, so it uses visible marks such as age instead. This paper writing "assume he is a social person" for a specific person is the same practice.*

</note>

This paper takes biological humans as its object without claiming the mechanisms in it are found only in biological humans. By [recutting](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c22), other clusters of [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), such as an ant nest, may have corresponding [inside outside differences](concepts.md#c09) and [claimed boundaries](concepts.md#c10); this paper does not treat them.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Second-Order Capacity

**[Second-order capacity](concepts.md#c02)**: having both [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) and [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02).

Notes:
1. This is a joint name of this paper and does not claim the two are one capacity. By the definitions of [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) and [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), neither definition takes the other as a condition.
2. [Second-order capacity](concepts.md#c02) is a capacity, not a condition of every [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18): having it means being able to make these two kinds of distinction, not having made them every time (definition of [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01): to say a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) has second-order distinction means it can make such a distinction about any of its own distinctions).
3. **[Second-order capacity](concepts.md#c02) is an [illusory construct](concepts.md#c01)** (0.5): this paper uses it as shorthand for complex first-order [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and does not claim any biological human really has it; by the [configuration assumption](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c01), confirming it from outside is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02) (Section 2).

<note>

*Second-order capacity is the name for two abilities together: looking back at one's own views, and hearing others' words as others' views. The two do not necessarily come together; this paper just gives one name to the case where both are present. Having the capacity does not mean using it every time. The name itself is also an illusory construct: it may be only shorthand for complex first-order behavior.*

</note>

### 3.2 Social Person

**[Social person](concepts.md#c03)**: a biological human with [second-order capacity](concepts.md#c02).

Note: a [social person](concepts.md#c03) is an [illusory construct](concepts.md#c01) (0.5). Calling a biological human a social person is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) the researcher makes with that biological human as object, whose material may include [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), under the [configuration assumption](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c01) (Section 2); this paper does not on that basis judge any biological human not to be a social person. This paper's conclusions about social persons all hold under this construct. Social persons and participants of a [second-order network](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c04) share the condition of having both [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) and view attribution; the difference is that a second-order network is the set formed by these [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) and the [couplings](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36) among them, while a social person is a single biological human. The object of this paper is the social person, not the second-order network. A social person has second-order distinction; by the definition of second-order distinction, what has second-order distinction is a self; so a social person is a self, and also a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) (definitions of second-order distinction and self).

<note>

*A social person is a biological human with both abilities. Saying who is a social person is the researcher's assumption, not something seen; nor can it be used to say who is not.*

*The acquaintance paper's "second-order network" is the web such people form together; this paper speaks of people one by one.*

</note>

### 3.3 We

**[We](concepts.md#c04)**: an [illusory construct](concepts.md#c01) used by a [social person](concepts.md#c03): one with which the social person, along some dimension, treats itself and other [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) it counts as a "[self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)" as one unit on the same side, to organize its [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28).

Notes:
1. **Defined by object, not content**: the object of [we](concepts.md#c04) includes oneself; this is a feature of the object. By method, the content of [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) is not formalized. For the construct whose object excludes oneself ([plural you](concepts.md#c05)), see 3.4.
2. **One-sided**: [we](concepts.md#c04) belongs to the [social person](concepts.md#c03) who uses it and does not require those drawn in to draw it the same way.
3. **The dimension is drawn by the one who draws**: the same pair of people can be [we](concepts.md#c04) along this dimension and not along another. This dimension is the [social person](concepts.md#c03)'s own [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), not the researcher's dimension.
4. **Counting as a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can be a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) about a class**: "people of this kind are all selves" does not require a [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) for each member of the class; what it requires is the capacity for view attribution.
5. **Counting as a self does not guarantee the other is a self**: counting as a self is treating the other as another [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) that makes [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); by the definition of [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), view attribution can be wrong: the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) taken as a view does not necessarily come from a self.
6. **Choice of scope**: this paper speaks only of the [we](concepts.md#c04) of [social persons](concepts.md#c03). A [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) with only [first-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c24) is recorded in this paper as having no we; by the definition of self, who is a self is defined by itself, and the boundary moves with its self-definition. This is this paper's notation, not a derivation.
7. **Degree and persistence**: each drawing is either drawn or not; to say a [social person](concepts.md#c03) "has" some [we](concepts.md#c04), and how strong this we is, is a matter of degree belonging to its [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), and is not formalized. By the premise of Section 1, each drawing [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself and takes part in later weights; a social person is a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) (3.2), and when not in [lock-in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) it cannot derive in advance the result of its own next [decision](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c06); so whether its [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) about some we recurs is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02) ([wall of the undetermined](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c10)).
8. **Not drawn into [we](concepts.md#c04) is not drawn as [plural you](concepts.md#c05)**: the [margin](concepts.md#c08) drawn by the [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) drawing we does not automatically produce a distinction drawing plural you; further drawing the [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) on the other side of the margin as plural you is still a distinction of the [social person](concepts.md#c03)'s own (3.4).
9. **Drawing and using**: drawing a [we](concepts.md#c04) is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) that uses this construct (0.5 Note 2); a we has effect only through the outputs that use it. A we never used in [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) is, in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06), no different from one never drawn; whether even the [social person](concepts.md#c03) who drew it will later make it the object of a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) again is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02), and it may long have forgotten it ([wall of the blind spot](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c09): no [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can fully distinguish itself at any time).

<note>

*"We" is drawn by a social person: in some respect, placing oneself and others one counts as "selves" on the same side. That I feel we are a we does not mean you feel so too. The same pair can be a we at home and not at the company. To say "we Taiwanese" one need not know every Taiwanese; it is enough to draw "people of this kind are all selves". One can also draw wrongly, for example treating a dog as family. Animals with only first-order distinction have only "self", whose boundary grows and shrinks, but no plural. How invested someone is in a "we" is not a score, and it keeps changing.*

*A "we" drawn only in the mind and never brought out for use is unknown to others, and one may forget it oneself; it cannot be told apart from not drawing at all.*

</note>

Calibration: "our family", "our company", "our department", "our country" are the [we](concepts.md#c04) of the same person along different dimensions. Someone draws the company as we, while colleagues treat him only as a contractor; someone draws the family dog into "our family".

Calibration: a boss privately plans to fire an employee but never says so and never acts on it; in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) this is no different from not planning it, and he himself may later forget it.

### 3.4 Plural You

**[Plural you](concepts.md#c05)**: an [illusory construct](concepts.md#c01) used by a [social person](concepts.md#c03): one with which the social person, along some dimension, treats other [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) it counts as a "[self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)", excluding itself, as one unit, to organize its [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28).

Notes:
1. **Defined by object**: the object of [plural you](concepts.md#c05) excludes oneself; this is a feature of the object; it differs from [we](concepts.md#c04) in object, not in content.
2. **One-sided**: [plural you](concepts.md#c05) belongs to the [social person](concepts.md#c03) who uses it; it does not require those drawn in to draw it the same way, nor that they draw any [we](concepts.md#c04).
3. **Choice of scope**: as in 3.3 Note 6, this paper records a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) with only [first-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c24) as having no [plural you](concepts.md#c05).
4. 3.3 Notes 3, 4, 5, 7, and 9 apply as well: the dimension is drawn by the one who draws; counting as a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can be a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) about a class; counting as a self does not guarantee that the other is a self; it has degrees and changes; it has effect only through the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that use it.

<note>

*"Plural you" is also drawn by oneself: in some respect, grouping others into a group that does not include oneself. When someone says "you mainlanders", whether those addressed accept it, the speaker does not know, and it does not affect this drawing.*

</note>

Calibration: when someone says "you mainlanders", those drawn in may see themselves as Taiwanese businesspeople, as Shanghainese, or as nothing at all.

**Notation**: "they" is used in this paper as an ordinary term, the name for several [plural yous](concepts.md#c05) taken together; taking them together is itself a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) (Conclusion 4.10).

### 3.5 Avowal

**[Avowal](concepts.md#c06)**: an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of a [social person](concepts.md#c03) whose object is the receiver's [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of the avower: along some dimension, it asks the receiver to place the avower in some class.

Notes:
1. **Defined by object**: the object is limited to others' [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of the one making the [avowal](concepts.md#c06); for [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) whose object is other distinctions, see Conclusion 5.6.
2. **An avowal is not a reading of a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18)**: whether and how deeply the avower draws it is not in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06); a lie is also an [avowal](concepts.md#c06).
3. **An [avowal](concepts.md#c06) is only a request**: whether the receiver draws accordingly, see Conclusion 6.5.
4. **Classing as an [avowal](concepts.md#c06) is the researcher's [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02)**: classing an output that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the researcher as an avowal is the researcher's view attribution; the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) contains only the physical changes of this [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) (Conclusion 4.6).
5. **Excludes classificatory statements about things**: "this is ours", if its object is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) about some thing, does not become an [avowal](concepts.md#c06) in this definition just because the word "we" appears in it; it is an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) about a thing; see 3.6 and Conclusion 5.7.

### 3.6 Belonging Distinction of Things

**[Belonging distinction of things](concepts.md#c07)**: a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) made by a [social person](concepts.md#c03): along some dimension, taking the [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) of a thing that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it as material, it classes the thing as "mine", "yours", "ours", or "yours (plural)". These four are [illusory constructs](concepts.md#c01) this distinction uses, having effect only through the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that use them, not rights the thing itself carries.

Notes:
1. **The [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) belongs to the one who draws**: "ours" and "yours (plural)" are used in distinctions about things, not about people; which [we](concepts.md#c04) or [plural you](concepts.md#c05) they refer to belongs to the content of the distinction and is not formalized in this paper (3.3 Note 1). Even if others hear the same name, they are not guaranteed to make the same distinction.
2. **People and things differ**: drawing B into [we](concepts.md#c04) is making a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) that draws we, with oneself and a [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) counted as a self as object; classing some thing (hereafter this thing) as ours is a distinction with this thing as object; the two delimit their objects by different definitions. This thing does not, by being classed as ours, become another "[self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)", nor does it thereby enter a shared subject.
3. **No fixed order required**: read literally, "ours" seems to presuppose a [we](concepts.md#c04); this presupposition is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) the researcher makes about two names (same type as Conclusion 4.10), not a distinction of a social person; a [social person](concepts.md#c03)'s distinctions are not bound by this order. This paper's definition does not require drawing we before drawing ours, nor drawing [plural you](concepts.md#c05) once there is a we, nor that all four classifications appear. A social person may also first make a distinction about a thing and then draw a we along the dimension of that thing (3.3 Note 3).
4. **No use or disposal implied**: classing as ours does not imply who may use it, who may not, how it is used or allocated, nor that anyone may demand that others draw accordingly. None of these is in this paper's definition.
5. **The dimension is drawn by the one who draws**: the same thing can receive different [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) from the same [social person](concepts.md#c03) along different dimensions; whether these distinctions agree is not required by the definition.
6. **No distinction in the record**: the researcher can [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) "this is ours"; which [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) the speaker actually made cannot be derived from the sentence, nor whether the listener agrees (Conclusion 4.6).
7. **Effect through use**: "mine", "yours", "ours", and "yours (plural)" are [illusory constructs](concepts.md#c01) used by the [belonging distinction of things](concepts.md#c07); they have effect only through the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that use them, such as saying "this is ours" and acting accordingly (3.3 Note 9).

<note>

*Classing a thing as "ours" is only one person's drawing about it. For example, A feeling that this thing is "ours" does not mean B thinks so, still less that A and B can certainly use it. When A says "this is ours", others can hear the sentence but cannot see A's drawing inside.*

*Literally, it seems there must be "we" before there is "ours"; that is the researcher's reasoning about two words, and the one who draws need not follow this order. One can first have a thing and then count "the people I think can use it" as we.*

*"Ours" is like money: it works only when brought out and used, by saying "this is ours" and acting accordingly.*

</note>

Calibration: of the same batch of goods, A classes it as "ours" and B as "yours"; both are in the same [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09), yet need not share the classification. This calibrates the possibility that [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) disagree; it is not a measurement of the parties' inner distinctions.

Calibration: a [social person](concepts.md#c03) first points at this thing, then draws "those he thinks can use this thing" as [we](concepts.md#c04); the dimension of this we is drawn by him through this thing. This is about whom he thinks can use it, not who really can, and it makes no [path](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07) infeasible for those not drawn in (Conclusion 7.7). Counting oneself among those who can use it draws a we; not counting oneself, as in "this classroom is for you (plural) to use", draws a [plural you](concepts.md#c05) (3.4).

### 3.7 Distinction Margin

**[Distinction margin](concepts.md#c08)**: a [social person](concepts.md#c03) makes a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) along some dimension, so that objects fall on different sides within its distinction. Drawing some [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) as [we](concepts.md#c04) and others as [plural you](concepts.md#c05), or classing things as ours or yours (plural), are examples of distinction margins.

Notes:
1. **It changes the person; only through output does it change the mode**: a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) changes the [social person](concepts.md#c03) who makes it; changes in the [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) come only through its [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) (Conclusion 5.1).
2. **Both sides need not have names**: drawing the [margin](concepts.md#c08) of [we](concepts.md#c04) does not require drawing all other objects as [plural you](concepts.md#c05); a classification along a dimension need not exhaust all objects either.
3. **It belongs only to the social person who makes it**: different [social persons](concepts.md#c03) can draw different [margins](concepts.md#c08) over the same people and things. To say an [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) shows "a [distinction margin](concepts.md#c08)" means that a social person is able to make a further [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) on material from that mode which has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it, not that the economic mode acquires a shared internal distinction.
4. This name only joins existing [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) under one name and sets up no causal mechanism by which distinctions directly change others or things. Its external effect goes through [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) (Section 5).

<note>

*Several nodes that used to run together can, in someone's eyes, be drawn onto different sides. This line is drawn by him, not drawn on the mode from the start; others need not draw it the same way.*

</note>

### 3.8 Inside Outside Difference

**[Inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09)**: a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) a researcher makes in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) of [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28): outputs of the same kind differ between members of some group and between those members and participants outside it.

Notes:
1. **It belongs to the researcher**: which [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) count as the same kind, which participants form a group, and along which dimension they are compared are drawn by the researcher under a stated [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23); under another cut the [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) can differ. The [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) contains only the physical changes of each output (Conclusion 4.6). As in 7.2 Note 3, the researcher's group need not match, in range or dimension, the [we](concepts.md#c04) a [social person](concepts.md#c03) draws.
2. **Not a distinction margin**: a [distinction margin](concepts.md#c08) is in the [social person](concepts.md#c03)'s [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13), not in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) (3.7); an [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) is drawn from [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) in the record and is not a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of the [social persons](concepts.md#c03) studied.
3. **It can fall on things**: [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of the same kind that differ when directed at some things rather than others also form an [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09).
4. **Not a rule or a right**: an [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) only describes differences in [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); it does not imply who is entitled or excluded, and it is not an institution (Conclusions 7.7, 11.2).

<note>

*An inside outside difference is what the researcher sees in the record: the same kind of thing is done one way to a group and another way to those outside it. For example, lending to relatives without interest and to outsiders with interest. This is a grouping the researcher sees, not necessarily the grouping in the parties' minds, and it does not mean anyone has a right to charge or not.*

</note>

Calibration: the same street vendor gives credit to regulars and asks strangers to pay cash. What the researcher [records](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) is that two kinds of [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) differ, not the vendor's inner sense of familiar and unfamiliar.

### 3.9 Claimed Boundary

**[Claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10)**: a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) a researcher makes in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06): from an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) whose content is a classification, it draws the boundary that output proposes. Such outputs include [avowals](concepts.md#c06), classificatory statements about people or things, and classifications written into a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26).

Notes:
1. **It belongs to the researcher**: which boundary an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) proposes is drawn by the researcher from that output and judged under a stated [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23); it is not a reading of the outputter's [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) (Conclusions 4.6, 6.2).
2. **In the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) together with the inside outside difference**: a [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) is drawn from an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) whose content is a classification; an [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) is drawn from how outputs of the same kind differ within and across a group (3.8). Both are drawn from [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) and are not [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of the [social persons](concepts.md#c03) studied.
3. **Classifications written into a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26)**, such as statutes, certificates, and regulations: the signal container is in [lock-in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) at every step from then on; for a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) to rewrite it is another [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that arrives at it (definitions of signal container, output, and [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10)).

<note>

*Classifications that someone says or writes, such as "you are my wife", a marriage certificate, or company rules, let the researcher see what kind of boundary they draw; this is called a claimed boundary. It is a boundary said or written, not the speaker's inner drawing, and not necessarily the boundary everyone actually acts out.*

</note>

## 4. Properties of Distinction

### 4.1 Drawn, Not Found

**Conclusion 4.1**: [we](concepts.md#c04) is drawn, not found: there is no we that exists ready-made, without [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), waiting to be discovered. This does not deny the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), and [economic modes](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) that exist before distinction; they are not in themselves a we drawn by some [social person](concepts.md#c03).

(Derived from the definitions of [we](concepts.md#c04) and of [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18): we is an [illusory construct](concepts.md#c01) drawn by a distinction; a distinction draws [what is seen](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c27); it does not discover an object that exists without distinction)

<note>

*"We" is drawn by oneself, not something already in the world waiting to be discovered.*

</note>

Calibration: "we Taiwanese" is not found by a census; a census can count how many hold a certain identity card, but not who draws whom as [we](concepts.md#c04).

### 4.2 Material

**Conclusion 4.2**: the material from which a [social person](concepts.md#c03) draws [we](concepts.md#c04) is only the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has already [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it, including others' [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and its own past [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). The material for its [belonging distinctions](concepts.md#c07) about things can likewise come only from this arrived history.

(Derived from the definition of [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) and the premise of Section 1: the material of a distinction is only the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it before it; its own past distinctions have all arrived at itself and are also in this history)

Note: the material is of two kinds, in the same shape as the [construction of the self](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c28). External: others' [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), such as admission, assignment, or being called "we". Own: the [we](concepts.md#c04) one has drawn before. How others draw [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) only through their outputs; what arrives is output, and whatever is read out of it is one's own [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) (definition of [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02)).

<note>

*We is not drawn out of nothing. There are only two kinds of material: what others have done or said that reached you, such as being admitted or assigned to a department; and what you have drawn before. How others draw in their minds, you cannot touch; you touch only their output.*

</note>

Calibration: only after being admitted does one start saying "our company".

### 4.3 Material Does Not Determine Distinction

**Conclusion 4.3**: the same material in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) does not imply the same [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18): whether a [social person](concepts.md#c03) draws some [we](concepts.md#c04), and how, and whether it classes some thing as mine, yours, ours, or yours (plural), and how, cannot be derived from the material in the record alone (0.6).

(Derived from the definition of [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), the scope of the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) (0.3), the [wall of provenance](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c08), the [law of the black box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25), and the [application assumption](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c50): the [writing](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c29) a distinction leaves has its content determined by the whole [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) up to that time; within it, one's own past distinctions are not in the record (0.3), and one's own origins cannot be fully obtained (wall of provenance); so even if two social persons have the same material in the record, their whole histories may differ. Moreover, under the application assumption, this paper assumes that a [social person](concepts.md#c03) is not in [lock-in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) at the moment of making a distinction; by the law of the black box, even if the whole history were known, its next moment could not be uniquely derived beforehand. This paper does not claim that each such distinction is unpredictable in principle; the latter part holds under the application assumption)

<note>

*The same material does not mean the same drawing. Given the same admission letter, some say "our company" and some say "I work here". Things are the same: from the record at hand alone, who will treat which thing as "ours" cannot be calculated with certainty beforehand. This assumes a person is not locked in at the moment of deciding.*

</note>

Calibration: given the same admission letter, some say "our company" and some say "I work here". Judas was one of the twelve disciples, inside that [we](concepts.md#c04); however much material he shared with them, how he would draw next could not be derived. (Signpost: the theological dispute over foreknowledge and freedom. Where they part: by the [law of the black box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25), no [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) within the [universe](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c02) can uniquely derive beforehand a [decision](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c06) that is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12); foreknowledge outside the universe is not discussed in this paper.)

### 4.4 On Oneself

### 4.7 History Only Accumulates

**Conclusion 4.7**: the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) of a [social person](concepts.md#c03) concerning some [we](concepts.md#c04) only grows by appending: something that did not [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it then will not arrive at it later; what arrives later is only the trace that thing left; and what has arrived is not erased. So a newcomer and an old-timer, even if they now draw the same we, may have different histories taking part in this [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18): something that had arrived at the old-timer before the newcomer joined, if it did not arrive at the newcomer then, will not arrive at it later. The length of history does not imply how strong this we is, nor differences in [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28).

(Derived from Conclusions 4.2 and 4.4, the definition of [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) in Wo Dynamics, the [law of non-restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28), and the [wall of provenance](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c08) of *Three Illusions*: history only grows by appending; what did not [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) then will not arrive later; each drawing takes part in later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). The length of history and how strong this [we](concepts.md#c04) is are two independent dimensions: length is how much has arrived, strength belongs to weight (3.3 Note 7), and the definitions do not let the former determine the latter)

Notes:
1. <note>

*A newcomer on the first day saying "our company" and a senior of thirty years saying the same sentence may have different histories behind them: what the senior went through in early years, the newcomer was not there for, and cannot make up later. </note>

### 4.8 We and Tacit Coordination Do Not Imply Each Other

**Conclusion 4.8**: if a [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) drawn in is a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), drawing [we](concepts.md#c04) may lead to more [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) back and forth between the one who draws and it: the [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) drawing we takes part in the drawer's [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) and may increase its outputs toward the one drawn in; if these outputs arrive at the one drawn in, and that one's outputs also arrive at the drawer, they become material for [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05); repeated dedicated history may show [tacit coordination](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c06). But tacit coordination does not take we as a condition, and we does not take tacit coordination as a condition.

(Derived from the definition of [we](concepts.md#c04) (3.3), Conclusions 4.2 and 4.4, the definitions of [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29), and [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), and the definitions of [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05) and [tacit coordination](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c06) in the acquaintance paper: the distinction drawing we arrives at oneself and takes part in [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), and when the weight differs, [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) may differ; output becomes material for dedicated history only when it arrives at the one drawn in through an edge; the criterion of tacit coordination is the dedicated history and behavior pattern between two [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), requiring neither friendliness nor any [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); the definition of we does not require dedicated history)

Notes:
1. Repeated compatible continuation among many people, within a stated dimension and time window and conditioned on [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), is [resonance](../paper_2ccbf179_58cf_4851_bd72_5fa886e4b2c3/concepts.md#c09) (definition of resonance in the stranger paper); the researcher's name for it is [culture](../paper_2ccbf179_58cf_4851_bd72_5fa886e4b2c3/concepts.md#c13).

### 4.9 Many We at Once

**Conclusion 4.9**: a [social person](concepts.md#c03) can draw different [we](concepts.md#c04) along different dimensions; these can include one another or overlap; the [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) drawing them can also conflict. The definition does not require them to agree.

(Derived from the definitions of [we](concepts.md#c04) and of [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18): we is drawn along some dimension, and the dimension is drawn by the one who draws (3.3 Note 3); a conflict is two distinctions being incompatible)

<note>

*The same person can have "our family", "our company", "our department", "our country", each in its own respect. They can nest, overlap, or fight one another.*

</note>

Calibration: (Signpost: Simmel's intersection of social circles; Merton's role set. Where they meet: a person belongs to several circles at once and stands at their intersection. Where they part: they treat circles as shared; this paper has no shared circles, only each person's [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) along each dimension.)

### 4.10 Inclusion Is Also a Distinction

**Conclusion 4.10**: the relation of inclusion between two [we](concepts.md#c04), such as "the department is inside the company", is not in the definition of any [we](concepts.md#c04); seeing the two as inclusion is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) outside these definitions; a [social person](concepts.md#c03) can draw the department as we without drawing the company as we.

(Derived from the definitions of [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) and of [we](concepts.md#c04): the relation of inclusion is not in the definition of any we; seeing the two as inclusion is a distinction outside these definitions)

<note>

*"Of course the department is inside the company" looks self-evident, but it is also a drawing. Some treat the department as their own without treating the company so.*

</note>

### 4.11 When They Conflict, Which Prevails Cannot Be Derived

**Conclusion 4.11**: the [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) by which a [social person](concepts.md#c03) draws its various [we](concepts.md#c04) along different dimensions all take part in its single [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of it falls out of the whole weight. So from which we this output comes cannot be derived.

(Derived from Conclusion 4.4 and the definitions of [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) and of [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17), and the [law of the black box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25): each drawing takes part in later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); the whole [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at a wo takes part in its weight; its own segment is fixed by dependence, not by any [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), and a dimension is a distinction; weight does not fix the result)

Notes:
1. An [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) may be classed by the researcher as "loyal" along the dimension of one [we](concepts.md#c04) and as "disloyal" along another; these are all [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) the researcher makes after the fact with this output as object, which may include [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) (Conclusion 4.6).
2. The [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) drawing different [we](concepts.md#c04) take part in the same [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); by [cognitive obscuration](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c35), pushing one direction up necessarily pushes down the sum of the other directions. A researcher counting which side a person mostly falls toward in such cases gets an [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45), not a reading of weight (definition of impression: all statistical estimates of a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) are impressions).

<note>

*When department and company conflict, what the person finally does falls out of the whole person together, not decided by any one "we" alone. Saying he "chose the department" is an onlooker's after-the-fact account. Counting which side he usually takes is also only an estimate of him.*

</note>

Calibration: on an evening of overtime, the [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) drawing "our company" and "our family" are both in his [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); pushing one up pushes the other down; he stays, and onlookers say he chose the company, but at the moment he stayed, the distinctions drawing both [we](concepts.md#c04) were in the weight. (Signpost: Lazarsfeld and others' cross-pressures; Stryker's identity salience. Where they meet: multiple belongings pull a person in different directions. Where they part: identity theory explains which identity a situation activates by its salience; this paper says which we made him do so cannot be derived, and salience, if taken as a statistical estimate, is by the definition of [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) an impression.)

### 4.12 We Under One Name

**Conclusion 4.12**: under the same name, such as the same party or the same country, the [we](concepts.md#c04) of its members are not guaranteed to be the same; drawing a smaller we under the name, such as a faction, is not excluded by the definition either.

(Derived from Conclusion 4.3 and the definition of [we](concepts.md#c04) Note 2: we is one-sided; the same material does not imply the same [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18))

<note>

*Members of the same party do not necessarily have the same "our party" in mind; forming smaller circles inside the party is natural too. Treating "the party" as one we shared by all is discussed in Section 10.*

</note>

Calibration: "No factions in the party, only a thousand oddities."

### 4.13 People's We and Things' Ours Do Not Imply Each Other

**Conclusion 4.13**: a [social person](concepts.md#c03) drawing B and other [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) into [we](concepts.md#c04) does not imply that it will class some thing as ours; classing some thing as ours does not imply that it will draw some we, nor that the presentations it drew into we will agree. A [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) about people and a distinction about things may be linked, but they delimit their objects by different definitions, and neither implies the other.

(Derived from the definition of [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), the definition of [we](concepts.md#c04) (3.3 Note 9), [belonging distinction of things](concepts.md#c07) (3.6), and [distinction margin](concepts.md#c08) (3.7): the construct used by the distinction drawing we has [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) counted as [selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) as its object; the belonging distinction of things has things as its object; neither definition implies the other)

<note>

*Treating B as we does not necessarily mean treating what B holds as ours. Calling a thing ours does not necessarily mean drawing some group of people, nor that others agree. The edge among people and the edge among things are different ways of drawing; having one does not mean having the other.*

</note>

Calibration: A draws B as [we](concepts.md#c04), yet classes some thing (hereafter this thing) as "yours"; or A may class this thing as "ours" while B does not class it so.

## 5. Distinction Changes the Person, Output Changes the Mode

### 5.1 Distinction Changes the Person; Only Output Changes the Mode

**Conclusion 5.1**: a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) changes the [social person](concepts.md#c03) who makes it: it becomes that social person's [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and takes part in its later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). Changes this [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) brings to an [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) come only through that [social person](concepts.md#c03)'s [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); before the output arrives, the mode's existing outputs, [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), feasible [paths](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07), and [persistence criterion](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c01) do not change in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) because of this distinction.

(Derived from the definitions of [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29), [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09), [threshold](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c03), and [feasible set](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c11), and Conclusion 4.4: the content of a distinction changes views and does not rewrite [what is seen](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c27); a [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) takes part in another node's [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) only through [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); an economic mode is the researcher's classification of nodes' outputs; each drawing takes part in later weights)

Note: this conclusion does not say that [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) do not matter. A distinction takes part in [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); as long as the [social person](concepts.md#c03) who made it is still a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) and this distinction still takes part in its weight, every later [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of that social person falls out of a weight that includes it (Conclusion 4.11, definition of wo); which output differs because of it, and by how much, cannot be derived ([law of the black box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25)).

<note>

*Once you draw someone as "one of us" in your mind, what changes first is you: this drawing becomes part of you and affects what you do later. For the mode to change, you must do or say something that goes outside. Before that, nothing outside has changed.*

</note>

Calibration: a boss has, in a [decision](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c06), already come down on firing an employee; until it is said, that employee still works here.

### 5.2 Toward Others Only Through Output

**Conclusion 5.2**: a [social person](concepts.md#c03)'s distinction drawing [we](concepts.md#c04), and its [belonging distinctions of things](concepts.md#c07), act on others only through its [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); the [illusory constructs](concepts.md#c01) it uses, such as we and "ours", have effect only through the outputs that use them. What [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others is its output, not its [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); if the others make a distinction of it, that is their own distinction.

(Derived from the definitions of [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), and [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), the definition of [we](concepts.md#c04) (3.3 Note 9), and the [belonging distinction of things](concepts.md#c07) (3.6 Note 7): the material of a distinction is only the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10); what is exchanged in society is views, not [what is seen](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c27), and if the listener makes a distinction, it is its own distinction of the sentence)

<note>

*Whom you count as your own in your mind, others cannot touch; what they can touch is only what you did and said. What others read from it is their own understanding.*

</note>

### 5.3 Distinctions May Shape Each Other Through Output

**Conclusion 5.3**: let A and B be two [social persons](concepts.md#c03). If A's output arrives at B, it takes part in B's later [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) and may change B's later [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); if B's output arrives at A, it may likewise change A's distinctions. If, after A's output has arrived at B, an output that later falls out of B's weight arrives at A in turn, A's output, by way of B, takes part again in A's own weight, forming a loop; the two sides' distinctions may shape each other through this loop. Whether distinctions change, and in which direction, cannot be derived. When either side's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) no longer [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the other, this [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) is broken; whether and when it breaks cannot be derived.

(Derived from the definitions of [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31), and [coupling](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36), Conclusion 5.2, and [cognitive obscuration](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c35): the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at a wo all takes part in its [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); where and how far a change pushes cannot be derived)

Notes:
1. "I think you think I am ..." is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) made by the side that says it; the layer "you think" is its guess about the other side's distinction, not a distinction the other side actually made (definition of distinction). So however many layers are stacked, they are in the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) of the side that makes it.
2. If these layers are distinctions actually made by each side one after another, and not merely multiple layers stated within one [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), they are not a simultaneous stack: by the definition of distinction, a distinction is not in its own material; by the premise of Section 1, it [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself only before the next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09); if one side's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) arrives at the other, a distinction the other side makes with it as material has as material only [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) before it that has already arrived at it. So this recursion, if it goes back and forth in time, is the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) of this conclusion. One layer up there is no new kind of act, only the same kind of act applied again; in that case each layer is a distinction (definition of distinction).
3. By Conclusion 5.2, only [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) flow on the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31), including [avowals](concepts.md#c06) (Section 6) and possibly outputs about things such as "this is ours"; [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) are in each side's [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13), not in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06).
4. The [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) of this conclusion is described with the loop of the tools paper and sets up no new concept.

<note>

*If what I do reaches you, it may change how you draw me; if what you do comes back, it may change how I draw you. Back and forth like this is a cycle. Whether it changes and which way, cannot be said. Layers like "I think you think I ..." each exist only in one person's mind, and happen back and forth over time, not stacked at once.*

</note>

Calibration: two new colleagues: one helps first, the other returns a meal, and they grow closer; or a misunderstanding drives them apart. (Signpost: the "mutual dominance" of Yogācāra. Where they meet: *Twenty Verses*, "through mutual dominance the two consciousnesses become determined": two minds condition and shape each other, neither directly touching the other. Where they part: Dharmapāla holds that seeds and manifestations are simultaneous mutual causes; this paper's [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) has delay, and each [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself one step late.)

### 5.4 Others' Distinctions and My Feasible Set

**Conclusion 5.4**: if some [antecedent](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c08) of some [path](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07) of B can be supplied only by A's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), then whether this path is feasible depends on A's output; A's output falls out under A's [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), and if A has made a relevant [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), it is one part of the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) taking part in that weight. Whether A's output differs because of that distinction, and by how much, cannot be derived.

(Derived from the definitions of [threshold](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c03), [antecedent](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c08), and [feasible set](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c11), Conclusions 5.2 and 4.6, the definition of [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), and the [law of the black box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25): if any antecedent is absent, the [path](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07) is infeasible; when an antecedent can be supplied only by A's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), whether it is present depends on A's output)

<note>

*Whether you can take some path sometimes depends on whether others will act, such as whether they lend you money. Whether they will falls out of their whole person; whether they count you as their own is only one part. Whether that was the reason, nobody can say, not even they themselves.*

</note>

Calibration: whether a friend lends you money or introduces you to a job decides whether a path of yours is passable; whether he does falls out of his whole person, and whether he counts you as one of his own is only one part. (Signpost: Granovetter's weak ties; Weber's social closure. Where they meet: opportunities are distributed through relationships. Where they part: they treat relationships as observable; in this paper [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) cannot be seen, only [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) can.)

### 5.5 Passable and Passed

**Conclusion 5.5**: if an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at a receiver, it can have two kinds of effect on the receiver's paths: first, supplying or removing an [antecedent](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c08) of some [path](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07), making it feasible or infeasible; second, taking part in the receiver's [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), making a difference to whether it takes some still feasible path. The second kind does not change a path's antecedents. An output can have both kinds or only one; which it has, and how strong, cannot be derived.

(Derived from the definitions of [threshold](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c03), [antecedent](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c08), [feasible set](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c11), and [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29), Conclusion 5.4, and the [law of the black box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25): the feasible set answers whether something is physically possible, not whether it will happen; [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) takes part in the receiver's [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); weight does not fix the result)

Notes:
1. Speech is also [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) (definition of output). If that [path](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07) is still in the [feasible set](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c11) in that round, and a threat, as speech, removes none of its [antecedents](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c08), the receiver not taking that path is a result falling out of its [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) (definitions of [threshold](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c03) and feasible set).
2. Whether a [path](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07) is feasible in a round is judged by the [threshold](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c03); recording "did not go" as "could not go" confuses the two kinds of effect.

<note>

*What others do affects the paths you can take in two ways: one really blocks the road, such as locking the door; the other makes you not want to go, such as threatening you. If someone threatens you with a knife but has not acted yet, the road may still be passable; you decide not to go. The two effects can both be present or only one; which, and how strong, cannot be said.*

</note>

Calibration: a boss tells an employee, "You're fired." The employee may stop coming to work: that sentence took part in his [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), and his [path](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07) is still passable; or the company may deactivate his access card, so he cannot get in even if he wants to: an [antecedent](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c08) of the path has been removed.

### 5.6 Influencing Others' Distinctions Is Still a Wager

**Conclusion 5.6**: a [social person](concepts.md#c03)'s [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) may have others' [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) as its object, such as taking a stance, persuading, or proposing a different distinction; if such output [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the other, it takes part in the other's [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), but whether the other's distinction changes because of it, and which way, cannot be derived. Under the [application assumption](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c50), the [decision](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c06) to make such output is still a [forced wager](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c11). The researcher classing some output as having others' distinctions as its object is the researcher's distinction, not a reading of the outputter's distinction.

(Derived from the definitions of [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) and [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), the definition of [decision](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c06), the definitions of [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29), [cognitive obscuration](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c35), the [law of the black box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25), the [application assumption](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c50), and the [forced wager](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c11))

<note>

*If you want others to count you as one of their own, all you can do is change what they receive: take a stance, say kind words, pick a side. Whether they change because of it, and which way, cannot be said, so this too is a wager. Onlookers saying "he is courting people" is the onlookers' judgment.*

</note>

### 5.7 Distinctions About Things Also Shape Each Other Through Output

**Conclusion 5.7**: A makes an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) with some thing (hereafter this thing) as its object, such as "this is ours"; if this output [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at another [social person](concepts.md#c03) B, it becomes part of B's later [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and takes part in B's later [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) and outputs about this thing. How B will later draw this thing, whether it makes the same classification, and how its outputs about this thing will fall out, cannot be derived. If B in turn makes outputs that arrive at A or C, the process can continue recursively.

(Derived from the [belonging distinction of things](concepts.md#c07) (3.6), Conclusions 4.2, 4.3, 5.2, and 5.3, the definition of [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), and [cognitive obscuration](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c35): others' [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), once it has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), becomes one's own [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13); it does not directly carry others' inner [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18))

Notes:
1. When A says "this is ours", the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) holds only this [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); whether A had in fact classed this thing as ours cannot be derived from it (Conclusion 4.6).
2. A's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), if it [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at B, is material for [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) B may later make; it does not guarantee that B accepts this classification; even if B also says "this is ours", it does not prove that the two sides' [we](concepts.md#c04) or their "ours" are the same.
3. This conclusion implies no rules of use, restricted use, disposal, or allocation of this thing, nor does it presuppose any ownership of this thing between A and B.

<note>

*When A says "this is ours", B receives one more sentence about the thing. B can repeat it, disagree, or do something else; but how A and B actually draw, others still cannot see. These words and acts, going back and forth, become new material later.*

</note>

Calibration: A says this thing is ours; B, having heard, says to C that this thing is ours. The researcher can see two [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); that the words are the same does not imply that A's and B's [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) about this thing are the same.

## 6. Avowal

### 6.1 Avowal Changes the Starting Point

**Conclusion 6.1**: once an [avowal](concepts.md#c06) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), it is in that wo's later [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and takes part in its later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); so as long as the receiver is still a wo, each later [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) it makes of the avower along this dimension starts from a history that includes this avowal. The same holds when the avowal arrives at the avower itself. A retraction is another [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); before it arrives, the earlier one has already taken part in the weights of that period.

(Derived from the definitions of [avowal](concepts.md#c06), [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), and [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), and the premise of Section 1: an arrived [writing](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c29) becomes part of [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and takes part in later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); the avower's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) arrives at itself before the next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09))

<note>

*Once "I am Taiwanese" is said, how those who heard it see you afterwards starts from having heard it; so do you. Saying "I take it back" is only saying one more sentence; the earlier one has already had its effect.*

</note>

Calibration: after a naturalization [avowal](concepts.md#c06), even if one later renounces the nationality, "once took the naturalization oath" remains in one's own and others' [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13).

### 6.2 Avowal and Distinction Can Diverge

**Conclusion 6.2**: an [avowal](concepts.md#c06) does not imply that the avower has drawn, along this dimension, the class it asks for; even when the avower does draw so, it does not imply how deeply.

(Derived from the definition of [avowal](concepts.md#c06) Note 2, Conclusion 4.6, and the definition of [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02): the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) contains only [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); view attribution can be wrong)

<note>

*Saying "I am Taiwanese" does not mean one has really drawn oneself into that class; even if one has, how deeply cannot be seen. Saying "this is ours" is an output about a thing, not an avowal as defined in this paper.*

</note>

Calibration: (Signpost: Goffman's presentation of self. Where they meet: people present themselves before others, and the presentation can differ from the "backstage". Where they part: Goffman assumes a backstage to compare with; in this paper, [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) cannot be fully read even by the one making the [avowal](concepts.md#c06) ([wall of the blind spot](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c09)), and there is no backstage to compare with.)

### 6.3 Avowal Returns to Oneself

**Conclusion 6.3**: an [avowal](concepts.md#c06) that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the avower itself is one piece of material for its later drawing of [we](concepts.md#c04); if the avowal has oneself as object, such as "I am a citizen of some country", it is also material for the [construction of the self](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c28). In which direction it pushes cannot be derived.

(Derived from Conclusions 4.2, 4.4, and 6.1, the definition of [construction of the self](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c28), and [cognitive obscuration](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c35))

<note>

*The "I am a citizen of some country" you have said comes back to you and becomes one piece of material for how you see yourself later. Whether it makes you identify more or feel more awkward, cannot be said.*

</note>

Calibration: someone who has said "our company" many times may come to feel it more and more, or may feel ever less sure saying it.

### 6.4 Avowal Reaches Later People Through a Signal Container

**Conclusion 6.4**: an [avowal](concepts.md#c06) placed in a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) can, when the avower is absent or even after it has made its [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43), [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at later receivers.

(Derived from the definition of [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26): the content of a signal container can be written by a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) and [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) to wo at many [moments](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09))

<note>

*Written oaths, signed documents, identity cards: others can still see them when the person is absent, and after the person has died.*

</note>

Calibration: oaths, signatures, identity cards, genealogies.

### 6.5 Whether One Draws Accordingly Cannot Be Derived

**Conclusion 6.5**: if an [avowal](concepts.md#c06) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at a [social person](concepts.md#c03), whether this social person draws the avower into the class the avowal asks for cannot be derived.

(Derived from Conclusion 4.3, [cognitive obscuration](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c35), and the [law of the black box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25))

Note: an [avowal](concepts.md#c06) is [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); if it [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at a receiver that is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), it takes part in the receiver's [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) (Conclusion 6.1) and may lead the receiver not to take some [path](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07); it may also supply or remove an [antecedent](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c08) of some [path](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07); which effect it has cannot be derived (Conclusion 5.5). Making some path infeasible for those who do not draw accordingly belongs to imposing one's [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) on others, and is left for later work (Section 11).

<note>

*When you say "I am Taiwanese", whether others count you so cannot be said. An avowal only gives material; it does not force anyone. The kind where "whoever does not count you so will suffer for it" is left to the next paper.*

</note>

Calibration: (Signpost: Austin's speech acts. Where they meet: words themselves do things. Where they part: Austin treats the listener's uptake as one condition for a speech act to succeed; this paper says that once an [avowal](concepts.md#c06) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) it changes the starting point; whether it is taken up cannot be derived and does not affect this.)

## 7. Economic Mode and Inside Outside Difference

### 7.1 An Economic Mode Does Not Need Second-Order Capacity

**Conclusion 7.1**: an [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) does not take participants' having [second-order capacity](concepts.md#c02) as a condition; so it does not take participants' drawing [we](concepts.md#c04), [plural you](concepts.md#c05), or [belonging distinctions of things](concepts.md#c07) as a condition of its standing.

(Derived from the definition of [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) in the Pattern paper: the definition takes neither [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01), [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), nor the above classifications as conditions)

Note: so the [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) of the Pattern paper, the [resonance](../paper_2ccbf179_58cf_4851_bd72_5fa886e4b2c3/concepts.md#c09) of the stranger paper, and the [tacit coordination](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c06) of the acquaintance paper do not take the [social person](concepts.md#c03) as a condition; this paper does not change them. This does not mean that participants who already have [second-order capacity](concepts.md#c02) certainly make no [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); it means only that the economic mode itself does not take these distinctions as a necessary [antecedent](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c08).

<note>

*An economic mode can exist first, without anyone calling others "we" or calling things "ours". The division of labor in rat colonies and ant colonies relies on outputs that connect, not on participants first drawing everyone onto one side.*

</note>

Calibration: the division of labor in rat colonies and ant colonies; the coordination of immune cells.

### 7.2 Distinctions Take Arrived Mode Outputs as Material

**Conclusion 7.2**: when a [social person](concepts.md#c03) makes a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) with people or things in some existing [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) as its object, the material can only be [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has already [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it: it may include [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of that mode that have arrived at it, and also outputs from outside the mode that have arrived at it; a distinction does not arrive before the material it depends on.

(Derived from the definition of [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), Conclusion 4.2, and the [belonging distinction of things](concepts.md#c07) (3.6): a distinction cannot take [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has not yet [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10))

Notes:
1. What this paper calls "second-order reshaping" refers to a [social person](concepts.md#c03) making further [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) about the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of an existing mode, and these distinctions taking part in later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) and possibly entering new [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) through output (if it [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others and comes back). It is a description of this process and sets up no new mechanism by which distinctions directly alter physical structure; a distinction changes the social person who makes it, and the mode changes through its output (Conclusion 5.1).
2. "First an economic mode, then reshaping" means that in the reshaping analyzed here, the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of an existing mode first become material; it does not claim that all [economic modes](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) precede all [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of [social persons](concepts.md#c03) in time.
3. An [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) is the researcher's [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); what a [social person](concepts.md#c03) draws is its own distinction; the two need not match in range or dimension.

<note>

*A researcher who sees that the outputs of a group of vendors connect can record them as a night market; only when the vendors themselves receive some of those outputs can they draw "our street" or "these are ours". What the researcher and the vendors draw need not be the same.*

</note>

### 7.3 Reshaping Adds Margins of Two Kinds, People and Things

**Conclusion 7.3**: a [social person](concepts.md#c03) can make further [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) with the [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) material of an [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) as object:
1. drawing the existing practice that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it as a practice, and drawing its own earlier [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of this practice as a view, rather than a necessary arrangement prior to distinction;
2. drawing [distinction margins](concepts.md#c08) about people, such as [we](concepts.md#c04) and [plural you](concepts.md#c05), over the participants that have [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it;
3. drawing [distinction margins](concepts.md#c08) about things, such as mine, yours, ours, and yours (plural), over the [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) of things that have [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it.

All three are possibilities; having [second-order capacity](concepts.md#c02) does not guarantee that they occur, and they need not occur in order. Drawing [we](concepts.md#c04) about people does not necessarily bring classing things as ours (Conclusion 4.13).

(Derived from the definition of distinction, Conclusion 7.2, the definitions of [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01) and [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), the definitions of [we](concepts.md#c04) and [plural you](concepts.md#c05) (3.3, 3.4), the [belonging distinction of things](concepts.md#c07) (3.6), and the [distinction margin](concepts.md#c08) (3.7): the boundary itself can be distinguished; the object and the [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) still fall out of each [social person](concepts.md#c03) itself)

<note>

*Before, there were only outputs that connected; now a vendor can draw people as "we/you (plural)" and goods as "ours/yours (plural)". These are only drawings he adds himself; they need not appear together, and others' agreement is not guaranteed.*

</note>

Calibration: vendors on a vacant lot set up stalls again and again, and the researcher records them as a night market by their output relations; one vendor later says "our street" and, pointing at a batch of goods, says "this is ours". Speech is recordable [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); whether there is in fact a corresponding inner [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) is not in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06).

### 7.4 Reshaping Is Still a Wager

**Conclusion 7.4**: if the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of second-order reshaping [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at another participant, and that participant is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), they take part in its [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); whether its later [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) (if it is a [social person](concepts.md#c03)) and outputs change because of it, and which way, cannot be derived. After reshaping, the existing [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) may continue or may undergo a [criterion break](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c10); it is not guaranteed to favor continuation.

(Derived from the definition of [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), Conclusions 5.3 and 5.7, [cognitive obscuration](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c35), the [law of the black box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25), and the definition of [learning](../paper_2ccbf179_58cf_4851_bd72_5fa886e4b2c3/concepts.md#c04): learning has no preset direction; change is not guaranteed to favor the continuation of the mode)

<note>

*When someone starts saying "this is ours", whether others will think so and what they will do afterwards cannot be said. The mode may run as usual, or change its practice, or break.*

</note>

Calibration: someone says a batch of goods is ours; others keep moving the goods, and someone else takes a different stance; the researcher can [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) the later [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); each person's [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) cannot be derived from these outputs.

### 7.5 We Is Not Necessarily Built on an Economic Mode

**Conclusion 7.5**: drawing [we](concepts.md#c04) does not require that the one who draws and the [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) drawn in be in the same [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09).

(Derived from the definitions of [we](concepts.md#c04) and of [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) and Conclusion 4.2: the material for drawing we is only [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has already [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10); the definition does not take both sides' being in one [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) as a condition)

Note: so this section discusses "[distinction margins](concepts.md#c08) on an [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09)"; it does not claim that all [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) drawing [we](concepts.md#c04) or all distinctions about things are produced by economic modes.

<note>

*Not every "we" grows out of the division of labor in one undertaking. Even without taking part in a common mode, people may draw distant people as we.*

</note>

Calibration: fans draw the members of a distant team as "[we](concepts.md#c04)" without having to take part together in any particular [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09).

### 7.6 An Economic Mode Shows a Distinction Margin

**Conclusion 7.6**: if a [social person](concepts.md#c03), with people or things of some [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) that have [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it as material, draws [we](concepts.md#c04), [plural you](concepts.md#c05), or [belonging distinctions of things](concepts.md#c07), then that economic mode shows a [distinction margin](concepts.md#c08) for this social person. This [margin](concepts.md#c08) is drawn by the social person itself; it is not a classification carried by the existing [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and arrival structure, nor a boundary shared by the other participants.

(Derived from the definition of [distinction margin](concepts.md#c08) (3.7) and Conclusions 7.1, 7.2, and 7.3: an [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) can stand without such classifications, yet classifications can be made on the material of the mode that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10))

Notes:
1. "Shows" means only showing within that [social person](concepts.md#c03)'s [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); for an [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) to show an inside and an outside, it goes through its [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) (Conclusion 5.1).
2. [Margins](concepts.md#c08) about people and about things can overlap, conflict, or appear separately; there is no a priori margin shared by all participants.
3. A [distinction margin](concepts.md#c08) is in the [social person](concepts.md#c03)'s [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13), not in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06); its effect on others still goes through [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) (Conclusion 5.2).

<note>

*The economic mode could already run. Now one person among them, in their own eyes, draws the same people and things onto different sides; this is a new layer of distinction appearing on the economic mode, not the old line suddenly changing.*

</note>

### 7.7 A Distinction Margin Implies No Use or Exclusion

**Conclusion 7.7**: a [social person](concepts.md#c03) classing some thing (hereafter this thing) as ours does not imply any particular relation of use, taking, disposal, or exclusion concerning this thing for itself, for those it draws as [we](concepts.md#c04), or for those it draws as [plural you](concepts.md#c05); nor does it imply that other social persons class this thing according to its classification.

(Derived from the [belonging distinction of things](concepts.md#c07) (3.6) Notes 1 and 4, and Conclusions 4.3, 4.13, 5.2, and 6.5: a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) is neither the definition of a right nor something that directly [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others' distinctions)

<note>

*Saying "this is ours" means neither that one may use it nor that others may not; it does not even mean that others counted in the "we" think so. All we know now is that this person has drawn a margin of their own.*

</note>

Calibration: A says this thing is ours; B still uses this thing, and C uses it too. The [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of use can be [recorded](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06); who is entitled to use it cannot be derived from A's [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); nor can B's and C's distinctions be derived from their use.

### 7.8 Distinctions Take Part in the Mode's Later Operation Through Output

**Conclusion 7.8**: the [distinction margins](concepts.md#c08) a [social person](concepts.md#c03) draws over people and things, as part of its own [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13), take part in its later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); if the outputs it lets fall [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at another participant, and that participant is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), they take part in its later weight; if it is a social person, they may also become material for its further distinctions and outputs. This lets an [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) that did not originally take [we](concepts.md#c04) or ours as a condition take in repeated [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) formed by distinctions through [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28). Whether particular outputs differ because of some [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), and which way, cannot be derived.

(Derived from Conclusions 4.4, 5.2, 5.3, 5.7, 7.1, and 7.6, the definition of [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), [cognitive obscuration](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c35), and the [law of the black box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25): a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself and takes part in one's own [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); only [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) arrives at others; neither beforehand nor afterwards can a whole output be uniquely attributed to some distinction)

Notes:
1. "Taking part in operation" in this conclusion does not mean that some [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) necessarily causes an observable change in [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); the same output may also fall out under different distinctions.
2. A researcher who sees A and B both say "this is ours" sees only two [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); it cannot infer back that they already share a [we](concepts.md#c04), or have the same [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) about the same thing.
3. The [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) can keep continuing by its original criterion; what this paper adds is a description of the sources of material for its later [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); it does not replace the Pattern paper's judgments.

<note>

*How someone divides people and things returns to their own history; what they do and say may reach others and lead them to make distinctions about the same matter later. The original economic mode can thus gain an extra loop in which human distinctions take part, but no particular result is guaranteed.*

</note>

Calibration: A says "this batch of goods is ours"; B, having heard, may say the same to C, or may not. Either way, once A's words [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at B, they become part of B's later [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13); the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) holds only the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of transmission, not whether each person's "ours" is the same (0.3).

### 7.9 A Distinction Margin May Make an Inside Outside Difference Appear in the Mode

**Conclusion 7.9**: a [social person](concepts.md#c03)'s [distinction margin](concepts.md#c08) takes part in its [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) it lets fall toward people or things on different sides may differ. If these outputs [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at other participants, they may make some of their [paths](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07) feasible or infeasible (Conclusion 5.4); if a participant reached is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), these outputs also take part in its weight, possibly leading it not to take some still feasible paths (Conclusion 5.5). Therefore, a distinction margin, through output, may make an [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) appear in the [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06). Whether an inside outside difference appears, where it falls, and from which [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) some difference comes, cannot be derived.

(Derived from the definitions of [distinction margin](concepts.md#c08) (3.7) and [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) (3.8), Conclusions 5.2, 5.4, and 7.8, and the [law of the black box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25): [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) take part in [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); only [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others; output can decide an [antecedent](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c08) of another's [path](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07); weight does not fix the result)

Notes:
1. A [distinction margin](concepts.md#c08) changes the [social person](concepts.md#c03) who makes it; what changes the mode is the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that falls out of its [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) (Conclusion 5.1).
2. This conclusion does not say that some [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) necessarily brings an [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09), nor that an inside outside difference will persist; the same [social person](concepts.md#c03)'s [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) toward the two sides may also be the same.

<note>

*Dividing people in one's mind into insiders and outsiders first changes the person, and the mode has not changed yet; but it is part of what the person does later. What they do to insiders and outsiders may differ, such as whether to lend money or what price to give. If these acts reach others, the paths others can take may differ. The researcher may then see in the record: within the same mode, one practice inside and another outside. Whether this will happen, and from which division, cannot be said.*

</note>

Calibration: in hiring, an employee recommends juniors from "our school" first; what the researcher [records](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) is that hiring [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) differ between alumni and non-alumni.

### 7.10 An Inside Outside Difference Among Many Does Not Need a Shared We

**Conclusion 7.10**: the [distinction margins](concepts.md#c08) of several [social persons](concepts.md#c03), each through its own [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), may make the same [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) appear in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06): in the outputs of many, the researcher sees that outputs of the same kind differ within a group of participants and across it. This inside outside difference does not require these social persons to share a [we](concepts.md#c04), nor that their [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) agree; it is judged by output. Changes in each person's distinctions and the movement of the inside outside difference in the record need not be in step: distinctions may change while outputs do not; outputs may change without implying that distinctions have changed.

(Derived from the definition of [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) (3.8), the definition of [we](concepts.md#c04) (3.3 Note 2), and Conclusions 4.6, 7.8, and 7.9: each [social person](concepts.md#c03)'s [distinction margin](concepts.md#c08) takes part in the mode through its own [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); we belongs only to the social person who uses it; the same output can fall out under different [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); distinctions are not in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06))

Notes:
1. Saying that several [social persons](concepts.md#c03) "all draw someone inside" is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) the researcher makes with these social persons as object, which may include [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) of each one's outputs; the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) holds only their [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) (Conclusion 4.6).
2. "Joining" and "leaving" have two senses: a [social person](concepts.md#c03) drawing some [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) in or out belongs to that social person's [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) (3.7); the [boundary shift](concepts.md#c11) of the inside outside difference in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) belongs to the researcher's [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) (3.8). The two can be out of step.

<note>

*Each member of a family has an "our family" in mind, which need not be the same. But as long as what they all do differs between family and outsiders, such as whether a meal is charged or whether things must be asked for first, the researcher will see a clear boundary in the record. This boundary does not need a shared "our family". When someone moves in or out, the drawing in the mind and the practice in action need not change together.*

</note>

Calibration: a roommate who lives under the same roof is not counted by the family in "our family"; a son who has worked elsewhere for years is still counted in. The names in the household register are the content of a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) (Conclusion 4.5), not "our family" itself. A new daughter-in-law is already treated like family in food and lodging, while her in-laws have not yet drawn her in; a divorced father still draws his child into "our family", while his [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) toward the child are already different.

### 7.11 Claimed Boundary and Inside Outside Difference Can Diverge

**Conclusion 7.11**: if an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) whose content is a classification arrives at its receivers, and later outputs of the outputter and of other [social persons](concepts.md#c03) [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) in turn, then if there is an [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06), it is drawn from outputs the researcher has registered and, under a stated [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23), counted as of the same kind; these later outputs, if registered and counted as of that kind, may be among them; it is not drawn from the [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) alone. When later outputs are incompatible with the claimed boundary, the two can diverge in the record. The gap between them can be registered; how large it will turn out to be cannot be derived in advance, and why it is so cannot be derived.

(Derived from the definitions of [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) (3.9), [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) (3.8), [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06), [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45), and [enforcement](../paper_2ccbf179_58cf_4851_bd72_5fa886e4b2c3/concepts.md#c11), and Conclusions 5.5, 6.5, 7.9, and 7.10: others' impression of a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) takes as material its past outputs (definition of impression); rules written into a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) are not guaranteed to be followed; the inside outside difference is judged by [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); whether one draws accordingly cannot be derived)

Notes:
1. If the receiver is a [social person](concepts.md#c03), the output whose content is a classification is one piece of material for its later [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) (Conclusion 4.2): it can compare later [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) with it. The result of the comparison cannot be derived.
2. A [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) and an [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) are both [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) the researcher draws from [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28): the former from one output whose content is a classification, the latter from outputs of the same kind, which may include that one (3.8, 3.9); an [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) is also not a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of any [social person](concepts.md#c03) studied (3.8 Note 2).
3. This conclusion does not say that the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) a [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) is drawn from has no effect: if this [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the receiver, and the receiver is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), it takes part in its [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) (Conclusion 5.5).
4. The gap is relative to the [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) and dimension the researcher states (3.8 Note 1, 3.9 Note 1); under another cut the gap can differ.

<note>

*A boundary said or written is not a boundary acted out. A man says at his wedding "you are my wife", then spends his days carousing and does not care when his wife has someone else; onlookers will not treat him as a husband, though the marriage certificate still says so. Where the boundary finally falls depends on what everyone says and does, possibly including afterwards. How far the two boundaries differ, the researcher can record; why they differ so much, cannot be said.*

</note>

Calibration: one who [avowed](concepts.md#c06) marriage but does not act like a husband is not treated as a husband by onlookers, while the marriage certificate still names him husband; people smoke where smoking is expressly banned; the company forbids moonlighting, and everyone moonlights; the speed limit on a road is one number, and the actual speed of traffic is another. (Signpost: the debate on names and realities; the Thomas theorem and Merton's self-fulfilling prophecy. Where they meet: spoken classifications and actual consequences can be viewed apart. Where they part: the rectification of names requires names to match realities, while this paper only registers the gap and does not require a match; the Thomas theorem says a definition taken as real is real in its consequences, while this paper says that even if the consequences in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) match the claim, the claim does not thereby become a reading of [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18).)

### 7.12 Inside Outside Differences and Claimed Boundaries Do Not Imply Second-Order Distinction

**Conclusion 7.12**: the researcher registering an [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) or a [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) implies neither that participants have [second-order capacity](concepts.md#c02) nor that the difference or boundary comes from some [distinction margin](concepts.md#c08).

(Derived from the definitions of [illusory construct](concepts.md#c01) (0.5) and [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) (3.9), and Conclusions 7.1, 4.6, and 7.8: [second-order capacity](concepts.md#c02) is an illusory construct; a claimed boundary is drawn from [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); an [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) does not take second-order capacity as a condition; [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) are not in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06); a whole output cannot be uniquely attributed to some distinction)

<note>

*Seeing that insiders and outsiders are treated differently, or that someone claims a boundary, does not mean anyone has drawn "we" in their mind. Ants also tell nestmates from outsiders; by this paper's notation, ants have no "we".*

</note>

Calibration: ants recognize nestmates and attack [individuals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c20) from other nests; chimpanzee groups patrol their territory. (Signpost: Morgan's canon; the "logical problem" described by Povinelli and Lurz. Where they meet: from behavior one cannot tell [second-order capacity](concepts.md#c02) from complex first-order behavior. Where they part: they ask how to judge capacity from behavior; this paper's scope of the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) does not register [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) in the first place, so it makes no judgment.)

## 8. We and the Exterior

### 8.1 Two Layers Coexist

**Conclusion 8.1**: other [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) drawn into [we](concepts.md#c04) by a social person (not including itself) are still that [social person](concepts.md#c03)'s [exterior](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c04) if they are sources of [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it.

(Derived from the definition of [exterior](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c04) in the Pattern paper and the definitions of [we](concepts.md#c04) and of [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18): the exterior is every source of [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) other than oneself; the content of a distinction changes views and does not rewrite [what is seen](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c27))

Notes:
1. The [exterior](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c04) is structural, determined by sources of [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), and the researcher's [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) may register part of it; the [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) drawing [we](concepts.md#c04) is not in the record (Conclusion 4.6). The two layers coexist; the latter does not replace the former.
2. Drawing [we](concepts.md#c04) does not divide the whole [exterior](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c04): the part of the exterior not drawn into we remains exterior as in the Pattern paper; how a [social person](concepts.md#c03) draws the rest belongs to content and is not formalized in this paper (3.3 Note 1).

<note>

*When you treat a colleague as "we", he is structurally still a person outside you, your exterior. Drawing "we" is only your inner drawing; it does not change who he is or where he sends things to you from. Other people and things you have not drawn in are also still exterior.*

</note>

Calibration: family is also [exterior](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c04): family members' words must reach you to [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at you.

### 8.2 Crossing the Line Does Not Compose a New Self

**Conclusion 8.2**: the [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) drawing [we](concepts.md#c04) places oneself and other [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) on the same side; but this does not imply that there is a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) composed of them.

(Derived from the definitions of [we](concepts.md#c04), distinction, [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), and [recutting](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c22): several [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) recut into one presentation is a self only if it is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) and draws itself as a self by [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); drawing we is a distinction of a [social person](concepts.md#c03), not a distinction of that whole about itself)

Note: this differs from a [self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)'s boundary moving. By the definition of self, who is a self is defined by itself; when the self-definition differs, the self's boundary differs; what the self-definition takes in is part of this self. [We](concepts.md#c04) is the opposite: those drawn in are still counted as other selves. The former is the self growing; the latter is the self unchanged, with other [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) counted as selves beside it.

<note>

*Drawing "we" places you and others together, but it does not thereby give birth to a new, thinking big "self". This differs from "the self growing": with a cochlear implant, the implant becomes part of you; treating a colleague as we, the colleague is still another person.*

</note>

Calibration: (Signpost: Luhmann's system and environment. Where they meet: a boundary is drawn by the side that draws it, and inside and outside are environment to each other. Where they part: Luhmann's system draws its own boundary; in this paper no group draws boundaries; those who draw are always individual [social persons](concepts.md#c03).)

### 8.3 Drawing as We Is Not an Arrival

**Conclusion 8.3**: drawing [we](concepts.md#c04) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the one who draws (Conclusion 4.4), and does not make the output of those drawn in arrive at the one who draws: whether the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of a [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) drawn in arrives at the one who draws still depends on that output and on the [exterior](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c04).

(Derived from Conclusion 4.4 and the definitions of [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) and [writing](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c29): a writing counts as arrival only when it falls as a [stimulus](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c30) on a [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04); the content of a writing is determined by the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) up to that time, which includes arrivals from the [exterior](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c04), and is not determined by this [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) alone)

<note>

*Treating distant relatives as "our family" does not make their news reach you more easily. Whether it arrives still depends on the route and on conditions outside.*

</note>

Calibration: when a typhoon closes the harbor, townspeople who draw their fishing-village relatives into the [we](concepts.md#c04) of their family still cannot get the catch delivered.

### 8.4 Distinctions About Things Do Not Rewrite Their Arrival

**Conclusion 8.4**: a [social person](concepts.md#c03) classing some thing (hereafter this thing) as mine, yours, ours, or yours (plural) does not make the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of this thing, or physical changes related to it, directly [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at anyone; whether they arrive is still judged by the definition of arrival. What output the social person then makes about this thing is not uniquely decided by this [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18).

(Derived from the [belonging distinction of things](concepts.md#c07) (3.6), the [distinction margin](concepts.md#c08) (3.7), the definition of [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), and Conclusions 5.2 and 8.3, and the [application assumption](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c50) and the [law of the black box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25))

<note>

*Even if you class that distant batch of goods as "ours", it will not ship itself to you because of it. Whether a thing arrives still depends on the existing physical conditions; what you do afterwards cannot be derived from this drawing alone either.*

</note>

## 9. Boundary Shift

### 9.1 Boundary Shift

**Conclusion 9.1**: [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) whose content is a classification, and other outputs, if they arrive at others, and the others are [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), take part in their [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) others let fall may [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) again at the original outputter and at third parties (Conclusions 5.3, 5.7, 6.3). This recursion may make the [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) or the [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) differ between [check windows](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c03); the researcher records this difference as a [boundary shift](concepts.md#c11). The structure of a [boundary shift](concepts.md#c11) in the record can be described by output, for example: differential outputs on the two sides reinforce each other; differential outputs of many turn from compatible to incompatible, and the inside outside difference fades; the outputs a participant receives change from those of one side to those of the other; the distance between the claimed boundary and the inside outside difference lengthens or shortens. Along a stated dimension, these structures can be described by [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37), [divergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c38), [stasis](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c52), [taiji](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c39), and [merging](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c41). Whether a boundary shift appears, in which direction, and when, cannot be derived.

(Derived from the definitions of [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31), [coupling](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36), [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37), [divergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c38), [stasis](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c52), [taiji](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c39), [merging](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c41), and [check window](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c03), the definitions of [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) (3.8) and [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) (3.9), Conclusions 5.3, 5.7, 6.3, and 7.10, and the [law of the black box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25): if [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) loops back through [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), it forms a loop; a loop's course is judged by output; inside outside differences and claimed boundaries are both drawn from output; [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) does not fix the result)

Notes:
1. A [boundary shift](concepts.md#c11) is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) the researcher makes by comparing two [check windows](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c03); the same stretch of [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) under another [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) can be recorded as shifting or as not shifting (3.8 Note 1).
2. This conclusion only describes the structure of a [boundary shift](concepts.md#c11) in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) and does not predict direction. If polarization makes the [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) or the [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) in the record differ between [check windows](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c03), the researcher can record it as one kind (see Conclusion 9.4).
3. Changes in [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) and the [boundary shift](concepts.md#c11) in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) need not be in step (Conclusion 7.10).

<note>

*A boundary does not stay put once drawn. If what one person says and does reaches others, their responses may come back and reach bystanders too; round after round, the boundary may move. Comparing records from two periods, the researcher can see how it moves: the two sides grow ever more opposed; differential treatment that was uniform becomes each doing their own; someone treated as an outsider comes to be treated as one of their own; the boundary said and the boundary acted drift apart or come closer. Whether it moves, which way, and when, cannot be said.*

</note>

Calibration: a new daughter-in-law is at first treated as a guest in food and lodging and, years later, as family: the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) a participant receives change from one side to the other; after two companies merge, their two practices toward "our own people" gradually merge into one; the company rules forbid moonlighting, at first no one moonlights, later everyone does, and the rules are unchanged: the distance between the [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) and the [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) lengthens. (Signpost: Wimmer's strategies of ethnic boundary making, such as expansion, contraction, inversion, and blurring. Where they meet: boundaries move, in several recognizable forms. Where they part: they attribute the movement to actors' strategies; this paper describes only the structure of the movement in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06), and strategies belong to [illusory constructs](concepts.md#c01).)

### 9.2 Output Toward a Plural You Reaches Those Drawn In

**Conclusion 9.2**: when a [social person](concepts.md#c03)'s [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) made according to some [plural you](concepts.md#c05) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) drawn in, what arrives is output, not [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); if those drawn in make a distinction of it, that is their own distinction.

(Derived from the definitions of [plural you](concepts.md#c05), [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), and [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), and Conclusion 5.2)

<note>

*When you treat a group as "you (plural)" in your mind, they cannot touch that; what they can touch is what you did and said to them.*

</note>

### 9.3 Plural You May Become Material for the Other Side's We

## 10. No Shared We

### 10.1 A We Does Not Belong to Two People

**Conclusion 10.1**: a [we](concepts.md#c04) belongs only to the [social person](concepts.md#c03) who uses it; there is no we that belongs to two social persons at once. Whether two social persons' we agree is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02); even if they agree, whether this can be confirmed is also not guaranteed: neither side's distinction [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the other side, nor do the two sides' distinctions arrive at the researcher; what arrives is only [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) (Conclusion 5.2); and from output alone it cannot be derived that the two sides' [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) agree.

(Derived from the definition of [we](concepts.md#c04), Conclusions 4.3, 4.6, and 5.2, and the definitions of distinction, [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), impression, and [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02): drawing we is one [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of a [social person](concepts.md#c03); the same material does not imply the same distinction; A's view of how B draws is a distinction A makes with B as object, which may include [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) or view attribution)

Notes:
1. The first half follows directly from the definition; the second half says that even if the two sides' [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) happen to agree, whether this can be confirmed is also [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02); this does not exclude that, in a concrete case, further constraints make confirmation hold (definition of not guaranteed).
2. This non-sharing also applies to [belonging distinctions of things](concepts.md#c07); two people using the same name "ours" still does not mean there is one [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) made jointly by both (see Conclusion 10.7).

<note>

*"We" is always only a construct one person uses, and drawing it is that person's own drawing; there is no kind shared by two people. Whether what you drew and what I drew are the same may not be confirmable, because all anyone sees is what the other did and said.*

</note>

### 10.2 Coordination Needs Only Compatible Output

**Conclusion 10.2**: the continuation of an [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) does not require participants' [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) to agree; [resonance](../paper_2ccbf179_58cf_4851_bd72_5fa886e4b2c3/concepts.md#c09) and [taiji](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c39) are judged by the structure of [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), and [mismatch](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c08) by whether adjacent output sequences are compatible; none of the three is judged by participants' distinctions.

(Derived from the definitions of [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09), [persistence criterion](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c01), [resonance](../paper_2ccbf179_58cf_4851_bd72_5fa886e4b2c3/concepts.md#c09), [taiji](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c39), and [mismatch](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c08), and Conclusion 7.1: an economic mode is judged by the persistence criterion the researcher states, which concerns only [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10); resonance takes arrival as a condition and is judged by whether output patterns follow on compatibly; in a taiji each side's output arrives at the other within [coupling](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36), and [divergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c38) is judged by participants' output patterns; mismatch is judged by whether adjacent output sequences are compatible)

<note>

*People can carry things on together without the same drawings in mind; what they do only needs to fit.*

</note>

Calibration: (Signpost: Parsons's and Luhmann's double contingency. Where they meet: neither side can see through the other, and each one's action depends on its estimate of the other. Where they part: Parsons resolves it with shared value norms, Luhmann with communication systems; this paper needs no shared view at all: the continuation of the [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) does not require participants' [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) to agree.)

### 10.3 Asymmetry

**Conclusion 10.3**: A drawing B into [we](concepts.md#c04) while B does not draw A into we, this asymmetry can persist while both sides' [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) are compatible; A's view of how B draws can change through [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) newly [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at A, or through A's own later [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); the changed view is still a distinction A makes with B as object, which may include [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) or [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), and not a reading of B's distinction.

(Derived from Conclusions 10.1 and 10.2 and the definitions of [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), and [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45): the material of a distinction is only the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has already [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it, so for this distinction of A to take in new content from B, something new must arrive at A)

Note: when B has said "we" and the like to A as an [avowal](concepts.md#c06), by Conclusion 6.2 it does not imply that B draws so; if B's later [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at A and are classed by A as inconsistent with this avowal, they are one piece of material for A to change its view; A may also change its view through other material or its own later [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18).

<note>

*You treat him as one of your own, while he treats you only as a colleague; this can last a long time without anyone noticing, as long as things keep getting done. Only when something reaches you, such as being left out of a meeting, may you change your mind; and the changed mind is still only your guess about him.*

</note>

Calibration: a manager usually says "we are one family"; on the day the layoff list is published: "I thought I was one of us." Judas was among the twelve; before he handed Jesus over, the others in the same [we](concepts.md#c04) saw only his [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28).

### 10.4 Taking We as Already There

**Conclusion 10.4**: [we](concepts.md#c04) is an [illusory construct](concepts.md#c01); taking a drawn we as a we already there, waiting to be confirmed, is [illusion](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c14), such as taking "I am a citizen of some country" as innate, or taking a name as a shared we. This paper does not further classify which kind of illusion it is.

(Derived from the definition of [we](concepts.md#c04) (3.3), Conclusions 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, and 10.1, and the definition of [illusion](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c14): what one acts on is what [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself with [lossiness](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c13), not [what is seen](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c27) itself; taking a we one has drawn as already there is taking one's own [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) as the thing distinguished itself)

Notes:
1. Taking an [avowal](concepts.md#c06) one hears as the other's [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18), such as taking "I love this country" as how the other truly feels, is a kind of [world illusion](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c15) (Conclusion 6.2).
2. A [social person](concepts.md#c03) has [second-order capacity](concepts.md#c02) and can see that [we](concepts.md#c04) is drawn by itself; but being able to see is not seeing. Seeing is itself a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) and may become a new layer: "I have seen through it: we is only something I drew" (definition of [second-order distinction](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c01); [wall of the blind spot](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c09): distinctions about oneself [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at oneself and become part of oneself).
3. Guessing how others draw is itself a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) with others as object (Conclusion 10.1); it may include [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) (an estimate of their [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06)) and may include view attribution (classing an output that arrives at oneself as the other's distinction); its material is only the history that has arrived at oneself, and the material of impression is of three kinds: others' outputs that arrive at oneself, the stretch one overlaps with the other, and the [coupling](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36) the other is in (definition of impression); an [avowal](concepts.md#c06) is only one kind of material, not a reading of distinction (3.5 Note 2, Conclusion 6.2). Taking this guess as the other's distinction is also a kind of [world illusion](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c15): the other's distinction has material and [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) (Conclusion 4.2) but is not uniquely derived from material (Conclusion 4.3); this guess is not a reading, and its correctness is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02). If the other makes a distinction, that is its distinction about the world; guessing it is one more layer of distinction one makes oneself, and what is guessed is the distinction one attributes to the other, which the other may not actually have made (definition of [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02): it may be mistaken; definition of impression). When newly [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) does not fit this guess, society may seem jarring.

<note>

*Taking "we" as something innate and shared by all is illusion. Saying "I was born a citizen of some country" and saying "our party is of one mind" are both such illusions. A social person can see this, but does not necessarily look; and having seen it, "I have seen through it" may become a new illusion.*

*Society often feels "jarring" perhaps because we use our own logic to guess how others draw. How others draw is their view of the world; your guess of their view is again your view, and they may not actually see it that way. You can only guess from what they say and do, and what they say is not necessarily their inner drawing, so you can guess roughly, with no guarantee of being right.*

</note>

Calibration: "No factions in the party."

### 10.5 We and the Economic Mode Each Continue

**Conclusion 10.5**: an [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) continuing or breaking under its [persistence criterion](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c01) does not imply that participants will later still draw [we](concepts.md#c04) of the same kind or make [belonging distinctions of things](concepts.md#c07) of the same kind; even if participants later make [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of the same kind again, it does not imply that the original economic mode still continues. Neither implies the other.

(Derived from the definition of [criterion break](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c10) in the Pattern paper, 3.3 Note 7, 3.6 Note 5, and Conclusion 10.2: an [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) is judged by a criterion of [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) stated by the researcher; whether a [social person](concepts.md#c03) again makes a similar [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) is not in that criterion)

<note>

*That the company still exists does not mean anyone will later still draw it as "we"; that the company folded does not prevent someone from later making such a drawing again. The persistence of an economic mode and people's distinctions are not the same thing.*

*Author's consideration: this subsection keeps the relation of non-implication; it sets up no separate criterion for "the persistence of the same we".*

</note>

Calibration (assuming each person's [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) are as described; they cannot be derived from visible speech alone):

- The mode breaks; someone later again draws a [we](concepts.md#c04) of the same kind.
- The mode breaks; someone later no longer draws a [we](concepts.md#c04) of the same kind.

### 10.6 A Common World and a Shared We

Note (not a conclusion): why everyone lives in the same world is answered by Wo Dynamics: the [universe](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c02) and [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) are there to begin with, and what [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at each person is a different part of the same universe, discounted. This paper deals with a layer above: without a shared [we](concepts.md#c04), the [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) still continues.

Calibration: (Signpost: Yogācāra's common seeds and the simile of lamps. Where they meet: the *Cheng Weishi Lun* says that the receptacle world each person transforms is different, yet they resemble one another and occupy no different place, "like the light of many lamps, each pervading and seeming one". Where they part: Yogācāra explains the resemblance of the world by common seeds; in this paper the common world is the [given](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c03) [universe](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c02), and the simile of lamps falls on the layer of [we](concepts.md#c04): each person's we shines its own, seems one, and never is one.)

### 10.7 No Shared "Ours"

**Conclusion 10.7**: A classing some thing (hereafter this thing) as "ours", and B also classing this thing as "ours", does not imply that A and B make the same or one [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) about this thing, still less that the [we](concepts.md#c04) the two people draw agree. A's and B's different distinctions about the same thing can coexist while their [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) are compatible.

(Derived from the [belonging distinction of things](concepts.md#c07) (3.6), Conclusions 4.6, 5.7, 10.1, and 10.2: [belonging distinctions](concepts.md#c07) are each in the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) of the one who makes them; the same [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) does not guarantee the same [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); the continuation of a mode does not require distinctions to agree)

Notes:
1. "Ours" is an [illusory construct](concepts.md#c01) used one-sidedly, not a shared property of the thing; claiming "this is ours" is visible [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), and not evidence of one and the same [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) in each person.
2. Even if the name "ours" recurs through the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of many, the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) holds only the transmission of the name and outputs; there is no [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) shared across many people.
3. This conclusion does not discuss ownership, who may use the thing, or who may require the other to adopt a classification.

<note>

*Both people saying "this is ours" does not mean the "we" in their eyes is the same, still less that "this thing" in their eyes falls on the same side. What can agree may be only the words spoken and the things done.*

</note>

### 11.2 Left for Later

- **Authority, hierarchy, legal persons**: this paper has only each person's own [we](concepts.md#c04) and ours, and no mechanism by which one side's [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) must be followed by another; an [avowal](concepts.md#c06) or a classificatory statement about things is [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), which, if it [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at a receiver that is a [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), takes part in its [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) and may lead it not to take some [path](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07); but this paper has no mechanism by which these words necessarily make some [path](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07) infeasible for those who do not draw accordingly (Conclusions 5.5, 6.5, 7.7). These three require additional mechanisms and are left for later research.
- **Other concepts**: ownership, institutions, price, value, money, allocation, and markets are left for later research. "Ours" in this paper is only an [illusory construct](concepts.md#c01) used about things; this paper does not treat it as a synonym of ownership, right of use, or rules of allocation.

<note>

*"This is ours" is only something a person said; it cannot directly imply that others may not use the thing. Why a superior can tell you what to do, or why a company can sign contracts, this paper cannot address either; they require additional mechanisms of external constraint. Ownership, institutions, money, and markets are all left for later.*

</note>

## 12. Cases

This whole section is calibration and serves as the source of no derivation. When the cases apply conclusions to specific people, they hold, by Section 2, under the [configuration assumption](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c01). The two cases are taken from different times and places: the first looks at how a [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) written into statute is rewritten and moves; the second looks at how [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) around a stretch of sea leave an [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06), and how a boundary not originally written down was later written into law. Expressions in the cases such as "in order to", "wanting to", or "identifying with" are distinctions recorders or later writers make with the people concerned as object, which may include [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02), not readings of [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18).

<note>

*Both cases in this section are calibration throughout, not evidence. The first looks at how a boundary written in statute is rewritten and moves; the second looks at how an inside and an outside of a stretch of sea appear in the record, and how they were later written into law. Where "in order to", "wanting to", or "identifying with" appear in the cases, they are what recorders or later writers said, not anyone's inner drawing.*

</note>

### 12.1 California's Foreign Miners' Tax (1850 to 1870)

Note on data: the sources read are the Wikipedia article "Foreign Miners' Tax Act of 1850", the U.S. National Park Service page on Chinese immigration to California, a digital exhibit page of Santa Clara University, and the EBSCO entry on foreign miner taxes in the Gold Rush, all as passages extracted by a tool, not original documents. The sources disagree on the 1852 tax: one says 3 dollars a month, another 4 dollars a month.

<note>

*Note on data: the sources are pages from Wikipedia, the National Park Service, Santa Clara University, and EBSCO, extracted by a tool, not originals. Sources give different amounts for the 1852 tax.*

</note>

1. **[Cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23)**: the range is California's mining districts, with the year as the [check window](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c03). The [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) are the state legislature, the governor, tax collectors, miners from various places, and the [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) in which the statute is written. The [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of the same kind the researcher compares are miners' paying the tax, being driven off, and leaving the mines.
2. **Initial state**: from 1848, the gold-mining structure in the mining districts recurred year by year, with miners from many places; there was no tax by origin in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06).
3. **New [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10)**: on 13 April 1850, Governor Burnett signed an act levying 20 dollars a month on miners who were not U.S. citizens and had not become citizens under the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo; California Native Americans were exempt.
4. **[Claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) and [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09)**:
   - The statute, written into a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26), proposes a boundary, from which the researcher can draw a [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10): citizens inside, non-citizens outside.
   - After protests by Irish, English, Canadian, and German miners, the statute was rewritten to exempt "free white persons" and miners who could become U.S. citizens.
   - On 19 May 1850, about four thousand miners, mostly Mexican and Peruvian, protested in Sonora; a militia of about five hundred tax collectors and Anglo miners drove them off.
   - Many Mexican and Chinese miners left the mines; revenue fell far short of the expected two hundred thousand dollars.
5. **[Boundary shift](concepts.md#c11)**:
   - In 1851, under Governor McDougall, the act was repealed.
   - In 1852 a foreign miners' license tax was enacted, with the amount raised gradually afterwards; the National Park Service page records it as aimed at Chinese immigrants.
   - By the time the law was voided in 1870, Chinese miners had paid more than five million dollars in taxes, about a quarter of the state's revenue in that period.
6. **What it shows**:
   - A statute is a classification written into a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26); from then on it is in [lock-in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) at every step along with the [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26); rewriting, repeal, and re-enactment are each another [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that arrives at it, and before it [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), the earlier one has already taken part in the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) of that period (3.9 Note 3, definition of [wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16)).
   - Paying the tax, being driven off, and leaving the mines are [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) comparable in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06); a researcher comparing them grouped by origin obtains an [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) (3.8). Under another grouping the result can differ.
   - The sources place the rewriting of the statute after the protests of European miners: if the protests [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the legislators, they took part in their [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); later [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) changed the [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10). Whether the protests had an effect thereby, and how much, cannot be derived (Conclusion 5.6).
   - Between 1850 and 1870 the [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) was rewritten, repealed, and re-enacted, and the miners the tax was recorded by the sources as aimed at also shifted from Mexicans and others to Chinese; the researcher can record this as a [boundary shift](concepts.md#c11) (Conclusion 9.1).
   - Miners leaving the mines may be the tax removing an [antecedent](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c08) of the [path](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07) of lawful mining, or the tax taking part in their [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) so that they did not take a path that was still feasible; which, cannot be derived (Conclusion 5.5).
7. **What is in the record and what cannot be derived**:
   - In the record: what the statute said, and when it was rewritten, repealed, and re-enacted; the events of protest and pursuit; revenue falling short of expectations; the total and share paid by Chinese miners.
   - Cannot be derived: whom the legislators, tax collectors, or Anglo miners drew as [we](concepts.md#c04) and whom as [plural you](concepts.md#c05); why any miner left; what the population of the mining districts would have been without this tax.

<note>

*First case (foreign miners' tax): in 1850 California levied 20 dollars a month on non-citizen miners; after European miners protested, the statute was changed to exempt free white persons; Mexican and Peruvian miners who protested were driven off by a militia, and many left. Repealed in 1851 and re-enacted in 1852, it later fell mainly on the Chinese, who had paid more than five million dollars by 1870. What is in the record is the statute and the events; what cannot be derived is whom anyone counted as their own in their mind, and why anyone left.*

</note>

### 12.2 Maine's Lobster Waters (Twentieth Century)

Note on data: the source read is Waring and Acheson, "Evidence of cultural group selection in territorial lobstering in Maine" (2017), as passages extracted by a tool, not the original; its account of harbor gangs and their waters mostly cites Acheson's works of 1975, 1988, and 2003.

<note>

*Note on data: the source is a 2017 journal article, extracted by a tool; its account of harbor gangs mostly cites the anthropologist Acheson's earlier fieldwork.*

</note>

1. **[Cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23)**: the range is lobster fishing along the Maine coast, with the year as the [check window](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c03). The [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) are the fishermen of each harbor, the state legislature, and the marine resources authority; the sea is the [natural environment](../paper_2ccbf179_58cf_4851_bd72_5fa886e4b2c3/concepts.md#c16). The [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of the same kind the researcher compares are setting traps in some stretch of sea and what is done to others' traps.
2. **Initial state**: state law requires a state license to set lobster traps; damaging others' traps is illegal.
3. **Structures that appear**:
   - The fishermen of each harbor form a harbor gang and hold an unofficial stretch of sea bounded by coves, ledges, river mouths, and buoys, usually under a hundred square miles; a gang has as few as eight boats and rarely more than fifty.
   - When people from other harbors come in to set traps, they first receive verbal warnings, then their traps are destroyed; those whose traps are destroyed often retaliate.
   - Legally, a license is enough to fish; unofficially, one must also be accepted by the harbor gang.
   - Mainland harbors mostly defend strongly near the harbor and more loosely farther out; island harbors and a few mainland harbors defend the whole area to its outer edge, with clear boundaries.
4. **[Claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) and [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09)**:
   - State law proposes a boundary, from which the researcher can draw a [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10): license holders inside, those without outside.
   - The harbor gang's warnings are [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) whose content is a classification, proposing another boundary, from which the researcher can draw another [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10): people of this harbor inside, people of other harbors outside. This boundary was at first not written into a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26).
   - The [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06): setting traps in the same stretch of sea, the traps of people of this harbor stay, while those of people from other harbors are destroyed.
5. **[Boundary shift](concepts.md#c11)**:
   - Around 1960, fishermen of upper Muscongus Bay began setting traps at the mouth of the bay in fall and winter, and cut traps back and forth with the fishermen of New Harbor and Round Pond for years; the upper-bay fishermen gradually moved into the outer bay, which became a mixed fishing area.
   - In 1984, the fishermen of Swan's Island petitioned the marine resources authority to turn their formerly unofficial waters into an official conservation zone, excluding mainland fishermen and setting a limit of 475 traps. In 1998 Monhegan also obtained special status by legislation, barring boats from other harbors from fishing within two miles of the island.
   - The lobster zone management law of 1995 drew seven legal zones along the coast, with zone councils elected by license holders.
6. **What it shows**:
   - If some fisherman classes this stretch of sea as ours, that is a [belonging distinction of things](concepts.md#c07) belonging to him and not in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06); the record holds only [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) such as warnings, trap setting, and trap cutting. Which trap cutting comes from such a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) cannot be derived (3.6, Conclusions 4.6, 7.9).
   - State law says all license holders may fish, yet in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06) the traps of people from other harbors in this stretch of sea are destroyed: the state law's [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) and the [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) in the record diverge (Conclusion 7.11).
   - Trap cutting may have removed an [antecedent](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c08) of the [path](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07) of fishermen from other harbors in this stretch of sea, or may only have taken part in their [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) so that they no longer came in; which, cannot be derived. Warnings may also only take part in the receiver's [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) without removing an [antecedent](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c08); which effect they have cannot be derived (Conclusion 5.5).
   - Even if the harbor gang's fishermen class the waters as ours, it does not imply who is entitled to use them: under state law, license holders from other harbors can still fish there, and trap cutting is illegal (Conclusion 7.7).
   - The warnings and trap cutting of several fishermen make up the same [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06), without requiring them to share a [we](concepts.md#c04) or their [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) to agree (Conclusions 7.10, 10.1).
   - The expansion of upper Muscongus Bay is a [boundary shift](concepts.md#c11) of the inside outside difference in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06); the conservation zones and the zone management law wrote into a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) a boundary previously proposed only by warnings, and the new [claimed boundary](concepts.md#c10) moved toward the existing [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09); the inside outside difference after the legislation is not in the data (Conclusion 9.1).
7. **What is in the record and what cannot be derived**:
   - In the record: what the state law said; the rough extent and defense of the harbor gangs' waters; the warnings and trap cutting suffered by people from other harbors; the expansion around 1960; the legal changes of 1984, 1995, and 1998.
   - Cannot be derived: whom any fisherman drew as [we](concepts.md#c04), and which stretch of sea he classed as ours; from which [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) some trap cutting came; why the harbor gangs formed here; whether each person's distinctions changed after the conservation zones were set up.

<note>

*Second case (lobster waters): Maine law requires only a license, and any license holder may fish; but the fishermen of each harbor hold their own waters, and outsiders who come in are first warned, then have their traps cut. So the record shows an inside and an outside, different from what the law says. Later some islands won official conservation zones, and the state passed a zone management law, so the written boundary moved closer to the boundary acted out. What is in the record is the law, the warnings, the trap cutting, and the expansion; what cannot be derived is who counts which stretch of sea as their own in their mind.*

</note>

## 13. General Proposition

**General proposition**: the standing and continuation of an [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) do not need any participant to draw [we](concepts.md#c04). Once a [social person](concepts.md#c03) has [second-order capacity](concepts.md#c02), it can, on the material of an existing economic mode that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it, draw we and [plural you](concepts.md#c05) about people, and make [belonging distinctions](concepts.md#c07) about things, using [illusory constructs](concepts.md#c01) such as mine, yours, ours, and yours (plural), so that the mode shows, for this social person, a [distinction margin](concepts.md#c08) drawn by itself. The [margin](concepts.md#c08) does not belong to the mode itself, does not require other social persons to agree, and implies no relation of ownership, use, disposal, exclusion, or allocation.

Once made, these [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) become the [social person](concepts.md#c03)'s own [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and take part in its later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) it lets fall toward people and things on the two sides of the [margin](concepts.md#c08) may differ; if these outputs [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at others, they may change others' feasible [paths](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c07); if the others are social persons, they may also become material for their later distinctions and outputs. In this way a [distinction margin](concepts.md#c08), through output, may make an [inside outside difference](concepts.md#c09) appear in an [economic mode](../paper_d244789a_9261_4a0d_a98a_feb8db56b511/concepts.md#c09) in the record: outputs of the same kind differ within a group of participants and across it. Each person's unshared distinctions drawing [we](concepts.md#c04) and [belonging distinctions of things](concepts.md#c07), if each person's outputs arrive at one another, may also shape each other through output; this recursion may produce a [boundary shift](concepts.md#c11) in the [record](../paper_81952268_47b1_4d8c_9605_270c42646693/concepts.md#c06). Differential outputs of several social persons may make up the same inside outside difference in the record, without requiring them to share a we. Spoken and written classifications, if registered in the record, are material from which the researcher can draw [claimed boundaries](concepts.md#c10), while an inside outside difference in the record, if any, is drawn from outputs of the same kind that the researcher has registered, later outputs possibly among them if registered and classed as the same kind, so the two can diverge. An inside outside difference in the record does not imply that participants have [second-order capacity](concepts.md#c02). As for who makes which distinction, how distinctions change, whether an inside outside difference appears, where it falls, from which distinction some difference really comes, and whether the mode therefore continues or breaks, this paper derives none of these uniquely.

(A summary of Conclusions 5.3, 5.7, 7.1, 7.3, 7.6, 7.7, 7.8, 7.9, 7.10, 7.11, 7.12, 10.1, 10.2, and 10.7; the parts that depend on Conclusion 4.3 hold under the [application assumption](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c50).)

<note>

*Without "we", the outputs of people and things can still connect and form economic modes. With "we" and "ours", each person can draw their own edges of people and edges of things on the existing mode. These edges need not be the same, and they give no one the automatic right to use things; but what a person does to those inside and outside may differ, and if these acts reach others, they may change the paths others can take and may become material for others' next distinctions. So, in the researcher's record, an economic mode may show an inside and an outside. Conversely, seeing an inside and an outside, one cannot conclude that anyone has drawn "we".*

</note>
