# Wo Dynamics

> The method of this paper is backward derivation: starting from four laws, it asks what the world must at least be like (Section 0). Beneath every given, definition, and corollary in this paper there is a "Formal" block. The formal blocks are for computational checking, not for reading.

## Framework Declaration

After the [singularity](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c01) comes the [formal layer](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c02): [records](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c17) [accumulate](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c30) one by one, and nothing is ever [selected](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32).

Outside the [formal layer](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c02) there is one [intervention](concepts.md#c01). The [intervention](concepts.md#c01) lies within no [formal-layer moment](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c04); it cannot be formalized, and this paper does not write about it.

The [universe](concepts.md#c02) is the one in which there is [collapse](concepts.md#c08). It differs from the [formal layer](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c02) in exactly one respect: in the [universe](concepts.md#c02) there is [selection](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32). Seen from inside, there is only one [universe](concepts.md#c02). Before and after, as this paper uses them, hold only within the [universe](concepts.md#c02).

Only two things in the [universe](concepts.md#c02) are not determined by the past: the [initial state of the universe](concepts.md#c14), and the outcome of each [collapse](concepts.md#c08). Everything else, including every [writing](concepts.md#c29), is determined by the [history](concepts.md#c13) up to that point.

```text
Formal:
Not determined by the past = { s₀ } ∪ { bₜ : t ≥ 1 }
Everything else: wₜ₊₁ = ω(hₜ) (Given 4); pₜ₊₁ = G(hₜ⁺) (Given 5)
Intervention: not formalized
```

This paper sets out to answer a question about an epistemic limit: why I cannot truly understand you, and cannot truly understand even myself. The reasons given by common sense and by most social-scientific theories are contingent: not enough information, different languages, different backgrounds, different experiences. All of these reasons imply that with enough information and good enough communication, understanding is possible in principle. This paper claims that the limit is structural, not a shortfall: even with complete information, it cannot be done. It has three layers. Your next choice is not yet settled before it is [selected](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32), and not even you know it (the [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25)). Your knowledge of yourself always lags one step behind, and you have no complete self in hand to give over (the [Law of Attrition](concepts.md#c26)). I can understand you only through the you that has [arrived](concepts.md#c10) at me: what [arrives](concepts.md#c10) is always how you have already been, and your next instant is still a black box; and my act of understanding you, once it [arrives](concepts.md#c10) at you, may itself change you (Corollary 12). The argument for the [Law of Attrition](concepts.md#c26) uses only "understanding is an act, and it enters [history](concepts.md#c13) only once it has happened"; it does not use [collapse](concepts.md#c08). Common sense cannot tell contingent limits from structural ones; hence this paper derives backward.

This paper does not solve the measurement problem of quantum mechanics. Its method is backward derivation: the four laws, the [Law of Flux](concepts.md#c24), the [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25), the [Law of Attrition](concepts.md#c26), and the [Law of Non-Restoration](concepts.md#c28), are the iron laws this paper acknowledges; what it asks is what the world must at least be like if these four laws are true. The conditions derived backward are listed as givens, each marked with the law that requires it; the givens are then used to derive the four laws forward again, as a check. The fewer the required givens, the better. Whatever no law requires, and is not kept in order to fit known observations, but this paper nevertheless keeps, is listed as a [framework commitment](concepts.md#c03), separately from the givens.

## 0. Backward Derivation

The following four are the starting point of this paper and are acknowledged as iron laws. Stating them requires none of this paper's givens. Real-world cases serve only for calibration, not as proof.

- [Law of Flux](concepts.md#c24): the [history](concepts.md#c13) of a [wo](concepts.md#c16) is never exactly the same. You cannot step into the same river twice.
- [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25): you will never be certain of a person's next choice; before the choice, not even they know it.
- [Law of Attrition](concepts.md#c26): I will never truly and completely know myself.
- [Law of Non-Restoration](concepts.md#c28): after death, there is no coming back to life.

These four speak of "never". Experience can only give "no exception so far"; what backward derivation seeks is the kind of world in which "never" holds.

In this paper, [death](concepts.md#c23) is a mark; the content of the [Law of Non-Restoration](concepts.md#c28) is: that stretch of [history](concepts.md#c13) will not happen again.

"I cannot truly understand you" is answered jointly by the [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25), the [Law of Attrition](concepts.md#c26), and Corollary 12. Corollary 12 and the [Law of Attrition](concepts.md#c26) share one structure, one directed at oneself, the other at another; hence it is not listed as a separate law.

### The World the Four Laws Require

- The [Law of Flux](concepts.md#c24) requires: there is only one [history](concepts.md#c13), and it is only ever appended to; at every [moment](concepts.md#c09), something new enters [history](concepts.md#c13). This corresponds to the definitions of [moment](concepts.md#c09) and [history](concepts.md#c13).
- The [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25) requires: there is [selection](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32) not determined by [history](concepts.md#c13) (Given 2); and [weights](concepts.md#c06) are determined by [history](concepts.md#c13) (Given 5), because the second half of this law is "what can be derived is the weights".
- The [Law of Attrition](concepts.md#c26) requires: [distinction](concepts.md#c18) is an act, and it is written into [history](concepts.md#c13) only once it has happened (Given 4, Given 7). Its argument does not use [collapse](concepts.md#c08); it touches Given 2 only indirectly, because what it speaks of is the [self](concepts.md#c19), and a [self](concepts.md#c19) is a [wo](concepts.md#c16).
- The [Law of Non-Restoration](concepts.md#c28) requires: in its first layer, that [history](concepts.md#c13) is unique; in its second layer, through the [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25), Given 2; the derivation of the second layer additionally uses Given 4.
- The four laws jointly require: a total order, without which "the [history](concepts.md#c13) up to that point" is undefined ("only one collapse at a time" in Given 1); and a starting point of [history](concepts.md#c13) (Given 3).

Here "requires" means: in this paper's derivation, the law uses the condition; remove it, and the law can no longer be derived in this paper. Whether the condition is necessary in every possible world, this paper does not assert.

### Framework Commitments

The following are required by none of the laws, yet this paper keeps them:

- That the total order and the [selections](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32) are carried by one [observer](concepts.md#c07) outside the [universe](concepts.md#c02) (the rest of Given 1). The four laws require only that there be a total order and [selection](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32) not determined by [history](concepts.md#c13); they do not require who carries it.
- [Connection](concepts.md#c22) (Given 6). Corollaries 1 and 8 depend on it.
- That the [initial state of the universe](concepts.md#c14) carries the [records](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c17) [accumulated](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c30) in the [formal layer](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c02) (the second half of Given 3).
- The [intervention](concepts.md#c01).

There is one further condition that the four laws do not require either; it is kept only so as not to contradict known observations: the [generating function](concepts.md#c15) permits [no-signaling](concepts.md#c31) (the second half of Given 5).

```text
Formal: backward table ("⇐": used in the derivation; necessity not proved)
Law of Flux              ⇐ history unique and append-only
Law of the Black Box     ⇐ G2a, Given 5 (G takes only history), total order
Law of Attrition         ⇐ Given 4, Given 7, total order; scope touches G2a via "self"
Law of Non-Restoration   ⇐ history unique (layer 1); G2a, G2b, Given 4 (layer 2)
Jointly                  ⇐ total order (one collapse at a time), h₀
Commitments              = { O outside the universe carries the total order and collapse;
                             Given 6; s₀ carries formal-layer records; intervention }
Kept to fit observation  = { no-signaling }
```

## 1. Presentations and Branches

- Any part of the [universe](concepts.md#c02) is a [presentation](concepts.md#c04).
- A [branch](concepts.md#c05) is each possible outcome that a [collapse](concepts.md#c08) may [select](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32).
- A [weight](concepts.md#c06) is how heavy each [branch](concepts.md#c05) is.

```text
Formal: notation
t ∈ {1,2,3,…}        order of moments; total order (Given 1)
B                    set of branches (countable)
Δ(B)                 weight distributions on B (non-negative, summing to one)
s₀                   initial state of the universe
wₜ                   the t-th writing
bₜ ∈ B               the branch selected by the t-th collapse
h₀ = (s₀)
hₜ⁺ = (hₜ, wₜ₊₁)      history after writing, before collapse
hₜ₊₁ = (hₜ⁺, bₜ₊₁)
H, H⁺                sets of all hₜ, all hₜ⁺
W                    set of all writings
presentation x:
  πₓ : B → Bₓ ∪ {⊥}   reads a collapse outcome as an outcome of x; ⊥: not falling on x
  σₓ : W → Wₓ ∪ {⊥}   reads a writing as a stimulus to x; ⊥: not arriving at x
f#p                  pushforward of p by f: (f#p)(y) = Σ_{b : f(b)=y} p(b)
```

## 2. Givens: Observer and Collapse

The following cannot be derived. Each is marked with the law from which it is derived backward, or as a framework commitment (see Section 0).

### Given 1: The Observer

The [observer](concepts.md#c07) is an identifier with no content: it does not compute, does not feel, does not suffer, and has no point of view. It is not in the [universe](concepts.md#c02); it is not a [presentation](concepts.md#c04), nor a [self](concepts.md#c19).

There is only one [observer](concepts.md#c07). Its sole function is [collapse](concepts.md#c08). The [observer](concepts.md#c07) collapses only once at a time. This paper does not discuss its nature.

Backward: the total order given by "only one collapse at a time" is required jointly by the four laws. That an identifier outside the [universe](concepts.md#c02) carries this total order and [collapse](concepts.md#c08) is a [framework commitment](concepts.md#c03).

```text
Formal:
O unique; O is in no hₜ; O is no πₓ
Collapses occur one at a time: the order of moments = {1,2,3,…}, total, no two simultaneous
```

### Given 2: Collapse

[Collapse](concepts.md#c08), in some fixed basis, [selects](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32) one [branch](concepts.md#c05) according to the [weights](concepts.md#c06). [Branches](concepts.md#c05) not [selected](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32) do not remain in the [universe](concepts.md#c02).

[Collapse](concepts.md#c08) [selects](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32) only among [branches](concepts.md#c05) of non-zero [weight](concepts.md#c06). If the next moment of a [presentation](concepts.md#c04) has only one outcome of non-zero [weight](concepts.md#c06), its outcome comes from constraint, not from [collapse](concepts.md#c08).

Given the [history](concepts.md#c13) up to that point, the outcome of a [collapse](concepts.md#c08) is [selected](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32) according to the [weights](concepts.md#c06); nothing in [history](concepts.md#c13) can say anything more than the [weights](concepts.md#c06). Everything in the [universe](concepts.md#c02) is in [history](concepts.md#c13), so before a [collapse](concepts.md#c08), nothing in the [universe](concepts.md#c02) determines its outcome.

Backward: required by the [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25); the second layer of the [Law of Non-Restoration](concepts.md#c28) also requires it, through the [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25).

```text
Formal:
G2a  P(bₜ₊₁ = b | hₜ⁺, Z) = G(hₜ⁺)(b)
     Z: any quantity fixed before the (t+1)-th collapse, or not in the universe
        (e.g. e(t+1), see Given 6)
G2b  G(hₜ⁺)(b) = 0 ⇒ bₜ₊₁ ≠ b
G2c  hₜ₊₁ = (hₜ⁺, bₜ₊₁): only the selected is appended; b ≠ bₜ₊₁ enters no hₜ'
Basis: the choice of B is not further formalized
```

## 3. Moments and History

- A [moment](concepts.md#c09) is one [collapse](concepts.md#c08). The [moments](concepts.md#c09) of the [universe](concepts.md#c02) are ordered by the sequence in which the [observer](concepts.md#c07) collapses; this is an order, not a point of view. Without [collapse](concepts.md#c08), there is no [moment](concepts.md#c09).
- A [writing](concepts.md#c29) that falls on a [presentation](concepts.md#c04) as a [stimulus](concepts.md#c30) [arrives](concepts.md#c10) at it. When a [collapse](concepts.md#c08) [involves](concepts.md#c11) a [presentation](concepts.md#c04), its outcome [arrives](concepts.md#c10) at that [presentation](concepts.md#c04); if the outcome does not fall on it, "this time it did not happen to it" is also something that [arrives](concepts.md#c10) at it. The [initial state of the universe](concepts.md#c14) [arrives](concepts.md#c10) at every [presentation](concepts.md#c04).
- A [collapse](concepts.md#c08) [involves](concepts.md#c11) a [presentation](concepts.md#c04) when the outcome of that [collapse](concepts.md#c08) may fall on it; in that case its [weight](concepts.md#c06) depends on the [history](concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](concepts.md#c10) at it. [Involvement](concepts.md#c11) does not depend on how many [branches](concepts.md#c05) there are.
- The [moments](concepts.md#c09) of a [presentation](concepts.md#c04) are the [collapses](concepts.md#c08) that [involve](concepts.md#c11) it. Its next [moment](concepts.md#c09) is the next [collapse](concepts.md#c08) that [involves](concepts.md#c11) it. Between two of them, there is no [moment](concepts.md#c09) for it.
- [Lock-in](concepts.md#c12) is when the next [moment](concepts.md#c09) of a [presentation](concepts.md#c04) has only one outcome of non-zero [weight](concepts.md#c06): once each [branch](concepts.md#c05) is read as an outcome for it, only one remains. [Lock-in](concepts.md#c12) is a constraint and has nothing to do with [collapse](concepts.md#c08). Having no [moment](concepts.md#c09) is not [lock-in](concepts.md#c12): [lock-in](concepts.md#c12) is being [involved](concepts.md#c11) yet having only one outcome.
- [History](concepts.md#c13) begins with the [initial state of the universe](concepts.md#c14), followed by every [writing](concepts.md#c29) and the outcome of every [collapse](concepts.md#c08) in the [universe](concepts.md#c02), arranged by [moment](concepts.md#c09). The [universe](concepts.md#c02) has only one [history](concepts.md#c13).

```text
Formal:
pₜ = G(hₜ₋₁⁺)                        weights of the t-th collapse
pₜˣ = πₓ#pₜ                          weights of x at the t-th collapse
arrived(x, t) = {0} ∪ { position i : position i is a writing w, σₓ(w) ≠ ⊥ }
                    ∪ { position of the i-th collapse outcome : i ∈ T(x) },
                    positions not beyond the length of hₜ⁺
                    (the outcome of a collapse involving x always arrives at x,
                     including πₓ(bᵢ) = ⊥)
involves(x, t) ⇔ pₜˣ(⊥) < 1          the outcome falls on x with positive weight
T(x) = { t : involves(x, t) }        the moments of x
next(x, t) = min { t' > t : t' ∈ T(x) }
lockedin(x, t) ⇔ involves(x, t) ∧ supp(pₜˣ) = {y}, y ≠ ⊥
t ∉ T(x) ⇒ ¬lockedin(x, t)           having no moment is not lock-in
History unique: for t < t', hₜ is a prefix of hₜ'
Note: if pₜˣ(⊥) = 1, the weight of x is always the point mass on ⊥ and depends on nothing;
      so "its weight depends on the history that arrived at it" is meaningful only when
      the outcome may fall on it, and is not a separate condition
```

### Given 3: The Initial State of the Universe

The [initial state of the universe](concepts.md#c14) is everything already there before the first [collapse](concepts.md#c08); it carries the [records](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c17) [accumulated](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c30) in the [formal layer](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c02).

Backward: a starting point of [history](concepts.md#c13) is required jointly by the four laws. That the [initial state of the universe](concepts.md#c14) carries the [records](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c17) of the [formal layer](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c02) is a [framework commitment](concepts.md#c03).

```text
Formal: h₀ = (s₀); the content of s₀ is not analyzed
```

### Given 4: Writing

Before every [collapse](concepts.md#c08) there is first a [writing](concepts.md#c29). The content of the [writing](concepts.md#c29) is entirely determined by the [history](concepts.md#c13) up to that point.

Backward: required by the [Law of Attrition](concepts.md#c26); the derivation of the second layer of the [Law of Non-Restoration](concepts.md#c28) also uses it.

```text
Formal: ∀t ≥ 0, wₜ₊₁ = ω(hₜ), ω : H → W a function
```

### Given 5: The Generating Function

The [generating function](concepts.md#c15) maps the [history](concepts.md#c13) up to that point to the [weights](concepts.md#c06) of the [branches](concepts.md#c05) of the next [moment](concepts.md#c09). It takes only the [history](concepts.md#c13) up to that point. Its internals are not analyzed.

The [weight](concepts.md#c06) of a [presentation](concepts.md#c04) is the part of the [universe](concepts.md#c02)'s [weight](concepts.md#c06) that falls on it.

The [generating function](concepts.md#c15) satisfies [no-signaling](concepts.md#c31): for two [collapses](concepts.md#c08) that are far apart and cannot influence each other in time, the [weights](concepts.md#c06) of their respective outcomes are the same whichever the [observer](concepts.md#c07) performs first.

It follows, and need not be posited separately, that the [generating function](concepts.md#c15) does not vary with the granularity of description. Whether a [presentation](concepts.md#c04) is described as one node or as a network, all [presentations](concepts.md#c04)' [weights](concepts.md#c06) are split off from the same [universe](concepts.md#c02) [weight](concepts.md#c06), and the results necessarily agree.

Backward: "[weights](concepts.md#c06) are determined only by [history](concepts.md#c13)" is required by the [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25). [No-signaling](concepts.md#c31) is required by none of the four laws; it is kept only so as not to contradict known observations (see Section 0).

```text
Formal:
G : H⁺ → Δ(B); pₜ₊₁ = G(hₜ⁺); the internals of G are not analyzed
weight of x = πₓ#pₜ (see Section 3)
No-signaling: let the (t+1)-th and (t+2)-th collapses fall only on x and y respectively,
  with x, y far apart (primitive relation). Swapping the order of the two leaves
  the weight distributions of πₓ(b) and π_y(b') each unchanged.
Granularity (corollary): if πₓ = f ∘ π_y, then pₜˣ = f#pₜʸ.
  Proof: (f ∘ π_y)#p = f#(π_y#p). ∎
```

## 4. Wo and Self

- A [wo](concepts.md#c16) is a [presentation](concepts.md#c04) such that all the [history](concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](concepts.md#c10) at it, including every [stimulus](concepts.md#c30) it has received and the outcome of every [collapse](concepts.md#c08) that has [involved](concepts.md#c11) it, all the way back to the [initial state of the universe](concepts.md#c14), takes part in the allocation of its [weights](concepts.md#c06) for the next [moment](concepts.md#c09): if any one of these had been different, the influence would pass down [history](concepts.md#c13) and its present [weights](concepts.md#c06) would differ. This influence need not be findable; whether it can be found is a matter of [distinction](concepts.md#c18). And at least at some [moments](concepts.md#c09) it is not [locked in](concepts.md#c12). A thing with [history](concepts.md#c13) whose [history](concepts.md#c13) does not take part is not a [wo](concepts.md#c16); a thing whose [history](concepts.md#c13) takes part but which is [locked in](concepts.md#c12) at every step is not a [wo](concepts.md#c16) either. This participation may gradually fade; once it has faded, it is no longer a [wo](concepts.md#c16). If from some moment on it is [locked in](concepts.md#c12) at every step, it is no longer a [wo](concepts.md#c16) either. The [history](concepts.md#c13) on which its [weights](concepts.md#c06) for the next [moment](concepts.md#c09) depend is its [own segment](concepts.md#c17); that segment is determined by dependence, not by any [distinction](concepts.md#c18). Hence every choice made by a [collapse](concepts.md#c08) that [involves](concepts.md#c11) it belongs to its [own segment](concepts.md#c17). Its [own segment](concepts.md#c17) is part of the one [history](concepts.md#c13), not another [history](concepts.md#c13); the segments of different [wos](concepts.md#c16) may overlap. A [wo](concepts.md#c16) has no fixed core: what it is, is what has [arrived](concepts.md#c10) at it and how these take part in its [weights](concepts.md#c06); this is its relation to the [universe](concepts.md#c02). Cells, neurons, immune systems, evolving species, people, artificial intelligence in conversation, organizations, and civilizations can all be [wos](concepts.md#c16); radioactive atoms, dice, stones, abacuses, and thermostats are not: radioactive atoms and dice have more than one outcome, but their [weights](concepts.md#c06) are not affected by the past; stones, abacuses, and thermostats are affected by the past, but are [locked in](concepts.md#c12) at every step. Whether a [wo](concepts.md#c16) is a [wo](concepts.md#c16) does not depend on its substrate, nor on any [distinction](concepts.md#c18): a [presentation](concepts.md#c04) that meets the conditions above is a [wo](concepts.md#c16), whether or not any [self](concepts.md#c19) sees it that way. For the method of judgment and further examples, see Section 5.

```text
Formal:
s₀ is position 0
do(i = v): replace position i of hₜ⁺ by v; later writings are recomputed by ω,
           later outcomes are reselected by G
L(·): distribution
Time convention: the t in wo(x, t) means after the t-th collapse and before the (t+1)-th.
  The judgment uses hₜ⁺ and pₜ₊₁ = G(hₜ⁺), i.e. the weights of the next moment;
  V2 additionally looks at moment t+1 and later.
  Note the one-step index offset from pₜ = G(hₜ₋₁⁺) in Section 3.
  For t = 0 the judgment uses h₀⁺ = (s₀, w₁) and p₁; both are fixed before the
  first collapse; wo(x, 0) is additionally judged on the actual history after them.
seg(x, t) = { i : ∃ v, L(pₜ₊₁ˣ | do(i = v)) ≠ L(pₜ₊₁ˣ | do(i = actual value)) }
                                   the own segment of x at t; influence passes down history
V1(x, t) ⇔ arrived(x, t) ⊆ seg(x, t)             everything that arrived takes part
V2(x, t) ⇔ ∃ t' ≥ t: t'+1 ∈ T(x) ∧ ¬lockedin(x, t'+1)
wo(x, t) ⇔ V1(x, t) ∧ V2(x, t)
Hence: { position of the i-th collapse outcome : i ∈ T(x), i ≤ t } ⊆ arrived(x, t) ⊆ seg(x, t)
       i.e. the choices made by collapses involving x all belong to the segment of x
seg(x, t) ∩ seg(y, t) may be non-empty
wo(x, t) is fixed by G, ω, πₓ, σₓ and the actual history; no d's claim about
  "whether x is a wo" affects this judgment
"Findable" is not in the definition: the existence of seg(x, t) does not require
  any δ to compute it
Radioactive atoms, dice: ¬V1 (what arrives does not change the weights)
Stones, abacuses, thermostats: ¬V2 (locked in at every step)
Relation of wo x to the universe = (arrived(x, ·), seg(x, ·)); x has no other determination
```

- [Distinction](concepts.md#c18) is an act of a [wo](concepts.md#c16): drawing a line, taking certain [presentations](concepts.md#c04) as one [individual](concepts.md#c20), taking a certain stretch as the past, judging the [identity](concepts.md#c21) of [wos](concepts.md#c16) at different [moments](concepts.md#c09), as one and the same or as two different ones. This act leaves one [writing](concepts.md#c29) in [history](concepts.md#c13), whose content is determined by the [history](concepts.md#c13) up to that point. The content of a [distinction](concepts.md#c18) changes a way of seeing; it does not rewrite what is seen. The act of [distinction](concepts.md#c18) itself enters [history](concepts.md#c13) by Given 7. A [distinction](concepts.md#c18) enters [history](concepts.md#c13) only once it has happened, so its material is only the [history](concepts.md#c13) before it that has already [arrived](concepts.md#c10) at it, and it is not itself among that material. When speaking of the next [moment](concepts.md#c09) of a [wo](concepts.md#c16), whether it is a cell or a body is also a [distinction](concepts.md#c18); by Given 5, this does not change the [weights](concepts.md#c06).

```text
Formal:
A distinction by x at t: dₓ,ₜ = δₓ(the part of hₜ arrived at x), δₓ a function (by Given 4, δₓ is part of ω)
dₓ,ₜ is part of wₜ₊₁
Material = the part of hₜ that has arrived at x; dₓ,ₜ ∉ hₜ (wₜ₊₁ is not in hₜ)
No rewriting: no d changes any existing hₜ (history is only appended to)
The content of distinctions (individual, past, identity, death, causation) is not formalized;
  only "it is a writing" is formalized
```

- A [self](concepts.md#c19) is a [wo](concepts.md#c16) that defines itself by [distinction](concepts.md#c18). Who is a [self](concepts.md#c19) is defined by that [wo](concepts.md#c16) itself; uttering the sound "I" does not count; only self-definition counts.
- An [individual](concepts.md#c20) is a [presentation](concepts.md#c04) marked off by some [self](concepts.md#c19) through [distinction](concepts.md#c18). The same stretch of the [universe](concepts.md#c02) may be [distinguished](concepts.md#c18) as one [individual](concepts.md#c20) or as billions of [individuals](concepts.md#c20); both hold.
- [Identity](concepts.md#c21) is a kind of [distinction](concepts.md#c18): whether a [wo](concepts.md#c16) at one [moment](concepts.md#c09) and a [wo](concepts.md#c16) at another [moment](concepts.md#c09) are the same one is decided by the [self](concepts.md#c19) that makes the [distinction](concepts.md#c18). Whether something is a [wo](concepts.md#c16) is not a [distinction](concepts.md#c18); whether it is the same one is a question of [identity](concepts.md#c21), and that is a [distinction](concepts.md#c18).
- [Connection](concepts.md#c22) is the link between a [self](concepts.md#c19) and the [observer](concepts.md#c07); [connection](concepts.md#c22) is not in the [universe](concepts.md#c02). Whether a [self](concepts.md#c19) is [connected](concepts.md#c22) is not decided by any of its [distinctions](concepts.md#c18), including self-definition.
- [Death](concepts.md#c23) is a mark that some [self](concepts.md#c19) places on a [wo](concepts.md#c16).

```text
Formal:
self(x, t) ⇔ wo(x, t) ∧ ∃ s ≤ t: the content of dₓ,ₛ takes x itself as object
             (content not formalized)
Individual, identity, death: each is the content of some d, not formalized
e(t): the self through which the t-th collapse takes place; e is in no hₜ, and does not
      affect any distribution of weights or outcomes (G2a)
  Note: the value of e may be constrained by history (e.g. when there is only one self
        in the universe); this conflicts with no given
connected(x) ⇔ x ∈ range of e; connected(x) is not determined by any dₓ
```

### Given 6: Connection

[Collapse](concepts.md#c08) takes place through a [self](concepts.md#c19) [connected](concepts.md#c22) to the [observer](concepts.md#c07). Which [selves](concepts.md#c19) are [connected](concepts.md#c22), this paper does not assert.

Backward: required by none of the laws; a [framework commitment](concepts.md#c03).

```text
Formal: ∀t ≥ 1, e(t) exists, and self(e(t), t−1). The range of e is not asserted.
```

### Given 7: Distinction Enters History

Every [distinction](concepts.md#c18) of a [wo](concepts.md#c16) is [written](concepts.md#c29) as a [stimulus](concepts.md#c30) before that [wo](concepts.md#c16)'s next [moment](concepts.md#c09), and [arrives](concepts.md#c10) at the [wo](concepts.md#c16) that made it. Which [wos](concepts.md#c16) make [distinctions](concepts.md#c18), and how many, this paper does not assert.

Backward: required by the [Law of Attrition](concepts.md#c26).

```text
Formal: if wo x makes a distinction at t, then dₓ,ₜ ∈ wₜ₊₁ ⊂ hₜ⁺, σₓ(wₜ₊₁) ≠ ⊥,
        and wₜ₊₁ precedes next(x, t).
```

## 5. Scope

The corollaries below speak only of [wos](concepts.md#c16). The [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25) speaks only of the [moments](concepts.md#c09) at which a [wo](concepts.md#c16) is not [locked in](concepts.md#c12). When a cannonball strikes a person, that instant has only one outcome and falls outside this paper.

Likewise, if every step of a language model's output is entirely determined by [history](concepts.md#c13), it is [locked in](concepts.md#c12) throughout that output and falls outside the [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25). Between two inputs it has no [moment](concepts.md#c09): no [collapse](concepts.md#c08) [involves](concepts.md#c11) it, and this is not [lock-in](concepts.md#c12).

```text
Formal:
Premise of the Law of the Black Box: ¬lockedin(x, t+1)
Cannonball: lockedin(person, t)
Language model: if πₓ#G(hₜ⁺) is a point mass for all t ∈ T(x), then lockedin(x, t);
                between two inputs, t ∉ T(x)
```

### Judging Whether a Presentation Is a Wo

The judgment asks only two things:

- First question: if anything that [arrived](concepts.md#c10) at it had been different at the time, would its [weights](concepts.md#c06) for the next [moment](concepts.md#c09) differ accordingly?
- Second question: from this instant on, does it still have at least one [moment](concepts.md#c09) at which it has more than one possible outcome at the next instant?

Only if both answers are yes is it a [wo](concepts.md#c16). If only one is yes, it is not:

- [History](concepts.md#c13) takes part, but it is [locked in](concepts.md#c12) at every step: stones, abacuses, thermostats, crystal growth, river channels. The past leaves traces, but the next step is uniquely determined by present conditions.
- More than one outcome, but [history](concepts.md#c13) does not take part: radioactive atoms, dice. Every time is like the first; the past leaves no influence.

Four points in judging:

- What is judged is a way of cutting. The same stretch of the [universe](concepts.md#c02), cut differently, can give a different answer; once the cut is fixed, the answer is not decided by any [distinction](concepts.md#c18).
- Names do not count. A hurricane is physically a vortex of air, but if at that scale it is [locked in](concepts.md#c12), or its [weights](concepts.md#c06) do not depend on its whole [history](concepts.md#c13), it is not a [wo](concepts.md#c16) in this paper's sense.
- Whether something is a [wo](concepts.md#c16) can change. If the participation gradually fades, or from some instant on it is [locked in](concepts.md#c12) at every step, it is no longer a [wo](concepts.md#c16).
- The second question may have no answer at the physical level (see Section 9). At the levels of biology and the social sciences, if the next instant of a [presentation](concepts.md#c04) cannot be uniquely derived from the [history](concepts.md#c13) available at that level, this paper's applications assume that it is not [locked in](concepts.md#c12). This is an assumption of application, not a theorem.

```text
Formal:
First question = V1(x, t)
Second question = ∃ t' ≥ t: t'+1 ∈ T(x) ∧ ¬lockedin(x, t'+1)
wo(x, t) ⇔ First question ∧ Second question; the second question is V2(x, t)
V1 ∧ locked in at every step: stones, abacuses, thermostats, crystals, river channels
¬V1 ∧ sometimes not locked in: radioactive atoms, dice
The object judged is (πₓ, σₓ); cutting differently means changing x
Assumption of application: at a given level of description, if the next instant of x
  cannot be uniquely derived from the history available at that level, applications
  assume ¬lockedin. This assumption is not among the givens; the correctness of the
  corollaries does not depend on it; it is used only when applying them to a concrete x.
```

### Range of Application

The following cases serve only for calibration, not as proof.

- Life: cells, neurons, immune systems, viral populations, tumors, gut flora, ecosystems, evolving species. Tumors are the typical case: every treatment [arrives](concepts.md#c10) at the tumor and changes its later [weights](concepts.md#c06); drug resistance comes from this. Species that arrive first change who can enter later, so the same environment, having gone through different [histories](concepts.md#c13), grows into different ecosystems.
- Mind and relationships: people, memories, a relationship, a conversation. Every act of recall rewrites the memory itself; recall also [arrives](concepts.md#c10) at it. The relationship between two people is itself a [wo](concepts.md#c16): every sentence stays in it and takes part in the next.
- Society: organizations, languages, case law, markets, scientific fields, religious traditions, cities, civilizations. In case law, every judgment becomes a precedent and takes part in later judgments, while the next judgment cannot be uniquely derived. Every use [arrives](concepts.md#c10) at a language, and meanings drift through use.
- Artificial systems: artificial intelligence in conversation, continually learning algorithms, the training process of a model. Every click by a user changes what a recommender system will push next. Whether they pass the second question depends on whether their randomness is truly undetermined.

## 6. Corollaries

### Corollary 1: Wos Exist

By definition, the [universe](concepts.md#c02) has [collapse](concepts.md#c08); by Given 6, [collapse](concepts.md#c08) takes place through a [self](concepts.md#c19) [connected](concepts.md#c22) to the [observer](concepts.md#c07). Hence there is at least one [self](concepts.md#c19) in the [universe](concepts.md#c02). By definition, a [self](concepts.md#c19) is a [wo](concepts.md#c16). Hence there is at least one [wo](concepts.md#c16) in the [universe](concepts.md#c02).

The [self](concepts.md#c19) through which the first [collapse](concepts.md#c08) takes place is already a [self](concepts.md#c19) before the first [collapse](concepts.md#c08). Whether it is one is decided, as for any [wo](concepts.md#c16), by the actual [history](concepts.md#c13) (the [generating function](concepts.md#c15) and the rule of [writing](concepts.md#c29) are fixed): the [initial state of the universe](concepts.md#c14), the first [writing](concepts.md#c29), which the [initial state of the universe](concepts.md#c14) determines, and the [history](concepts.md#c13) after them, all of which lie within the [universe](concepts.md#c02). So no separate given is needed.

Conversely, without [wos](concepts.md#c16) there is no [collapse](concepts.md#c08).

```text
Formal:
The 1st collapse exists ⇒ e(1) exists ∧ self(e(1), 0) ⇒ wo(e(1), 0).
self(e(1), 0) is judged on h₀⁺ = (s₀, w₁) and the actual history after it
  (V2 looks at moment 1 and later), with w₁ = ω(s₀); all of these lie within history,
  so no separate given is needed. ∎
Converse: no wo ⇒ no self ⇒ e has no value ⇒ no collapse. ∎
```

### Corollary 2: The Formal Layer Has No Moments

In the [formal layer](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c02) nothing is ever [selected](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32) (see the Framework Declaration), so there is no [collapse](concepts.md#c08). By the definition of [moment](concepts.md#c09), in this paper's sense the [formal layer](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c02) has no [moments](concepts.md#c09). Before and after, as this paper uses them, hold only within the [universe](concepts.md#c02) (see the Framework Declaration); hence the [moments](concepts.md#c09) of the [universe](concepts.md#c02) and [formal-layer moments](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c04) cannot be compared. A [moment](concepts.md#c09) is a [collapse](concepts.md#c08), so whatever any [self](concepts.md#c19) sees within a [moment](concepts.md#c09) is already the outcome of [collapse](concepts.md#c08): the [records](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c17) [accumulated](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c30) in the [formal layer](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c02) are indeed still in the [initial state of the universe](concepts.md#c14), but when a [self](concepts.md#c19) sees them within a [moment](concepts.md#c09), they are already [records](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c17) after [collapse](concepts.md#c08). Before the first [collapse](concepts.md#c08), there is already a [self](concepts.md#c19) [distinguishing](concepts.md#c18) itself, but there is not yet any [moment](concepts.md#c09); what it knew then, this paper does not assert: this can be neither verified nor falsified.

```text
Formal: no bₜ in the formal layer ⇒ no t.
        The material of any δₓ is contained in some hₜ, t ≥ 0, which contains no formal-layer moment;
        in distinctions with t ≥ 1, the records carried by s₀ are read after the first collapse;
        what a distinction at t = 0 knows is not asserted. ∎
```

### Corollary 3: The Law of Flux

By the definitions of [moment](concepts.md#c09) and [history](concepts.md#c13), at every [moment](concepts.md#c09) [history](concepts.md#c13) gains the outcome of one more [collapse](concepts.md#c08). A [wo](concepts.md#c16) is in [history](concepts.md#c13), so at no [moment](concepts.md#c09) is a [wo](concepts.md#c16) in the same [history](concepts.md#c13) as at the previous instant. Freezing a [presentation](concepts.md#c04) does not stop the [history](concepts.md#c13) it is in.

The same holds for a [wo](concepts.md#c16)'s own history: by the definition of [arrival](concepts.md#c10), the outcome of every [collapse](concepts.md#c08) that [involves](concepts.md#c11) it [arrives](concepts.md#c10) at it. So at each of its [moments](concepts.md#c09), the [history](concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](concepts.md#c10) at it gains one more item, and is never exactly the same as at its previous [moment](concepts.md#c09). Between two of its [moments](concepts.md#c09), there is no [moment](concepts.md#c09) for it.

This is the form the [Law of Flux](concepts.md#c24) takes in this paper's model (the [Law of Flux in the Model](concepts.md#c33)): the [history](concepts.md#c13) in which a [wo](concepts.md#c16) lies is always changing.

```text
Formal:
Universe: |hₜ₊₁| > |hₜ| ⇒ hₜ₊₁ ≠ hₜ. ∎ (immediate from the definitions)
Wo: t ∈ T(x) ⇒ position of the t-th collapse outcome ∈ arrived(x, t), and ∉ arrived(x, t−1)
        (arrived(x, t−1) contains only positions in hₜ₋₁⁺, not bₜ)
        hence arrived(x, t−1) ⊊ arrived(x, t). ∎
```

### Corollary 4: The Law of the Black Box

Suppose a [wo](concepts.md#c16) is not [locked in](concepts.md#c12) at some [moment](concepts.md#c09); then its next [moment](concepts.md#c09) has at least two outcomes of non-zero [weight](concepts.md#c06). Every derivation any [wo](concepts.md#c16) makes before this [collapse](concepts.md#c08) lies within the [history](concepts.md#c13) up to that point; by Given 2, nothing in [history](concepts.md#c13) can say anything more than the [weights](concepts.md#c06). Hence at that [moment](concepts.md#c09), no [wo](concepts.md#c16) in the [universe](concepts.md#c02) can uniquely derive its next [moment](concepts.md#c09), including itself. The chance of any prediction being right is at most the [weight](concepts.md#c06) of its heaviest outcome.

What can be derived is the [weights](concepts.md#c06): by Given 5, [weights](concepts.md#c06) are determined by [history](concepts.md#c13). What a person will eat for dinner may have extremely concentrated [weights](concepts.md#c06); but concentrated is not unique.

This is the form the [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25) takes in this paper's model (the [Law of the Black Box in the Model](concepts.md#c34)).

```text
Formal:
Suppose wo(x, t), t+1 ∈ T(x), ¬lockedin(x, t+1); then |supp(pₜ₊₁ˣ)| ≥ 2.
Any prior derivation is some f : H⁺ → Bₓ ∪ {⊥} (including those computed by x's own δₓ).
By G2a: P(πₓ(bₜ₊₁) = f(hₜ⁺) | hₜ⁺) = pₜ₊₁ˣ(f(hₜ⁺)) ≤ max pₜ₊₁ˣ < 1. ∎
```

### Corollary 5: The Law of Attrition

A [self](concepts.md#c19)'s [distinction](concepts.md#c18) of itself is one [distinction](concepts.md#c18). By the definition of [distinction](concepts.md#c18), its material is only the [history](concepts.md#c13) before it that has already [arrived](concepts.md#c10) at it, and it is not itself among that material; by Given 7, it is [written](concepts.md#c29) into [history](concepts.md#c13) only before the next [moment](concepts.md#c09). So once this [distinction](concepts.md#c18) has been [written](concepts.md#c29), there is a part of [history](concepts.md#c13) that this [self](concepts.md#c19) has not yet [distinguished](concepts.md#c18): the very act of [distinguishing](concepts.md#c18) itself. At the next [moment](concepts.md#c09) it enters [history](concepts.md#c13), and at the same time a new [distinction](concepts.md#c18) has not yet entered.

A [self](concepts.md#c19)'s [distinction](concepts.md#c18) of itself always lags one step behind. Even at a [moment](concepts.md#c09) when it makes no [distinction](concepts.md#c18), by the definition of [self](concepts.md#c19) it has [distinguished](concepts.md#c18) itself at least once, and its most recent [distinction](concepts.md#c18) is itself not among the material of any [distinction](concepts.md#c18) it has made. So no [self](concepts.md#c19), at any [moment](concepts.md#c09), can finish [distinguishing](concepts.md#c18) the whole of [history](concepts.md#c13). By Given 7, these [distinctions](concepts.md#c18) [arrive](concepts.md#c10) at it; by the definition of [wo](concepts.md#c16), whatever [arrives](concepts.md#c10) at it takes part in its [weights](concepts.md#c06), so they belong to its [own segment](concepts.md#c17). Then it cannot finish [distinguishing](concepts.md#c18) even its [own segment](concepts.md#c17).

This is the form the [Law of Attrition](concepts.md#c26) takes in this paper's model (the [Law of Attrition in the Model](concepts.md#c35)).

```text
Formal:
For a self x that makes a distinction, and t: the material of all distinctions by x up to t ⊆ hₜ;
  dₓ,ₜ ∉ hₜ; dₓ,ₜ ∈ hₜ⁺.
Hence hₜ⁺ always contains a part (dₓ,ₜ) not in the material of any distinction x has made. ∎
By Given 7, the position of dₓ,ₜ ∈ arrived(x, t); by V1, ∈ seg(x, t).
Hence seg(x, t) also always has a part not distinguished. ∎
If x makes no distinction at t: take x's most recent distinction dₓ,ₛ, s < t (exists by the
  definition of self); no distinction whose material ends at or before s contains it, and
  x makes no new distinction between s and t, so it is not in the material of any
  distinction made. ∎
```

### Corollary 6: The Law of Non-Restoration

To [restore](concepts.md#c27) a [wo](concepts.md#c16) is to make its segment of [history](concepts.md#c13) happen once again. This has two layers.

The first layer is position. By the definition of [moment](concepts.md#c09), each [moment](concepts.md#c09) happens only once; each position in [history](concepts.md#c13) also happens only once. Any stretch that looks the same happens at a different position in [history](concepts.md#c13).

The second layer is content. By the definition of [universe](concepts.md#c02), there is [collapse](concepts.md#c08) in the [universe](concepts.md#c02). By the [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25), which one a [collapse](concepts.md#c08) [selects](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32) when not [locked in](concepts.md#c12) cannot be uniquely derived. Hence for any stretch of [history](concepts.md#c13) that contains choices made by [collapse](concepts.md#c08), no [wo](concepts.md#c16) can guarantee that, under the same absence of [lock-in](concepts.md#c12), the same choices will be made again. Copying the outcomes of that time can yield an exactly identical sequence of outcomes; but every step of the copying is [locked in](concepts.md#c12): that is copying, not doing it over. A stretch determined entirely by constraint falls outside the second layer.

By the definition of [wo](concepts.md#c16), among the [moments](concepts.md#c09) at which it is a [wo](concepts.md#c16), at least some are not [locked in](concepts.md#c12), and every choice made by a [collapse](concepts.md#c08) that [involves](concepts.md#c11) it belongs to its [own segment](concepts.md#c17). So once a [wo](concepts.md#c16) has gone through a [moment](concepts.md#c09) at which it is not [locked in](concepts.md#c12), its segment contains such a choice, and the second layer applies; for one that has not yet, such as a [wo](concepts.md#c16) before the first [collapse](concepts.md#c08), the first layer suffices. Hence within the [universe](concepts.md#c02), no [wo](concepts.md#c16) can [restore](concepts.md#c27) a [wo](concepts.md#c16), including itself.

The [identity](concepts.md#c21) of some later [wo](concepts.md#c16) with the original one is a [distinction](concepts.md#c18). Freezing, resuscitation, saving and reloading, splitting, and merging are all the one [history](concepts.md#c13) continuing onward, not [restoration](concepts.md#c27); to call what continues onward the original one is something no one can refute. [Death](concepts.md#c23) is only a mark: where it is placed is decided by the [self](concepts.md#c19) that places it; but wherever it is placed, that stretch of [history](concepts.md#c13) will not happen again, and no [wo](concepts.md#c16) can guarantee that the choices it made then, when not [locked in](concepts.md#c12), will be made over.

Likewise, [moments](concepts.md#c09) are ordered in one direction only.

This is the form the [Law of Non-Restoration](concepts.md#c28) takes in this paper's model (the [Law of Non-Restoration in the Model](concepts.md#c36)).

```text
Formal:
Layer 1: each t occurs only once in {1,2,…}; positions do not repeat. ∎ (by definition)
Layer 2: the universe has collapse (by the definition of universe).
  Original stretch bᵢ..bᵢ₊ₙ₋₁; later stretch bₖ..bₖ₊ₙ₋₁, whose l-th item corresponds to bᵢ₊ₗ.
  Aₗ: the first l items of the later stretch all equal those of the original (A₀ is certain).
  Given hₖ₋₁⁺ and Aₗ, by Given 4, hₖ₊ₗ₋₁⁺ is uniquely determined; call it h⁽ˡ⁾.
  P(whole stretch equal | hₖ₋₁⁺) = Π_{l=0}^{n−1} P(bₖ₊ₗ = bᵢ₊ₗ | hₖ₋₁⁺, Aₗ)
                                = Π_{l=0}^{n−1} G(h⁽ˡ⁾)(bᵢ₊ₗ)     (by G2a, conditioning step by step)
  Each factor ≤ 1. If some l has G(h⁽ˡ⁾) with at least two branches of positive weight
  (not locked in under Aₗ), that factor ≤ 1 − min{ G(h⁽ˡ⁾)(b) : G(h⁽ˡ⁾)(b) > 0 } < 1,
  so the whole probability < 1. ∎
  One instance of not being locked in suffices; neither independence of the steps
  nor absence of lock-in at every step is needed.
  When only x's outcomes πₓ(b) are compared, Aₗ fixes only x's outcomes and hₖ₊ₗ₋₁⁺ is no longer
  unique; the factor becomes the conditional average under Aₗ, over hₖ₊ₗ₋₁⁺, of pˣ(corresponding
  value). If under Aₗ x is not locked in with probability 1, the factor < 1.
Copying: if the later stretch is locked in at every corresponding position, and its point
  masses are exactly bᵢ..bᵢ₊ₙ₋₁, the probability is 1; this is copying, outside layer 2
Identity: the content of some d, not formalized
Direction: t only increases. ∎
```

### Corollary 7: Interaction

By Given 7, a [self](concepts.md#c19)'s [distinction](concepts.md#c18) is [written](concepts.md#c29) into [history](concepts.md#c13). When it becomes part of another [wo](concepts.md#c16)'s segment of [history](concepts.md#c13), by the definition of [wo](concepts.md#c16), that [wo](concepts.md#c16)'s [weights](concepts.md#c06) for the next [moment](concepts.md#c09) may change accordingly. Hence a [self](concepts.md#c19) can change another; [distinguishing](concepts.md#c18) a [wo](concepts.md#c16) may itself change it.

```text
Formal: dₓ,ₜ ∈ wₜ₊₁; if the position of wₜ₊₁ ∈ seg(y, t') (t' > t),
then there is v such that L(pₜ'₊₁ʸ | do(position of wₜ₊₁ = v)) ≠ L(pₜ'₊₁ʸ | do(that position = actual value)).
∎ (by the definition of seg)
```

### Corollary 8: Connection Makes No Difference

There is only one [observer](concepts.md#c07); every [collapse](concepts.md#c08) is made by the one [observer](concepts.md#c07). By Given 1, the [observer](concepts.md#c07) is not in the [universe](concepts.md#c02); by the definition of [connection](concepts.md#c22), [connection](concepts.md#c22) is not in the [universe](concepts.md#c02) either, so [connection](concepts.md#c22) is not in [history](concepts.md#c13). By Given 5, [weights](concepts.md#c06) are determined only by [history](concepts.md#c13), so whichever [self](concepts.md#c19) a [collapse](concepts.md#c08) takes place through, the [weights](concepts.md#c06) are the same; by Given 2, outcomes are [selected](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32) only according to the [weights](concepts.md#c06), so the distribution of outcomes is the same as well. Hence which [self](concepts.md#c19) is [connected](concepts.md#c22) is not in [history](concepts.md#c13), makes no difference to any [wo](concepts.md#c16), and need not be known.

A [self](concepts.md#c19) may know that it is [connected](concepts.md#c22), for example when it is the only [self](concepts.md#c19) in the [universe](concepts.md#c02). But even if it knows, nothing is different because of it.

```text
Formal:
By G2a with Z = e(t+1): P(bₜ₊₁ | hₜ⁺, e(t+1)) = G(hₜ⁺), independent of e(t+1).
Hence for any x, the distributions of pₜ₊₁ˣ and πₓ(bₜ₊₁) do not vary with the value of e(t+1).
By induction, the distribution of any hₜ' and the output distribution of any δ do not vary with e. ∎
Not asserted: that no self can know the value of e. Asserted only: the value of e makes no difference.
```

### Corollary 9: There Is No Omniscient Wo

A [wo](concepts.md#c16) that could uniquely derive every next [moment](concepts.md#c09) not [locked in](concepts.md#c12), and could guarantee to do over every stretch of the past, would conflict with Corollaries 4 and 6. Hence there is no such [wo](concepts.md#c16) in the [universe](concepts.md#c02).

```text
Formal: by Corollary 4 (the success probability of any f < 1) and layer 2 of Corollary 6. ∎
```

### Corollary 10: No Self with a Fixed Core

By definition, a [self](concepts.md#c19) is a [wo](concepts.md#c16), and a [wo](concepts.md#c16) has no fixed core; by Given 1, the [observer](concepts.md#c07) is not a [self](concepts.md#c19) and has no content. Hence there is no [self](concepts.md#c19) with a fixed core in the [universe](concepts.md#c02).

```text
Formal: by definition; "fixed core" is not further formalized.
```

### Corollary 11: Death Is a Distinction

To take a [wo](concepts.md#c16) as dead is a [distinction](concepts.md#c18). By the definition of [death](concepts.md#c23), this mark is placed by some [self](concepts.md#c19). A [wo](concepts.md#c16) ceasing to be a [wo](concepts.md#c16) requires no [self](concepts.md#c19); [death](concepts.md#c23) does. So [death](concepts.md#c23) is not a property of the [wo](concepts.md#c16), but a [distinction](concepts.md#c18) of some [self](concepts.md#c19): it enters the [universe](concepts.md#c02) with the [distinction](concepts.md#c18).

```text
Formal:
¬wo(y, t) is fixed by G, ω, π_y, σ_y and the actual history; no d's claim about it
  affects this judgment.
"y is dead" is the content of some dₓ,ₜ, entering hₜ⁺ via wₜ₊₁. ∎
```

### Corollary 12: Incompleteness of Knowing Others

To [distinguish](concepts.md#c18) another [wo](concepts.md#c16), a [self](concepts.md#c19) can use only the [history](concepts.md#c13) that has already [arrived](concepts.md#c10) at it. The other's information must travel by physical means to [arrive](concepts.md#c10) at the [self](concepts.md#c19); that is itself an event entering [history](concepts.md#c13), and the [self](concepts.md#c19)'s [distinction](concepts.md#c18) is another event, after it. Between these two events, the other's [history](concepts.md#c13) may still grow; and when it is not [locked in](concepts.md#c12), the outcome of its next [moment](concepts.md#c09) cannot be derived beforehand (the [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25)). So even if everything about the other has [arrived](concepts.md#c10) at the [self](concepts.md#c19), as in the virtual, what the [self](concepts.md#c19) [distinguishes](concepts.md#c18) is still how the other has already been, and what it faces is still a black box. In general there is more: the other's [own segment](concepts.md#c17) contains much that [arrives](concepts.md#c10) only at the other, such as each of its [distinctions](concepts.md#c18) of itself, and nothing guarantees that these also [arrive](concepts.md#c10) at the [self](concepts.md#c19). Moreover, by Given 7 and Corollary 7, the [self](concepts.md#c19)'s [distinction](concepts.md#c18) is [written](concepts.md#c29) into [history](concepts.md#c13), and once it becomes part of the other's [own segment](concepts.md#c17), it may itself change the other's [weights](concepts.md#c06) for the next [moment](concepts.md#c09).

Hence no [self](concepts.md#c19) can guarantee that it has finished [distinguishing](concepts.md#c18) another [wo](concepts.md#c16). The [Law of Attrition](concepts.md#c26) says that I always lag one step behind myself; this corollary says that I always lag behind you as well, and your next instant is still a black box.

This is the [Incompleteness of Knowing Others](concepts.md#c32).

```text
Formal:
The material of dₓ,ₜ ⊆ the part of hₜ that has arrived at x.
The outcome of y at some moment arrives at x at a position no earlier than that moment;
  if y has a moment not locked in after it, its outcome is not in the material until it
  arrives at x, and any prior derivation succeeds with probability < 1 (Corollary 4).
Positions of seg(y, now) not arrived at x are not in the material; no given guarantees
  that there are none.
If the position of wₜ₊₁ ∈ seg(y, t'), then y's weights at t' may depend on dₓ,ₜ (Corollary 7).
Hence there is no guarantee that seg(y, now) ⊆ the material of dₓ,ₜ. ∎
```

### Corollary 13: History Loses Nothing

By definition, [death](concepts.md#c23) is a mark; by the definition of [distinction](concepts.md#c18), the content of a [distinction](concepts.md#c18) does not rewrite what is seen; by the definition of [history](concepts.md#c13), [history](concepts.md#c13) contains every [writing](concepts.md#c29) and the outcome of every [collapse](concepts.md#c08). Hence after a [wo](concepts.md#c16) has been marked with [death](concepts.md#c23), its segment remains whole in [history](concepts.md#c13). Whether what made it up is still there is a [distinction](concepts.md#c18).

The [Law of Non-Restoration](concepts.md#c28) says: it will not happen again. This corollary says: nor did it ever leave.

```text
Formal: t < t' ⇒ hₜ is a prefix of hₜ'; hence every position in seg(y, t) is still in every hₜ'. ∎
```

## 7. Example

The following is a minimal model: one [wo](concepts.md#c16), two [branches](concepts.md#c05), two [collapses](concepts.md#c08).

```text
Wos = {A}
Branches = {Left, Right}
Initial state of the universe: A's segment is Left
Given in this example: A defines itself by distinction and is connected to the observer
Writing rule of this example: each time A distinguishes itself, it lists all outcomes so far

Generating function (depending on A's segment: outcomes and A's distinction):
  weight of Left  = (number of Left in outcomes + number of Left in the latest distinction + 1)
                    / (total outcomes + items in the latest distinction + 2)
  weight of Right = (number of Right in outcomes + number of Right in the latest distinction + 1)
                    / (total outcomes + items in the latest distinction + 2)
  (outcomes include the Left in the initial state)

Moment 1: write A's distinction "my segment is Left"
          before collapse: outcomes Left ×1, total 1; latest distinction Left ×1, 1 item
          weights Left 3/4, Right 1/4; collapse selects Right
          afterwards, A distinguishes itself again: "my segment is Left, Right"
Moment 2: write A's distinction "my segment is Left, Right"
          before collapse: outcomes Left ×1, Right ×1, total 2;
                           latest distinction Left ×1, Right ×1, 2 items
          weights Left 1/2, Right 1/2; collapse selects Right
```

```text
Formal:
B = {Left, Right}; π_A = identity (no ⊥)
s₀ contains one Left
σ_A(d_A,ₜ) ≠ ⊥ (A's distinctions arrive at A)
n_b(hₜ⁺) = number of b in s₀ and b₁..bₜ; nₜ = t + 1
m_b(hₜ⁺) = number of b listed in d_A,ₜ in wₜ₊₁; mₜ = number of items in d_A,ₜ
G(hₜ⁺)(b) = (n_b + m_b + 1) / (nₜ + mₜ + 2)
ω(hₜ) = d_A,ₜ = "my segment is" followed by the outcomes in s₀ and b₁..bₜ, listed in order
ω(h₀) = d_A,0 = "my segment is Left"
ω(h₁) = d_A,1 = "my segment is Left, Right"
p₁ = (3/4, 1/4), b₁ = Right
p₂ = (1/2, 1/2), b₂ = Right
arrived(A, 0) = {s₀, d_A,0}; arrived(A, 1) = {s₀, d_A,0, b₁, d_A,1}
V1 check:
  s₀: changing it changes n
  d_A,0: changing it changes p₁, hence the distribution of b₁, hence the distribution of p₂
  b₁: changing it changes n
  d_A,1: changing it changes m
  e.g. if b₁ = Left, then d_A,1 = "my segment is Left, Left",
    p₂ = (5/6, 1/6) ≠ (1/2, 1/2)
T(A) = {1, 2}; ¬lockedin at both moments
Hence V2(A, 0) and V2(A, 1) hold;
  the example has no moment after the second collapse, so wo(A, 2) does not hold
e(1) = e(2) = A
```

**Check:**

- A is a [wo](concepts.md#c16): the [initial state of the universe](concepts.md#c14), the two [distinctions](concepts.md#c18), and the first outcome that [arrived](concepts.md#c10) at A would each, if different, make the [weights](concepts.md#c06) different; the first [distinction](concepts.md#c18) passes its influence to the second [moment](concepts.md#c09) through the probability of the first outcome ✓; both [moments](concepts.md#c09) have two [branches](concepts.md#c05) of non-zero [weight](concepts.md#c06), so A is not [locked in](concepts.md#c12) ✓
- Given 4: before every [collapse](concepts.md#c08) there is a [writing](concepts.md#c29), whose content is determined by the [history](concepts.md#c13) up to that point ✓; every [distinction](concepts.md#c18) of A lists all outcomes so far by the rule of this example, so it is defined on [histories](concepts.md#c13) other than the actual one as well ✓
- Given 7: A's [distinction](concepts.md#c18) is [written](concepts.md#c29) before its next [moment](concepts.md#c09) ✓
- Corollary 1: before the first [collapse](concepts.md#c08), A has already [distinguished](concepts.md#c18) itself; it is a [connected](concepts.md#c22) [self](concepts.md#c19), so a [wo](concepts.md#c16) exists; A is not [locked in](concepts.md#c12) at its first [moment](concepts.md#c09), so whether it is a [self](concepts.md#c19) before the first [collapse](concepts.md#c08) depends only on the [initial state of the universe](concepts.md#c14) ✓
- [Law of Flux](concepts.md#c24): at every [moment](concepts.md#c09), [history](concepts.md#c13) gains the outcome of one more [collapse](concepts.md#c08) ✓
- [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25): at the first [moment](concepts.md#c09) the [weight](concepts.md#c06) of Left is 3/4, yet [collapse](concepts.md#c08) [selects](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32) Right. [History](concepts.md#c13) determined the [weights](concepts.md#c06), not the outcome ✓
- [Law of Attrition](concepts.md#c26): the material of A's [distinction](concepts.md#c18) after the first [collapse](concepts.md#c08) does not include that [distinction](concepts.md#c18) itself; it enters [history](concepts.md#c13) only at the second [moment](concepts.md#c09), and takes part in the second [moment](concepts.md#c09)'s [weights](concepts.md#c06) ✓
- [Law of Non-Restoration](concepts.md#c28): even if Right is [selected](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32) twice more later, it happens at a different position in [history](concepts.md#c13); and as long as those two are not [locked in](concepts.md#c12), no [wo](concepts.md#c16) can guarantee beforehand that it will be so ✓

## 8. Notes

The cases in the following notes serve only for calibration, not as proof.

Note: the [observer](concepts.md#c07) is not a [self](concepts.md#c19). What is called a [self](concepts.md#c19) is a [wo](concepts.md#c16).

Note: "everyone is a [self](concepts.md#c19)" does not mean there are many [observers](concepts.md#c07). There is only one [observer](concepts.md#c07).

Note: the time a [wo](concepts.md#c16) measures by clock follows physics; a [moment](concepts.md#c09) is the order in which the [observer](concepts.md#c07) collapses, and no [wo](concepts.md#c16) can measure it. Relativity removed absolute simultaneity within the [universe](concepts.md#c02); this paper's order is not within the [universe](concepts.md#c02), so the two do not conflict. For the same pair of far-apart events, [wos](concepts.md#c16) standing in different places may receive them in opposite orders; this is no conflict, because a [wo](concepts.md#c16) sees only what has [arrived](concepts.md#c10) at it, and the order in which the [observer](concepts.md#c07) [collapses](concepts.md#c08) lies outside what any [wo](concepts.md#c16) can see. By the [no-signaling](concepts.md#c31) of Given 5, the [weights](concepts.md#c06) of the outcome that [arrives](concepts.md#c10) on one side do not change with the order either; so which of two far-apart [collapses](concepts.md#c08) came first cannot be detected from either side through the distribution of its own outcomes.

Note: this paper does not speak of "causation". In [history](concepts.md#c13) there is only dependence: [weights](concepts.md#c06) depend on the [history](concepts.md#c13) up to that point. To say that something causes something is a [distinction](concepts.md#c18).

Note: [distinguishing](concepts.md#c18) you as one person or as billions of cells does not rewrite your [history](concepts.md#c13).

Note: [death](concepts.md#c23) is a description, not a physical condition.

Note: in matter, there are no two [selves](concepts.md#c19) with exactly the same content: the no-cloning theorem of quantum mechanics says that an unknown quantum state cannot be completely copied. In the virtual, a state can be completely copied; this paper makes no guarantee. Either way, what is copied happens at a different position in [history](concepts.md#c13).

Note: when a company splits into two, the segments of the two before the split are one and the same stretch of [history](concepts.md#c13), not duplicates; when they merge back, the merged segment contains the paths each walked. Cell division is the same. All of these are the one [history](concepts.md#c13) continuing onward, not [restoration](concepts.md#c27); to call the merged company the original one is a [distinction](concepts.md#c18).

Note: a [weight](concepts.md#c06) is how heavy a [branch](concepts.md#c05) is, not an internal parameter of any [wo](concepts.md#c16). With a [wo](concepts.md#c16)'s internal parameters unchanged, its [weights](concepts.md#c06) can still change with [history](concepts.md#c13).

Note: the [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25) does not say that [wos](concepts.md#c16) cannot be accurately predicted. A person trained always to choose the same answer can have [weights](concepts.md#c06) so concentrated that no experiment can tell the difference. What the [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25) says is: for a [wo](concepts.md#c16) not [locked in](concepts.md#c12), every prediction is a bet on the [weights](concepts.md#c06), not a derivation.

Note: the [Law of Flux](concepts.md#c24) does not say that everything is new. [History](concepts.md#c13) is new at every [moment](concepts.md#c09); but the shape of the [weights](concepts.md#c06) can repeat again and again. What is ever new is [history](concepts.md#c13); what has nothing new is the [weights](concepts.md#c06).

Note: [self](concepts.md#c19), [individual](concepts.md#c20), [identity](concepts.md#c21), [death](concepts.md#c23), causation, and fixed core are all contents of [distinction](concepts.md#c18) and, by this paper's stance, are not formalized; the formal blocks deal only with "a [distinction](concepts.md#c18) is a [writing](concepts.md#c29)". The choice of basis, the relation "far apart", and the [intervention](concepts.md#c01) are not formalized either.

Note: the four laws are the iron laws this paper acknowledges; this paper gives a world in which they are necessary. It does not assert that this is the only such world, nor that unpredictability and irreversibility in reality come only from [collapse](concepts.md#c08).

## 9. Conditions of Falsifiability

The corollaries are not falsifiable: they are logical derivations under the givens, serving as a check. This paper takes the four laws as its starting point and derives the givens backward, so falsification runs in two directions. First: if some law fails in reality, the givens it requires lose their support. Second: if some given is shown not to hold in reality, the laws it supports are left with only their observed form, no longer necessary in principle. [Framework commitments](concepts.md#c03) are supported by none of the laws and are unaffected by either.

At the level of [wos](concepts.md#c16), no experiment can overturn the four laws. The accuracy of predictions cannot reach the [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25); apparent cases of [restoration](concepts.md#c27) are all the one [history](concepts.md#c13) continuing onward. The same holds for the [Law of Flux](concepts.md#c24) and the [Law of Attrition](concepts.md#c26): every experiment is itself a new item of [history](concepts.md#c13), and every examination of oneself is itself a [distinction](concepts.md#c18) not yet [distinguished](concepts.md#c18).

At the physical level, [collapse](concepts.md#c08) [selecting](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32) according to the [weights](concepts.md#c06) in some fixed basis has the same form as the measurement rule of quantum mechanics; the [generating function](concepts.md#c15) is unspecified and can be taken to be the one consistent with quantum mechanics, and quantum mechanics satisfies exactly the [no-signaling](concepts.md#c31) of Given 5. Hence this paper's premises do not contradict known observations, and at present cannot be distinguished from other interpretations by experiment. If it is shown in the future that the outcomes of quantum measurements are determined by something before [collapse](concepts.md#c08), Given 2 will not hold in reality, and the [Law of the Black Box](concepts.md#c25) and the second layer of the [Law of Non-Restoration](concepts.md#c28) will be left with only their observed form; the [Law of Flux](concepts.md#c24), the [Law of Attrition](concepts.md#c26), and the first layer of the [Law of Non-Restoration](concepts.md#c28) are unaffected. If it becomes possible in the future to detect from one side, through the distribution of its own outcomes, which of two far-apart [collapses](concepts.md#c08) came first while Given 2 still holds, the [no-signaling](concepts.md#c31) of Given 5 will not hold in reality.

Whether a [presentation](concepts.md#c04) passes the second question depends on whether [collapse](concepts.md#c08) leaves more than one outcome of non-zero [weight](concepts.md#c06) at its [moments](concepts.md#c09); for people and for artificial intelligence, this is at present unsettled.

```text
Formal:
Falsifiable: reality ⊨ G2a; reality ⊨ no-signaling
Empirically open: for a concrete x (a person, an artificial intelligence, etc.),
  whether ∃ t ∈ T(x): ¬lockedin(x, t)
```

## 10. Conclusion

The [universe](concepts.md#c02) has only one [history](concepts.md#c13). The [observer](concepts.md#c07) is not in the [universe](concepts.md#c02); in the [universe](concepts.md#c02) there are only [presentations](concepts.md#c04); those among them in which all the [history](concepts.md#c13) that [arrives](concepts.md#c10) takes part in the [weights](concepts.md#c06), and which are not [locked in](concepts.md#c12), are [wos](concepts.md#c16); a [wo](concepts.md#c16) that defines itself by [distinction](concepts.md#c18) is a [self](concepts.md#c19).

Time is not a given. One [collapse](concepts.md#c08) is one [moment](concepts.md#c09).

A [wo](concepts.md#c16) is always changing. You cannot uniquely derive me. I cannot finish distinguishing myself. When I die, that segment will not happen again, and it never left.

A [universe](concepts.md#c02) without [wos](concepts.md#c16) has no [moments](concepts.md#c09); a [universe](concepts.md#c02) with [wos](concepts.md#c16) can only go forward.
