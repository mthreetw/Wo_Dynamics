# Interaction Dynamics - Changes and Updates of Dedicated History in Stranger Loops

## 0. Problem and Positioning

The question of this paper is: how do two strangers interact? This paper is a sub-paper of *Interaction Dynamics - Second-Order Networks under a Common Exogenous Background* (hereafter the acquaintance paper). The acquaintance paper deals with the case where [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05) is abundant in many dimensions, with sufficient material that keeps [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10); this paper deals with the case where [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05) is scarce, and with how the [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05) on one [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) goes from little to much and from much to little in each dimension.

<note>

*The acquaintance paper looks at loops among people who know each other. This paper looks at the time when they do not: the two sides have no shared past, or too little of it, so how do they begin to deal with each other, and why might their dealings fail to connect?*

</note>

"Acquaintance" and "stranger" are everyday words, not quantities in the model. The model has only the [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05) on one [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29), accumulated dimension by dimension; the [frequency](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c48) of meeting is a separate parameter and cannot replace [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05). Someone who sees the breakfast-shop owner every day has [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05) with him only in the dimension of "selling breakfast", and remains a stranger in every other dimension. This paper therefore does not divide people into two kinds, nor arrange them along one axis; it says only that in a stated dimension, the [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05) on this [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) is little, and everyday language usually calls this situation being strangers. A convention of this paper: "acquaintance" and "stranger" here refer only to those whose [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at some [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), that is, those within that [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)'s [reachable subgraph](concepts.md#c07). This is a convention about scope, not derived from the definition of [reachable subgraph](concepts.md#c07), and this paragraph is not a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*"Acquaintance" and "stranger" are only everyday ways of speaking. If you go to the breakfast shop every day, you have seen only how the owner sells breakfast; what he is like at home, you do not know. So how familiar two people are has to be looked at one aspect at a time, not by sticking one label on the whole person.*

</note>

This paper deals only with what has already [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10). An object for which no [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) has ever [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at a given [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) is not within that [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)'s [reachable subgraph](concepts.md#c07), is not a stranger in the sense discussed in this paper, and need not be discussed (Premise 5 and the convention above). This paper does not discuss feelings but experience: in this paper "experience" means the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) formed by [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that have already [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) (a convention of this paper; its scope is limited by Premise 3).

<note>

*Only what has really reached oneself counts. Someone one has never met is not on the map and need not be discussed. This paper does not talk about inner feelings; it talks about contact that has already happened.*

</note>

This paper does not distinguish a short model from a long model. Whether [culture](concepts.md#c13) and [templates](concepts.md#c14) can be treated as fixed for the time being depends on the speed of change in each dimension relative to the time window under view, and this is a continuous spectrum; when the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) is treated as an [exogenous background](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c02) under a stated time window and [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23), that time window and [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) must be stated together.

<note>

*The paper does not force "a short time in which the natural environment does not change" and "a long time in which it does change" into two separate theories. Whether something can be treated as unchanging for now depends on how fast it changes and how long a stretch of time you look at.*

</note>

The conclusions of this paper are of two levels, following the practice of the acquaintance paper. A constraint-level conclusion is derived only from the laws and premises listed in Section 1; if any one of them can be overturned, the conclusion fails. A conditional-level conclusion additionally uses some empirical antecedent or some stated assumption; the text points out which one it uses, and if reality violates it, that conclusion fails. An empirical antecedent serves only as an input to derivation and is not used to prove a constraint true. The cases in Section 9 are calibration and belong to neither level.

<note>

*Conclusions come in two kinds: those derived from the rules alone, and those that also rely on an assumption or an observation. For the latter, the text says clearly which one was used. The cases only set the model against reality for comparison; they do not count as evidence.*

</note>

## 1. Inheritance and Constraints

### 1.1 Inheritance

This paper uses the [Law of Flux](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c24), the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25), the [Law of Attrition](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c26), and the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28). It also uses [Unconfirmability of Drives](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c03): for an object that is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), its drives, that is, its motives, needs and purposes, cannot be fully observed from outside and cannot be confirmed by its own account, and others' after-the-fact judgments of its motives are only [view attributions](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02) or [impressions](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45). The methodological prohibitions that follow from this are listed in Section 8; this paper does not cite the numbering of upstream premises and steps, and restates the premises it needs in 1.3.

<note>

*The rules and the list of "what may not be said" are carried over directly, but upstream numbering is not cited; the conditions needed are written out again in this paper. Motives cannot be seen from outside, so nowhere does the paper write who did what for what purpose.*

</note>

### 1.2 Constraint Zero: The Cut

All statements made by the researcher in this paper about high-dimensional [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) are statements under some [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23). Treating the researcher as a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) (a setting of this paper), by Premise 2 and its basis, the [Law of Attrition](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c26), another's [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) cannot fully express itself and the researcher cannot fully receive it, so the researcher cannot obtain another's high-dimensional [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) as a whole from any single [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04); this paper does not assume that putting several [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) together can restore the whole, and the researcher can only specify dimensions, a time window and classification rules and then discuss one slice; this "cutting out of a part" is the [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23). This applies only to the researcher's statements in this paper and does not claim that every statement in the world about high-dimensional [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) is so; there is no "uncut" version to compare against. Every proposition must be reported together with the following: the set of dimensions, the time window, the classification rules, the participating [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25), and who chose the [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) and why. A proposition not reported together with these does not count as a conclusion of this paper. A [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) made by the researcher; once expressed it counts as one [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of the researcher and carries a choice, so who chose it and why must be stated. If the parties themselves also group, regarding which [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) are a group, this is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) by whoever groups, cutting a part out of the whole, and this paper establishes no separate concept. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Anything high-dimensional cannot be discussed without cutting it, so every sentence must make clear: which aspects are being looked at, which period of time, how things are classified, which objects, and who made these choices and why. What is not made clear does not count as a conclusion of this paper. Treating certain people as a group is also a kind of cut, and the parties themselves are cutting too.*

</note>

### 1.3 The Premises of This Paper

This paper has twelve further premises. None is a new law, and none is derived from the other premises. Premises 1 and 2 restate how the upstream laws are used; Premises 3 to 6, Premise 10, Premise 23 and Premise 24 are scope settings; Premises 7 to 9 are empirical antecedents.

```text
Premise 1 Each Distinction made by a Self arrives at that Self before the Self's next Moment.
Premise 2 For any Presentation P and any Wo V (P may be V itself), P's Own Segment always contains a part that has not arrived at V (by the Law of Attrition, a Self cannot fully distinguish itself; its Output comes only from this incomplete distinction, so no Presentation can fully express itself, and a receiver cannot fully receive it).
Premise 3 Scope setting: this paper discusses only the History formed by Outputs that have already arrived, and does not discuss feelings.
Premise 4 Scope setting: within the scope this paper treats, the space of situations (who, when, what, in what combination) cannot be exhausted; any finite History covers only a part of it.
Premise 5 Scope setting: a subgraph counts only what has already arrived, that is, the other side's Output has actually entered the History of some Wo (including Outputs that arrived by being relayed); no separate potential concept of "arrival by some possible path" is introduced.
Premise 6 Scope setting: whether a Template exists is judged by a criterion specified by the researcher; the criterion must be statable, re-obtainable, and applicable to new situations of a specified type.
Premise 7 Empirical antecedent: the Arrivals that a biological human (as a Self) can receive within a period of time are finite.
Premise 8 Empirical antecedent: nerve conduction and reaction in biological humans take time; the other side's reaction to an Output cannot occur simultaneously with that Output.
Premise 9 Empirical antecedent: within a stated dimension and time window, among a group of Selves, if the Frequency of Arrival is higher, Outcome Feedback is stronger, Learning is faster, and Exit Cost is higher, and the Natural Environment changes slowly in that dimension relative to the change in behavior patterns, then the Weight of each Node's Output falling within the compatible region in that dimension tends to become heavier; if these parameters are lower and the Natural Environment changes relatively fast, the Weight tends to become lighter. This says only that the Weight tends to change, and not that formation or non-formation is certain; all five parameters must be stated with how they are judged, the numerical ranges depend on data, and nothing is guaranteed outside the ranges.
Premise 10 Scope setting: each dimension has its own speed of change; within a stated time window, what changes more slowly than the time window is treated as a fixed constant, and what changes faster than the time window must not be treated as a constant.
Premise 23 Scope setting: within a stated time window, the History that a Wo accumulates is finite.
Premise 24 Scope setting: this paper sets no standard for grouping; how large to cut, which people to group, and which dimensions to look at are decided by whoever groups, who must state them.
Note: Premises 1 and 2 are restatements; Premises 3 to 6, Premise 10, Premise 23 and Premise 24 are scope settings; Premises 7 to 9 are empirical antecedents, serving as inputs to derivation and not used to prove a constraint true.
```

## 2. The Model

### 2.1 Types of Nodes and the Unit

A [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) is of three kinds: [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26), and [random source](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c27). A [small loop](concepts.md#c02) belongs only to a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16): the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) returns to its own [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and takes part in its own later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). A [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) (a book, a legal provision, the place where a [template](concepts.md#c14) resides) is not a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16); from some [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) onward it is [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) at every step and has no [small loop](concepts.md#c02); it can give [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) to a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), and its content can be written by a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), so one [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) writing and another [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) reading constitutes an indirect [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) between the two [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) through a [container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26). A [random source](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c27) (for example, a die) is neither a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) nor a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) and has no [small loop](concepts.md#c02); when it is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) at some [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09), only [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) can be discussed. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*There are three kinds of nodes: those that can change as a person does (Wo), things that no longer change once written (books, legal provisions), and things that are known only once thrown (a die). Only the first kind has "its own behavior returning to itself", so only it has a small loop.*

</note>

Under one [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23), a structure formed by two [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) and two kinds of [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) is called a [unit](concepts.md#c01): two [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) each have one [small loop](concepts.md#c02), and one [large loop](concepts.md#c03) connects the two. A [small loop](concepts.md#c02) is a [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) in which the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) returns to its own [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13); a [large loop](concepts.md#c03) is a [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) in which A's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at B, B's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at A, and each takes part in the other's later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). A [unit](concepts.md#c01) must state in which dimension it is judged. The persistence of a [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) across [moments](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02): when a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) [exits](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43), or the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) no longer holds from some [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09), the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) ends. A [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) and a [random source](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c27) can be attached to the [edges](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) of a [unit](concepts.md#c01), but are not the main body of the [unit](concepts.md#c01). A figurative way to put it is two fried eggs on a plate: two [small loops](concepts.md#c02) wrapped by a [large loop](concepts.md#c03); this is only a metaphor and not part of the model.

<note>

*The smallest complete unit: two people, each with a circle of "oneself with oneself", plus a circle "between the two". Like two fried eggs on a plate, but this is only to help imagination and does not count.*

</note>

Each [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) along an [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) is directed; of the two directions, only one may exist, and they need not occur simultaneously. For each [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) in a [unit](concepts.md#c01), its [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) may contain both its own [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) returning to itself and the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of others [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it; if both occur, both take part in its later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06).

<note>

*On one edge, my words reaching you does not mean your words also reach me, nor that both happen at the same time.*

</note>

### 2.2 One Round of Update

At every [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09), every [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) and every [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) may go through the following four steps until the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) it belongs to ends (see 2.1); of these, [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) occur by definition, while [learning](concepts.md#c04) and [outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05) may follow the corresponding [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) (this paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion).
1. [Output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28). For a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16)'s [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) at its next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09), the [generating function](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c15) determines the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) of the [branches](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c05) (the relative lightness or heaviness of the [branches](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c05)) from its [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) up to that time. An [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) is the result of a [selection collapse](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c08) in which one [branch](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c05) is [selected](../paper_54c0291d_05b3_431a_9f2a_1b8ec93c3c3f/concepts.md#c32) according to the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), together with the content of its [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). [Weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) is not a parameter inside any [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), and is not given directly by the [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16)'s "believing something better". When not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), only [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) can be derived, not outcomes. When the result of a [decision](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c06) cannot be calculated in advance, what a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) "believes to be better" belongs to its own [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) ([candidates](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c18), [self-persuasion](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c19)); once such a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at itself it enters the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and takes part in later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), with no guarantee of being right and no guarantee of consistency.
2. [Arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10). An [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at other [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) along an [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) or through a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26), enters the other side's [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13), and only increases, never decreases.
3. [Learning](concepts.md#c04). The process by which material that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) accumulates in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and may change later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06). [Learning](concepts.md#c04) is a result condition; it does not refer to a purpose, has no preset direction, and does not guarantee that anything is learned or learned correctly. The judgment looks only at whether the observable [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) has changed after [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), and does not prove which [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) the change came from.
4. [Outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05). A [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16)'s later [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) again at its own subsequent interactions and brings back actual consequences. The direction of [learning](concepts.md#c04) cannot be predicted beforehand, while the outcome can be judged afterward; the criterion lies in the responses of other [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) and the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16), not inside the [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) in which [learning](concepts.md#c04) occurs: having learned a language one can speak, having learned politeness one probably improves interaction, and conversely there may be a cost.

<note>

*It turns round by round: an action is first produced; it reaches oneself and others; everyone records what they learned into their own experience; later facts then come back and tell you the consequence of what you did. "What counts as better" is known only to the one making the choice, and others cannot judge it for him; there is also no guarantee of what learning will lead to.*

</note>

### 2.3 Combination

A [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) can be an end of many [edges](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) at once. An [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) is directed (in this paper "know" only means that the other side's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10)): "I know him" means his [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at me, and "he knows me" means my [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at him; of the two directions, only one may exist, and they need not occur simultaneously. A [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) can therefore have dozens or hundreds of incoming [edges](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) and, at the same time, dozens or hundreds of outgoing [edges](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29). This shape can be likened to a sea urchin (a metaphor, not part of the model). The [edges](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) of each [unit](concepts.md#c01) can share [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) with other [units](concepts.md#c01), and those [units](concepts.md#c01) in turn each share [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) with more [units](concepts.md#c01), so [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) can overlap one another. What is input and [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) on each [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) is high-dimensional content: language, behavior, the tools used and so on are all high-dimensional [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) passed along the network. The complexity comes from: feedback everywhere; the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) of each [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) at the next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) depend on the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it up to that time; influence returns along [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) and may change the input that [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) afterwards; and under a [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) that counts the researcher in, the researcher is also a [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) on the network. These [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) too may end (see 2.1). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*One person can know many people and be known by many, and knowing and being known need not be equal. These circles join into larger circles, and what is passed along each line is very complex. The person doing the research is also in the net.*

</note>

### 2.4 Parameters of an Edge

An [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) has three parameters: the [frequency](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c48) of [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05), and [exit cost](concepts.md#c06).
The [frequency](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c48) of [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) is how often [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) occurs; it is one of the assumptions about [impact](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c47), must be stated, and does not replace [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05). [Dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05) is counted by dimension: it is the stretch of [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) formed, between the two ends of this [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29), in a stated dimension, by [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that have [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at each other, so the two ends of the same [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) can have material that has arrived at each other in one dimension and none in another (zero before the first [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10)); [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05) is a stretch of history counted by dimension, not a size of the [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29), and is not assumed to be quantifiable. [Exit cost](concepts.md#c06) is the [outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05) that a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) must bear when it stops or reduces [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) on this [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29); each end counts its own and the two may be asymmetric. Low cost means the [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) can easily be abandoned; high cost means one can hardly help but continue. The observable version: after a negative [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) is given, the other side still keeps appearing and keeps giving [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); the outward appearance cannot tell the reason, and no motive is written. [Exit cost](concepts.md#c06) and [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43) are at different levels ([edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) versus [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25)) and do not imply each other; a high [exit cost](concepts.md#c06) does not imply not leaving, and an extremely high one is not the same as [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*A line has three values: how often the two meet; in which respects they have shared experience; how much it costs to leave. The cost of leaving can differ on the two sides. A high cost does not mean he will not go, and it is not the same as his being dead.*

</note>

[Impact](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c47) and [discrepancy](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c49) are not parameters of an [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) but properties of "an [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) relative to its receiver". [Impact](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c47) is how much [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) this [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) can move; it is a matter internal to the [generating function](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c15) and cannot be added up; [discrepancy](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c49) is how far it is from what the receiver has received in the past. When the same [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at different [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), its [impact](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c47) and [discrepancy](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c49) depend on each one's [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and can differ, and the movement of each one's [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) can also differ. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*The same sentence carries different weight for different people: some feel nothing on hearing it, others are greatly changed, because each person's experience differs. So this is not a property of the "line" but of "this sentence for this person".*

</note>

### 2.5 Reachable Subgraph and Natural Environment

Taking a given [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) as the center, within a stated time window, all [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) whose [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it, together with the [edges](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) between these [presentations](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04) and it, constitute its [reachable subgraph](concepts.md#c07) (Premise 5). Each [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) has one. By Premise 2, no [presentation](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c04)'s [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) can have all [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at any [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16). Within the stated time window, an object outside the subgraph has no [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) that has had [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at this [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), so the dedicated material between that object and this [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) within the window is zero (dedicated material is discussed only when the object is a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)); entering the subgraph is a new [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) and, by the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28), the material that has arrived only adds and never subtracts. [Arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) is directed: one side having material does not imply that the other side has material. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Each person holds a one-way map of "whom I have really come across", every one different, and none covers everyone. My having read his news does not mean I am on his map.*

</note>

The subgraph is finite: within a stated time window, the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that each [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) accumulates is finite (Premise 23); combined with the situation space being impossible to exhaust (Premise 4), the situations covered by any subgraph are only a part of it; for situations not covered, the existing [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) does not guarantee that this [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16)'s later [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) will be compatible with the other side's; where there is no other basis, it can probe afresh. These two items are constraint-level and use Premise 23 and Premise 4.

<note>

*The experience that a Wo accumulates in a period of time is finite; and one has met only a small part of the situations in the world, so for situations never met, the experience on hand is not guaranteed to help.*

</note>

Calibration (not a source of derivation): a person has read a great many public [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of some public figure, but the figure has never received his [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); this is a one-way [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10). The archives left by someone who has [exited](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43) can increase without limit while [outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05) is zero. Both show that the amount of data and [outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05) are two quantities that can come apart.

<note>

*A celebrity's materials can be plentiful, but he does not know you; the materials a dead person leaves can be endless, but he will never respond to you again. So "how much data there is" and "whether there is a response" must be looked at separately.*

</note>

The [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) is, under a stated [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23), the part that does not belong to any [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) in the model yet has [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at these [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25); it includes physical conditions, such as terrain, climate, water and food; it constrains the most basic behavior of each [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25). When the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) does not update with the [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31) in the model under a stated time window and [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23), it is treated as an [exogenous background](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c02), and an [exogenous background](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c02) can change; by Premise 10, to treat it as a fixed constant within that time window, its change in that dimension must be slower than the time window under view; when it changes faster than the time window, it must not be treated as a constant, and conclusions that depend on it being a constant must be re-examined (see Section 7). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*The natural environment is also a body of things that reach you. If it hardly changes during the stretch of time you are looking at, treat it as a background; if it changes quickly, it has to be counted into the model.*

</note>

This structure has three scales, not three kinds of position: the [unit](concepts.md#c01); the [reachable subgraph](concepts.md#c07) of one [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16); and a global graph within a particular range. A single [individual](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c20) may or may not obtain the global graph, depending on whether the output of every object in the network has arrived at it: if some object belongs to the network under discussion and some [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) has not yet been reached by that object's output, that object is outside this Self's [reachable subgraph](concepts.md#c07), and this Self's [reachable subgraph](concepts.md#c07) does not cover the entire network. An institution holding a large amount of network data (an institution is a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), made of responses and inputs, not a place or a building) may obtain a certain degree of whole-graph data, but its data are whatever has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the institution (not limited to the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of various [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19)), so by Premise 2 they remain incomplete and do not cover all dimensions. A [large node](concepts.md#c08) is, within a stated [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) and time window, a [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) whose [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at an extremely large number of [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25), covering almost every [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) within the range in question, for example a government, a public figure, or a large enterprise. A [large node](concepts.md#c08) is an actually existing [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25), not an imaginary researcher. Great influence is not only a property of the [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) itself: when the same [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at different [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), its [impact](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c47) and [discrepancy](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c49) depend on each one's [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and can differ, so broad [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) does not imply great influence; by Premise 2 no [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can fully receive others, so it does not imply omniscience either; having a purpose falls under the [Unconfirmability of Drives](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c03) and is not written in this paper. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*The same structure can be viewed at the unit, at one person's circle, or at a large expanse: three sizes. A large node (a government, a celebrity, a big company) is a real party, not an imagined bystander; that its words travel widely does not mean everyone is changed, still less that it knows everything or has any plans.*

</note>

### 2.6 Changes in Dedicated History across Dimensions

Contact increases [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05): the two sides' [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at each other, and the material only adds, never subtracts. Separation does not delete [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) (the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28)) but may make the material fall out of step with the other side's current state: during the separation, the other side's [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) keeps changing (the [Law of Flux](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c24)), and these changes may include a part that has not [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at you (Premise 2 only guarantees that the other side's [own segment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c17) always contains a part that has not [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at you, not that every stretch of change does). The other side's [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) has already changed, and what you hold is old material; the basis for using it to estimate the other side's present compatible region may weaken under the specified [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) (conditional-level, depending on the dimension that changed and the compatibility criterion). Therefore the accumulated amount alone is not enough to judge whether the material is "effective"; the distance between the material and the other side's current state may also have to be considered. All of the above holds for each dimension separately. The two sentences on the basis for estimating the other side's present compatible region from old material, and on "effective", are conditional-level; the rest is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Meeting often increases the shared experience in that respect; after a long separation, the past does not disappear, but the information you hold becomes old and the other person has changed. Each respect is counted on its own.*

</note>

What is ordinarily called being a stranger corresponds to at least two situations (not exhaustive): the object has just entered the [reachable subgraph](concepts.md#c07), where one-way [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) material begins to accumulate, and [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05) begins to accumulate only once the object is a [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) and the two sides' [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) have [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at each other; and there is old material but no new [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10). These two situations must not be confused. At a reunion, if there is a gap between the old material and the other side's present situation, and the gap makes the two sides' adjacent [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) incompatible under the specified dimension and criterion, that is [mismatch](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c08); whether there is a gap, how large it is, and whether it causes such incompatibility depends on data, and is conditional-level.

<note>

*"Not familiar" comes in two kinds: never having met, and having been close before but now grown distant. Whether the two will fail to connect when they meet again depends on the actual data.*

</note>

Position is not monotonic: contact only increases material and does not guarantee compatibility; separation only may make material outdated and does not erase it. Renewed contact is a new occurrence, not a [restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c27) of the original state (the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28)). Old material may make recalibration faster than starting from zero, or may lead to [mismatch](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c08) because the other side has changed; the speed is not guaranteed. These rules do not depend on the size of the [unit](concepts.md#c01): two friends, or two families, who have long been out of contact and grown estranged are the same structure, as long as the [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) is stated. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Familiarity does not go steadily up or down: more meetings do not necessarily mean a better fit, and a long separation is not a restart from zero. A reunion is a new occurrence.*

</note>

This paper does not assume that the effective amount of [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05) is a single number. It is compared only under a specified [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23): in some dimension and time window, whether compatible continuations are more, fewer, or in [stasis](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c52) compared with the previous point in time; a number is reported only for countable dimensions, and it is marked as belonging only to that [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) and is not extended to other dimensions. In this paper [outward data](concepts.md#c18) means the results that were selected and expressed, and their summaries; they are not equal to the full [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) and dependency paths. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*"Degree of familiarity" is not a score. It can only be compared within some respect and some period of time, giving more, less or unchanged. A number is reported only for respects that can be counted, and it belongs only to that respect.*

</note>

## 3. Direction by Dimension

For a pair of [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) and one dimension, look at how the distance between the two sides' [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) patterns changes over time. All three directions fall under [coupling](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36) and all require the dimension to be stated: becoming smaller is [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37); becoming larger is [divergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c38); staying unchanged is [stasis](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c52). What is called "repulsion" in daily speech is only everyday language, not another concept in the model. Where the two sides aim at each other and reinforce each other, upstream calls this [Taiji](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c39), and where both sides are [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) it appears outwardly as [conflict](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c10); [divergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c38) does not imply [Taiji](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c39). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Two people's ways of doing things in some respect become more alike (convergence), less alike (divergence), or stay as they were (stasis). Becoming less alike is not the same as quarreling; quarreling is the two sides aiming at each other and strengthening each other, which is the Taiji. Each time, it must be made clear which respect is meant.*

</note>

[Resonance](concepts.md#c09) is a term of this paper: within a stated dimension and time window, the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) patterns of several [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) repeatedly continue one another compatibly. [Resonance](concepts.md#c09) requires [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10): either arrival between the [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25)s, or arrival at all of them from a common source (such as a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) or the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16)). Without any arrival, there is no [resonance](concepts.md#c09). Its criterion is compatibility, that is, the opposite of [mismatch](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c08), not distance. The direction in which distance changes is not the criterion; whether distance becomes smaller, larger, or stays unchanged, it does not by itself decide compatibility: for example, in a question and answer, the two sides' patterns differ, yet they can still repeatedly continue each other compatibly. [Convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37) can lead to [resonance](concepts.md#c09), but does not entail it; [resonance](concepts.md#c09) also does not entail [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37). Whether there is [resonance](concepts.md#c09) can only be said afterward. [Resonance](concepts.md#c09) differs from [tacit coordination](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c06): [tacit coordination](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c06) is compatible continuation on the [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05) between two [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), counted by dimension; [resonance](concepts.md#c09) does not require [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05) and is not limited to two [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*"Resonance" is this paper's own term; it means many people repeatedly "fitting together" in some respect. Fitting together does not mean doing the same thing: one asks, one answers, the ways differ, yet they fit. Tacit coordination happens only between two people and is worn in by the experience between the two; resonance does not rely on that and is not limited to two people.*

</note>

In high dimensions, each dimension goes its own way: some [converge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37), some [diverge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c38), and some are in [stasis](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c52). The synchronization phenomena of systems theory serve only as a calibration comparison; this paper does not claim that they are special cases of the same kind of structure as this model, and does not cite synchronization theorems. This paper's own term is always [resonance](concepts.md#c09).

<note>

*Physics and systems theory have the phenomenon of "synchronization"; it is only used here for comparison, not as proof, and it is not claimed that the two are the same thing.*

</note>

Who judges what counts as a "cost" must be made clear.*

</note>

The materials that can be relied on over a low-frequency [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) are: reputation, etiquette, [templates](concepts.md#c14) (with [enforcement](concepts.md#c11)). When [exit cost](concepts.md#c06) is low, this [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) may cease to exist early on because of a single step; in that case these materials may let people form an [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) of the other side from the start: these materials are traces of the other side's [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) (the other side's own [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), or material relayed or carried through a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26)) and enter the receiver's [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) once their [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) occurs; since the other side's [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) are fixed by its [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) up to that time (the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25)) and the receiver cannot obtain that [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) as a whole (Premise 2, by the [Law of Attrition](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c26)), the receiver can only form an [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) from the material that has arrived and judge the other side's tendencies from it (an [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) is not a reading of [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06)), for example, a reputation for honesty lets one estimate that the other side's [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) tend toward honesty even without knowing whether the reputation is true; this judgment is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) of the receiver, enters its own [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and takes part in its later [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) (conditional-level, using low [exit cost](concepts.md#c06); the judgment is not guaranteed to be accurate, nor to ensure that behavior when meeting face to face fits the other side). Whether the materials are reliable is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02), and they can be misread (by the [Law of Attrition](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c26), the materials come only from incomplete [distinctions](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) and the receiver cannot fully receive them). In this paragraph the sentence on how the materials let people form an [impression](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c45) is conditional-level, and the rest is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*When the cost of leaving is low, at the start the relationship more easily breaks off because of a single step. Reputation, politeness and written rules may help reduce the friction of the first few steps, these things let you judge roughly what kind of person the other side is, just as when you hear that someone is honest, you cannot be sure it is true, yet you can still act on it. There is no guarantee that they work, and they may also go wrong.*

</note>

Biological-human antecedents: limited reception (Premise 7) and delayed reaction (Premise 8) give common limits, not common content. That [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) does not return to zero is not an empirical antecedent: that [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) only adds and never subtracts is a conclusion of the [Law of Non-Restoration](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c28) and the [Law of Flux](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c24), and is constraint-level. The parts of this paragraph that use Premise 7 and Premise 8 are conditional-level.

<note>

*Every person has a limit to what they can bear, and every reaction takes time; these are limits that everyone shares, not everyone learning the same thing. "The past does not disappear" needs no separate assumption, since the rules already say so.*

</note>

## 5. Culture (the Researcher's Name for Resonance)

[Culture](concepts.md#c13) is the researcher's name for the [resonance](concepts.md#c09) that appears among [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25). Under a specified [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23), dimension and time window, it is a set of compatible patterns left in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) by countless [units](concepts.md#c01), under the constraints of a particular [natural environment](concepts.md#c16), through repeated [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28), [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) and [outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05): which combinations of dimensions can continue without being repeatedly interrupted. For a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) within it, this set of patterns enters its [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) through concrete [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) and takes part in its [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06); the material that [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) earlier has entered the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13), and the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) takes part in the [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) through the [generating function](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c15); in these dimensions the [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) may not have to probe from zero every time (conditional-level, depends on data). This does not guarantee that every [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) obtains the same content, nor that the material is correct. [Culture](concepts.md#c13) is not bounded by whether it has been written down: compatible patterns that have [resonated](concepts.md#c09) into being are called [culture](concepts.md#c13) whether or not they have been written into a [container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26). Apart from the sentence marked conditional-level, this paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Culture is the name an onlooker gives to "many people repeatedly fitting together in many respects". For those inside it, because what earlier people left behind is already in hand, they do not have to feel their way from the beginning each time. This does not mean everyone learns the same, nor that what is learned is right; it counts whether or not it was written down.*

</note>

Grouping is a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18); by Premise 24, this paper sets no standard for it. How large to cut, which people to group, and which dimensions to look at are decided by the researcher; if the parties themselves also cut, the researcher describes it as a [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18). Christians and Muslims each cut for themselves and are not one body to each other; cut from outside, they are the Abrahamic religions; the model does not adjudicate. The cut itself is an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); once it [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the other side, if it enters the corresponding [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31), it may turn back to change the network that was cut. The [resonance](concepts.md#c09) pattern the researcher sees under a specified dimension and time window is one layer; if the parties also group themselves and express it, there is a further layer of the parties' own boundary-drawing and belonging. If both layers exist, they can be inconsistent and do not imply each other. [Culture](concepts.md#c13) has no subject and is neither held nor transmitted by anyone. [Culture](concepts.md#c13) is also scale-free: a village has [culture](concepts.md#c13), and a [culture](concepts.md#c13) over a wide range is also [culture](concepts.md#c13). The three sentences on the cut turning back to change the network that was cut, on the two layers when the parties also group themselves, and on the two layers being able to be inconsistent are conditional-level; the rest is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Whom to count as a group is decided by the one looking, and the ones being looked at are also deciding whom they count as. The two accounts can differ, and the model does not judge who is right. Culture belongs to no one, and can be discussed at any size.*

</note>

The formation of [culture](concepts.md#c13) cannot do without the constraints of the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16). When a nomadic [culture](concepts.md#c13) is moved to New York, its defaults do not work; there is no essential difference between a modern person entering a grassland and entering a desert, because in both cases the [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) patterns have no corresponding accumulation of [natural environment](concepts.md#c16). Some dimensions of [culture](concepts.md#c13) connect to survival conditions (finding water, food), and some are merely neglected; this paper sets up no division between "dimensions of [culture](concepts.md#c13)" and "dimensions that are not of [culture](concepts.md#c13)", only consequences that are more direct or more indirect. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Culture cannot be separated from the natural environment it is in. In some respects the consequences are direct (not finding water is serious), in others light (rudeness only gets one cold-shouldered). It is not that some respects count as culture and others do not.*

</note>

Strong [coupling](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36) and non-assemblability of [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) constraints (conditional-level, under some [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) of the researcher): the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) constrains the most basic behavior of each [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) (such as the ocean or the grassland). Under a specified [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23), dimension and time window, the [resonance](concepts.md#c09) patterns that remain tend no longer to include practices pushed away by negative feedback from the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16): for practices that receive negative [outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05) in their interaction with the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16), these feedbacks enter [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13), and if they move the [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) away from that practice and this moving away continues in later [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) (continuation is not claimed as necessary; whether [learning](concepts.md#c04) moves it that way depends on data), the practice tends not to be repeatedly [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at, so what is repeatedly left is the practices not yet pushed away by such feedback; the successions in the various dimensions may also depend on one another in [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13). Therefore under this [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23), [culture](concepts.md#c13) is not guaranteed to be freely assembled or transplanted like building blocks; having lost the original [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16), the transplanted [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) patterns may lose the corresponding [outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05) that supports them. This is a tendency, not a necessity; under a different [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23), the boundary and degree of [coupling](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c36) can differ.

<note>

*Under a certain view, a culture is tightly bound to its natural environment and past, and cannot be moved away in one piece like building blocks. This is only a tendency; under a different view, the boundaries drawn will differ.*

</note>

Tendency for [resonance](concepts.md#c09) to form easily (conditional-level, using Premise 9): when [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) [frequency](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c48) is high, [outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05) is strong, [learning](concepts.md#c04) is fast enough, [exit cost](concepts.md#c06) is high, and the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) changes relatively slowly, the [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) of the [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of the [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) falling within the compatible region in that dimension tends to become heavier, without requiring [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37); when [exit cost](concepts.md#c06) is low, [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) [frequency](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c48) is low, [outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05) is weak, [learning](concepts.md#c04) is slow, and the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) changes fast, the tendency is low. A high tendency does not guarantee formation, and a low tendency does not guarantee non-formation; nothing is guaranteed outside the range.

<note>

*When people meet often, feedback is clear, learning is quick and the natural environment changes slowly, it is easier for everyone to fit together; the reverse is harder. This is a tendency, not a guarantee.*

</note>

[Culture](concepts.md#c13) and [tacit coordination](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c06) stand side by side, neither depending on the other nor implying the other.

<note>

*Tacit coordination is worn in between two people; culture is among many people; having one does not mean having the other.*

</note>

[Outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05) and compatible patterns only describe and carry no purpose. This paper does not write the logical consistency of [culture](concepts.md#c13) as coming from the design of a will. Every [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16) may have [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) and [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at each [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09); the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) and other [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) may give [outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05) (including costs and [exit cost](concepts.md#c06)). [Weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) falls at whichever level the researcher cuts the [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25): taking a neuron, a person or a company as the [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) gives the [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) of the [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) at that level; under a stated [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23), how heavy each outcome is at a [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16)'s next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) depends on the [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) that has [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at it; this paper does not assume that the network as a whole has a single direction, and speaks only of the directions of individual [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) and individual [loops](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31), which may be consistent, opposite or unrelated to one another. (Conditional-level, depends on data) Under continuing [outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05), combinations of behavior that keep receiving negative [outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05) in their interaction with the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) tend not to be repeatedly [arrived](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at for long: these consequences enter [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13), and if they move the [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) away from this combination of behavior and this moving away continues in later [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) (the direction of [learning](concepts.md#c04) is not preset, and whether it moves away depends on data; continuation is not claimed as necessary), it appears less and less as an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); this describes only what is left afterward, and does not write that a [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) "adopts something in order to adapt", nor that the selection has a purpose. Apart from the sentence marked conditional-level, this paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*No one is designing culture. Each person does their own thing and has their own consequences; ways of doing things that seriously fail to fit the natural environment do not last, so what remains all looks "suitable", but this is only seen afterward and was not arranged by anyone.*

</note>

This does not imply that "all [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) will adopt behavior suited to that [natural environment](concepts.md#c16)": a [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) that is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12) can, at its next [moment](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09), only have [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) derived and not outcomes, so if some outcomes have a positive [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) yet do not fit the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16), individual [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) may deviate; negative [outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05) may be being neglected, rejected, or suffering loss, not necessarily [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43), so selection can be graded; other [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) themselves also give [outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05), and what is "suitable" moves with their [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28); when the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) changes faster than selection, what remains is not necessarily suited to the present [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) (see Section 7). "Computation", "selection", "elimination" and "survivor solution" are used in this paper only as metaphors and must be marked as metaphors. The sentence beginning "This does not imply" is conditional-level; the rest is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*It cannot be said that "everyone who remains is suitable": individuals may refuse to follow, they do not necessarily get into trouble for it, others' reactions also change, and when the natural environment changes quickly, what was suitable in the past is not necessarily suitable now. Words like "selection" and "elimination" are only metaphors.*

</note>

## 6. Template

A [template](concepts.md#c14) is abstracted, reusable rules or data, explicitly listed in a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26). The [container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) is [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), its content does not change by itself, and it provides a fixed point in some dimension, obtainable without first needing [resonance](concepts.md#c09). A single rule with two texts counts as two [templates](concepts.md#c14). A [template](concepts.md#c14) is fixed content in a [container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26), not a layer of [culture](concepts.md#c13). Whether a [template](concepts.md#c14) exists is judged by a criterion specified by the researcher (Premise 6). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*A template is a rule or body of data that has been organized to be reusable and written into something that does not change by itself (a book, a legal provision). The same rule written as two copies is two templates. Whether one exists is judged by a standard agreed beforehand.*

</note>

The source of a [template](concepts.md#c14) can be that some group's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) is explicitly listed and written into a [container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26). What has [resonated](concepts.md#c09) into being can be written as a [template](concepts.md#c14); once written, the content in the [container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) and the compatible patterns that people actually follow are two different things, their persistence is discussed separately, and neither implies the other. A [template](concepts.md#c14) is fixed content in a [container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26); the content [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the receiver along an [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) or through a [container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26); if the content is copied into another [container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26), then by the definition of a [template](concepts.md#c14), two texts of the same rule are two [templates](concepts.md#c14); the content in the [container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) need not wear out (the [container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) is [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), so the content does not change by itself), and the difference lies in the receiver's use (the same [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) has different [impact](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c47) on different [Selves](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), and no [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) can fully receive others; "need not wear out" refers to the content in the [container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26), not to what the receiver obtains). The relation of a [template](concepts.md#c14) to the group that carries it has two divisions: endogenous versus exogenous; non-[enforced](concepts.md#c11) versus [enforced](concepts.md#c11). These are relations, not kinds of things in the world, and are relative to the scope drawn. [Enforcement](concepts.md#c11) is a matter of another point in time: violation can be judged by a third party, but compliance is not guaranteed, and neither is it guaranteed that a violation is judged and carried out. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Rules can be slowly worked out by everyone and then written down; once written, the rules on paper and what people actually do are two things, each with its own fate. When passed along, the words on paper do not wear out, but different people use them differently. Whether the rule was written by insiders and whether it is enforced are both relative to the scope you draw.*

</note>

The [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of a [large node](concepts.md#c08), if explicitly listed in a [signal container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) (for example a decree signed by a head of state) and meets the abstraction and reusability that [templates](concepts.md#c14) require, is a source of [templates](concepts.md#c14); if it is an [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of the photograph kind, whether it [resonates](concepts.md#c09) into [culture](concepts.md#c13) after [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at many [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) can only be said afterward. The two carry different things but travel through the same network. Why that [node](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) acts as it does is not written, nor whether it has the result it wants. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*For the same important figure, a decree he signs is a rule written into a container, while a photograph he posts is just one output sent out; whether the photograph brings out a shared way of doing things has to wait for afterward. Why he acts so is not discussed here.*

</note>

A [template](concepts.md#c14) in the stranger paper may be material on a low-frequency [edge](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c29) that lets people first estimate the tendencies of the other side's [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), without probing from zero (conditional-level, using low [exit cost](concepts.md#c06); for the mechanism see Section 4; it is not guaranteed that behavior when meeting face to face fits the other side); whether it is reliable is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02), and it can be misread (reasons in Section 4). In some dimension, a common [template](concepts.md#c14) provides a fixed point that both sides can obtain; if both sides obtain and adopt it, and it applies in that dimension, each side can estimate the other without probing from zero (conditional-level); this does not imply that the two sides are compatible outside that dimension.

<note>

*If two strangers share a rule (for example the same set of game rules), they can connect first on that small piece, while in other respects they still have to feel their way themselves. Rules are not guaranteed to help, and may also be misunderstood.*

</note>

## 7. Persistence and Collapse

By Premise 10, each dimension has its own speed of change, and what changes more slowly than the time window is treated as fixed. An [exogenous background](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c02) is a setting of scope and does not claim that the background is unchanged; [culture](concepts.md#c13) and the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) can be treated as constants in dimensions whose change is slower than the specified time window, but if the time window is stretched long enough, [culture](concepts.md#c13) may also change with the continuing change of [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) (the [Law of Flux](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c24)). This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*"Treat this as not changing for now" is an approximation: it can be treated as fixed only if it changes more slowly than the time you are looking at.*

</note>

Constraint-level: [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) keeps changing and does not stop (the [Law of Flux](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c24)); how much it changes, in what direction, and whether things can still connect are [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02) (the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25)). Conditional-level: under some [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) of the researcher, when the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) changes so much in some dimension that it cannot return to what it was (for example, from grassland to New York), [culture](concepts.md#c13) tends to change too (see Section 5: the [outcome feedback](concepts.md#c05) of the original [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) may disappear, and the original [resonance](concepts.md#c09) pattern may lose its support; if it does, and the [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) of the original pattern moves away as a result and keeps moving away in later [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), [culture](concepts.md#c13) tends to change too). Under a stated [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23), if the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) changes in some dimension at a speed greater than the speed at which [resonance](concepts.md#c09) forms in that dimension, and this change makes previously workable practices unworkable and, if the [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) moves away from these practices as a result, and this moving away continues in later [arrivals](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), these practices tend to be hard to keep up as a stable [resonance](concepts.md#c09); whether other successions form depends on data.

<note>

*History keeps changing, and what it becomes is not guaranteed; that is the rule. When the environment changes drastically and cannot be gone back to (for example, moving from a grassland to New York), the culture it used to hold up can hardly stay as it was; and if the environment changes faster than ways of doing things can form, stable ways of doing things do not easily form.*

</note>

[Collapse](concepts.md#c15) is, at a specified scale, dimension and time window, the loss of a former order; an order means the defaults and patterns of continuation that can continue compatibly without being directed each time. The after-the-fact [distinction](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c18) is drawn by the [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19) making the judgment. [Collapse](concepts.md#c15) is scale-free, graded, and dimension by dimension: when the dimension of finding water is lost, the consequences fall directly on survival; when some etiquette is lost, the consequence may be only being cold-shouldered. The acquaintance paper did not use this term, which is a choice of scope and not a correction. [Collapse](concepts.md#c15) differs from [mismatch](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c08) and [exit](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c43) in criterion; it may be a cause or a form of them, but does not entail them. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*Collapse is when ways of doing things that used to connect without needing to be explained no longer connect, judged afterward by some person. It comes in different sizes and differs across respects. It is a different standard from "not fitting" and from "dead"; it can be a cause or a result of them, but one cannot be directly derived from the other.*

</note>

The limit cannot be uniquely inferred in advance from the [Law of the Black Box](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c25): each [Wo](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c16), at the [moments](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c09) when it is not [locked in](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c12), can only have [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) discussed, and whether [resonance](concepts.md#c09) is maintained can only be said afterward; the [proportion of failed expectations](concepts.md#c17) (the after-the-fact statistical proportion of originally compatible successions that failed to connect; an outward statistic, not a reading of true [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06)) cannot be used to uniquely infer whether the next succession will connect; it can serve only as a conditional reference that is not guaranteed to be accurate. The persistence of [templates](concepts.md#c14) and of [culture](concepts.md#c13) is mutually independent: a [template](concepts.md#c14) depends on a [container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26), [culture](concepts.md#c13) depends on the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) plus repeated [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10). Latin is a case where the [template](concepts.md#c14) exists but nobody uses it daily; Confucianism is a case where the [culture](concepts.md#c13) exists but that [template](concepts.md#c14) no longer [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) repeatedly; Java is a foreign [template](concepts.md#c14) within computer [culture](concepts.md#c13), which for Chinese people is peaceful learning. Whether rebuilding, replacement or emptiness follows [collapse](concepts.md#c15) is [not guaranteed](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c02); this paper does not write good or bad, and does not write what comes after. This paragraph is a constraint-level conclusion.

<note>

*There is no way to know beforehand whether something will collapse. Rules (templates) and customs (culture) each have their own fate: Latin books are still there but nobody uses them daily; the Confucian ethos is still there but the written-down rules are no longer repeatedly read; Java is a foreign rule that Chinese people learn slowly. What happens after a collapse is not guaranteed.*

</note>

## 8. List of Prohibitions

This paper prohibits the following in method.
1. No inference beyond the [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23). Results under a [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) must not be extended beyond that [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23): to dimensions not cut, beyond the sampling boundary, or to "that [culture](concepts.md#c13) pattern does not exist". Comparisons between different [cuts](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23) require consistent dimensions, time windows and classification rules.
2. Write only the possibility of [resonance](concepts.md#c09). [Resonance](concepts.md#c09) is written only as "possible, and possibly partial"; no guarantee is written, no necessary convergence is written, and it is not equated with good, correct or friendly. Without any [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) there is no [resonance](concepts.md#c09); with [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) there is still no guarantee.
3. Do not write motives. Drives must not be written as facts that can be observed, confirmed by self-report, or verified after the fact; [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) must not be explained with "in order to", and only the observable distribution of outputs is written (some kind of [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) in some dimension appears more often afterwards, or more often than in the previous period); where [weight] is discussed (weight is the lightness or heaviness of [branches](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c05), like a probability), it must be marked that the researcher relies only on an incomplete outward distribution, cannot directly determine the underlying [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) value, and can only make a conditional estimate; a distribution is not the [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) (see 2.6 last paragraph and item 13 of this section). Interpretations by recorders and researchers are always marked as [view attributions](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02); "having something to seek" is written only in its observable version.
4. [Learning](concepts.md#c04) must not be written as having a direction or as necessarily better; from the number of interactions, immersion time or ability, one must not infer that some item was learned on some occasion.
5. [Culture](concepts.md#c13), [templates](concepts.md#c14) and [tacit coordination](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c06) must not be inferred from one another; compliance must not be inferred from the existence of a [template](concepts.md#c14) or of [enforcement](concepts.md#c11); understanding, agreement or [tacit coordination](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c06) must not be inferred from compliance.
6. When using the term "[culture](concepts.md#c13)", one must state who cuts, which people are cut, and which dimensions are looked at; a word with only a name and no criterion must not be used to fill an explanatory gap, and the concrete [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) input must be pointed out.
7. Do not write that [culture](concepts.md#c13) "determines" survival, and do not write "formed in order to adapt".
8. Any conclusion that treats [culture](concepts.md#c13), [templates](concepts.md#c14) or the [natural environment](concepts.md#c16) as fixed must state the dimension-by-dimension time-scale ratio; local conditions are treated as given only within the stated time window, must not be written as permanently unchanged, and must not be written as driving all dimensions equally.
9. The [proportion of failed expectations](concepts.md#c17) must not be used to uniquely infer whether [collapse](concepts.md#c15) will occur, nor to prove that "this change is still within the limit".
10. One must not infer from the researcher's grouping that the parties identify themselves as one, nor the reverse.
11. A [large node](concepts.md#c08) must not be written as omniscient, nor may its purposes be written.
12. One must not write that acquaintances and strangers are two kinds of relationship; must not write that contact monotonically increases familiarity; must not write old material as valid for the present state without checking; must not write separation as the disappearance of [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13).
13. Outward statistics, sequence, or a recorded path must not be written as a direct reading of the true [weights](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) or dependency paths; a feeling of certainty must not be taken as evidence; when speaking of [convergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c37), [divergence](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c38) or [stasis](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c52), the dimension must be stated.
14. The researcher must not be assumed to be outside the [loop](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c31): if the researcher's observations, questions and records [arrive](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) at the observed [Self](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c19), they enter the other side's [history](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c13) and cannot be taken back.

<note>

*This section is a list of "must not say" that the paper sets for itself: do not present the results of a cut as the whole; do not say resonance is bound to occur or is bound to be good; do not write who did what for what purpose; do not say that learning makes things better; do not let culture, templates and tacit coordination be inferred from one another; do not say culture "determines" survival; do not take outward data as seeing through the inside; do not write important figures as knowing everything; do not say acquaintances and strangers are two kinds of people; and the researcher is also in the net.*

</note>

## 9. Cases

This whole section is calibration and is not a source of any derivation. The three cases are fragments from three local perspectives, not three types, and are used to see how the update rules run under different amounts of material. What is written is the difficulty of communication and talking past one another, not what they said, because thoughts cannot be confirmed from outside. Each case unfolds in seven steps: [cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23), initial state, new [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), change in [weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06), outcome, what it shows, and what can be said versus what cannot be inferred. The materials are all taken from public web pages and are not original files; each case states at the beginning what was read and what was not; the researcher's interpretation is kept separate from the record; where sources [conflict](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c10), they are only set side by side and no side is chosen.

<note>

*This section only sets real events beside the model to see how well it fits; it is not used to prove any conclusion. The data come from public web pages and not original documents, so each case begins by stating what was read.*

</note>

### 9.1 Portugal and the Tupiniquim (1500)

Data note. What was read: the full Portuguese text page of the letter of 1 May 1500 by Pero Vaz de Caminha (relevant passages extracted by a tool, not read page by page), the Wikipedia entry for Caminha, and a brief account of the first contact in a Brazilian academic article. Góis's *Chronicle* is known only as relayed by that article, and the original book was not read. There is no first-hand record of this contact from the shore side. The names of the people differ among the sources: Wikipedia writes Tupiniquim, while that academic article says the people are unidentified.

<note>

*What was read are pages on the web, not original files. The people on the shore left no record, so only the fleet's account can be seen. The names of the people differ among the sources and are set side by side.*

</note>

1. [Cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23). The scope is the roughly ten days (from 22 April) during which the fleet lay at anchor near Porto Seguro. There are two [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25): the fleet side, and the crowd on shore. The recorder Caminha belonged to the fleet side and is also the only source, so the record is one-sided.
2. Initial state. The record shows no language, [container](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26) or explicitly listed rule shared by both sides; in the dimensions of this contact, the two sides have no [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05).
3. New [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10). Mainly gestures and objects. Nicolau Coelho of the fleet side signaled to the other side to put down their bows, and they did so; the two sides exchanged objects (clothes, bells, mirrors and the like for maize, flour, beans, fruit and the like); on one occasion a man on shore gestured toward the ground, then pointed at a necklace and a candlestick. The letter also says "when signaled to leave, they left", and "there were no more words or understanding then".
4. [Weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) change. Only the outward record is written: after the fleet side made a gesture signaling them to put down the bows, the other side put down the bows; after the other side pointed at the ground and the necklace, the fleet side's record wrote "there is gold there". Caminha himself wrote: "We understood it so because we wished to understand it so." This is the recorder's attribution about his own interpretation, not a reading of what the other side's gestures meant.
5. Outcome. The fleet left on 3 May; the letter records an agreement to leave two exiled convicts behind.
6. What it shows. When material is at its least, updating relies only on probing: one side's [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10), the other side's later [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arrives](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) in turn, and they try step by step. Second, the recorder admits that his interpretation was affected by "wishing", a clear example of [view attribution](../paper_fb0addca_42b6_4884_aa18_438c695747ab/concepts.md#c02); at the same time there is no record from the other side, so the "meaning" on either side is only a one-sided attribution.
7. What can be said and what cannot be inferred. Can be said: in this record, gestures and exchange of objects occurred together, and the two sides' later [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) changed. Cannot be inferred: what the other side's gestures referred to, whether they really pointed to gold or silver, how they regarded the fleet's gestures; which people they were; whether the letter's description of them is accurate; why the fleet left those two men.

<note>

*Seven steps: how it is cut, what the starting state was, what happened, how outward behavior changed, what came after, what it shows, and what can and cannot be said. The point is: with no shared language, one can only try step by step; and the recorder himself admits that he understood things the way he wished.*

</note>

### 9.2 Ping-Pong Diplomacy (1971 to 1972)

Data note. What was read: summaries of the content of History Today's "Ping-Pong Diplomacy: Myth and Reality" and of the History.com entry, both extracted by a tool and not original files. The two sources are inconsistent on one detail (see step 4). The words "friendship" and "chance" in the text are attributions by the parties or the authors.

<note>

*What was read are summaries of two web articles. The two disagree on one point, set side by side with no side chosen. "Friendship" and "chance" are the parties' own accounts.*

</note>

1. [Cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23). The scope runs from the World Table Tennis Championships in Nagoya in April 1971 to the exchange of visits by the Chinese and American teams in 1972. The [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) are the Chinese team and the American team; the Chinese and American governments, the Japanese hosts and others are treated as [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) from outside.
2. Initial state. There had already been ambassador-level talks between the two countries (136 in total from 1955 to 1970, according to History Today), and in 1957 a delegation of American youth visited China. The record cannot directly show in which dimensions these contacts were compatible, because the compatibility criterion has not been specified, so this case does not call them "connecting" or "not connecting".
3. New [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10). In April 1971, the American player Glenn Cowan boarded the Chinese team's bus, and the team captain Zhuang Zedong, in front of reporters, gave him a Huangshan silk painting; afterward the American team was invited, entered from Hong Kong on 10 April, played matches and made visits in Guangzhou, Beijing and Shanghai; on 14 April they met Zhou Enlai. According to History Today, the Chinese side, following instructions to put "friendship first, competition second", let some matches go. The rules of the game are explicitly listed rules that both sides have, and provide a fixed point in this narrow dimension.
4. [Weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) change. Only the outward record is written: the American team was invited and entered, and the two sides continued compatibly in the dimension of the game (matches proceeded according to the rules). The sources are inconsistent: History.com says that on the same day (14 April) Nixon announced a relaxation of travel and trade restrictions; History Today says that Nixon had already lifted the travel ban and does not mention a trade embargo. Set side by side, no side chosen.
5. Outcome. In July 1971 Kissinger secretly visited Beijing; in February 1972 Nixon visited China; in 1972 the Chinese table tennis team visited eight American cities.
6. What it shows. In a narrow dimension, when there is a ready explicitly listed rule (the rules of the game), the two sides can connect without first being familiar; whether they connected outside this dimension has to be looked at separately in the records of other dimensions. "Friendship", "chance" and "spontaneous" are all attributions by the parties or the authors: the author of History Today holds that this contact was not by chance, which is the author's interpretation and is not taken as a record; that Mao Zedong approved the invitation, the article says, has documentary basis, and this paper cites it only as "the article records it so".
7. What can be said and what cannot be inferred. Can be said: in this record, after the game contact, contacts in other dimensions gradually increased. Cannot be inferred: that the table tennis contact caused the later visits; why the parties acted so; the respective purposes of Mao, Zhou and Nixon; whether the later diplomatic results depended on this event.

<note>

*The point: an explicit rule both sides have (the rules of the game) let the two sides connect first on that small piece; as for whether the later diplomatic results were caused by this game, that cannot be said. Why they acted so cannot be said either.*

</note>

### 9.3 Treaty of Wuchale (1889 to 1893)

Data note. What was read: the Wikipedia entry on the treaty, and a Warwick University student article (Katie Blair, on translation in the colonial period), both extracted by a tool and not original files. The correspondence between 1890 and 1893 was not read. Where the two sources contradict each other, they are only set side by side.

<note>

*What was read are summaries of two web pages; the correspondence of the years in between was not read. Where the two sources differ, they are set side by side.*

</note>

1. [Cut](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c23). The scope runs from the signing of the treaty to Menelik II's public repudiation (Wikipedia's date of signing is 2 May 1889; for the date of repudiation the entry says 1893 in one place and 1894 in another, which is inconsistent). The [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25): the Italian side and the Ethiopian side; the two texts (Italian and Amharic) are treated as two [signal containers](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c26), and the other European states are treated as [nodes](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c25) [arriving](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10) from outside.
2. Initial state. The two sides' [dedicated history](../paper_ae79e938_76e1_4bf9_b2ab_995c03e16c9e/concepts.md#c05) before the treaty is not stated in the sources, and this case does not write it.
3. New [arrival](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c10). Both texts have twenty articles. In Article 17, according to Wikipedia, the Amharic version says Ethiopia "may" use the Italian government to handle foreign affairs, while the Italian version says it "must" go through Italy; the difference is the permission versus compulsion of one verb. In October 1889 Italy notified the European states of its position that "Ethiopia is a protectorate"; the Ottoman Empire and Russia did not accept it, and the other states did.
4. [Weight](../paper_37b3643a_dc40_4463_8b36_4f4594acd986/concepts.md#c06) change. Only the outward record is written: each of the two texts is fixed content and does not change because of the two sides' dispute; what changes is the later [output](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28) of the two sides and of third parties. Menelik repudiated the protectorate reading; in 1895 Italy invaded.
5. Outcome. In the Battle of Adwa (the Warwick article says 1896) Ethiopia won and preserved its independence.
6. What it shows. The two texts are two [templates](concepts.md#c14) (one rule with two texts). The existence of a [template](concepts.md#c14) does not guarantee that both sides read the same rule: the incompatibility returned to the parties only through the third parties' notifications and the two sides' later [outputs](../paper_b9df3b68_23ee_4839_965b_ed0e6b283ee6/concepts.md#c28).
7. What can be said and what cannot be inferred. Can be said: the two texts are incompatible on Article 17; after this incompatibility, notification, repudiation and invasion followed. Cannot be inferred: who altered the text, whether it was deliberate, whether Menelik knew when signing that the two versions differed; the two sources contradict each other on these points (Wikipedia mentions only the difference in the verb and does not say who changed it; the Warwick article says Antonelli drafted both versions, that the Amharic version was altered, that Menelik did not know when he signed, and calls his later account racial discrimination, which is that article author's interpretation); whether the invasion was caused by the treaty dispute; what happened between 1890 and 1893.

<note>

*The point: one rule written as two copies with different content; what is written down does not change by itself, so each side holds its own copy. Who changed it and whether it was deliberate, the two sources do not agree on; no side is chosen.*

</note>
